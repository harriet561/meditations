沉思录 卷一

1
From my grandfather Verus: decency and a mild temper.
自吾祖父维鲁斯处：端正之品行，温良之性情。
2
From what they say and I remember of my natural father: integrity and manliness.
自吾生父之传闻与吾所忆：正直不阿，丈夫气概。
3
From my mother: piety, generosity, the avoidance of wrongdoing and even the thought of it; also simplicity of living, well clear of the habits of the rich.
自吾母处：虔诚之心，宽厚之德，远避恶行恶念；生活朴素，与富者之习气判然有别。
4
From my great-grandfather: not to have attended schools for the public; to have had good teachers at home, and to realize that this is the sort of thing on which one should spend lavishly.
自吾曾祖父处：不入公学，延请良师于家，且深知此事乃值得倾力以赴者。
5
From my tutor: not to become a Green or Blue supporter at the races, or side with the Lights or Heavies in the amphitheatre; to tolerate pain and feel few needs; to work with my own hands and mind my own business; to be deaf to malicious gossip.
自吾师保处：赛马场上，不党绿蓝；斗兽场中，不分轻重。忍耐痛苦，寡欲少求；亲操劳作，专注己事；于流言蜚语，充耳不闻。
6
From Diognetus: to avoid empty enthusiasms; to disbelieve all that is talked by miracle-mongers and quacks about incantations, exorcism of demons, and the like; not to hold quail-fights or be excited by such sports; to tolerate plain speaking; to have an affinity for philosophy, and to attend the lectures first of Baccheius, then of Tandasis and Marcianus; to write essays from a young age; to love the camp-bed, the hide blanket, and all else involved in the Greek training.
自狄奥格涅图斯处：避空虚之狂热；不信术士庸医所言咒语法术、驱魔逐鬼之类；不斗鹌鹑，亦不为此等嬉戏所动；容忍直言；亲近哲学，先后听讲于巴凯乌斯、坦达西斯与马尔基亚努斯之门；少年时即习作文章；爱行军之床、兽皮之毯，及希腊训练之一切。
7
From Rusticus: to grasp the idea of wanting correction and treatment for my character; not to be diverted into a taste for rhetoric, so not writing up my own speculations, delivering my own little moral sermons, or presenting a glorified picture of the ascetic or the philanthropist; to keep clear of speechifying, versifying, and pretentious language; not to walk around at home in ceremonial dress, or do anything else like that; to write letters in an unaffected style, like his own letter written to my mother from Sinuessa; to be readily recalled to conciliation with those who have taken or given offence, just as soon as they themselves are willing to turn back; to read carefully, not satisfied with my own superficial thoughts or quick to accept the facile views of others; to have encountered the Discourses of Epictetus, to which he introduced me with his own copy.
自鲁斯提库斯处：领悟吾之品性需纠正与磨砺；不为修辞之学所误——不撰写一己之臆断，不作琐碎之道德训诫，不粉饰苦行或博爱之虚名；远离高谈阔论、吟诗作赋与矫饰之辞；不着礼服于家中踱步，亦不为诸如此类之事；书写信函，质朴无华，如其自西努埃萨致吾母之书；于人有所冒犯或为人所犯之时，但见对方有回心转意之念，便欣然复归于好；读书精细，不以一己浮浅之见为足，亦不轻纳他人之皮毛；得遇爱比克泰德之《谈话录》，彼以己之藏本示吾。
8
From Apollonius: moral freedom, the certainty to ignore the dice of fortune, and have no other perspective, even for a moment, than that of reason alone; to be always the same man, unchanged in sudden pain, in the loss of a child, in lingering sickness; to see clearly in his living example that a man can combine intensity and relaxation; not to be impatient in explanation; the observance of a man who clearly regarded as the least of his gifts his experience and skill in communicating his philosophical insights; the lesson of how to take apparent favours from one's friends, neither compromised by them nor insensitive in their rejection.
自阿波罗尼乌斯处：精神之自由，漠视命运之骰子而无惑，一刹那间亦唯理性是依；始终如一，临剧痛而不改，遭丧子而不移，处久病而不变；观其生平，灼然可见人能兼紧张与松弛于一身；解说之际，不急不躁；彼之为人，分明视其传达哲学洞见之经验与技巧为末技；亦学得如何承受友人之恩惠——既不为之所缚，亦不以拒绝而失于人情。
9
From Sextus: a kindly disposition, and the pattern of a household governed by the paterfamilias; the concept of life lived according to nature; an unaffected dignity; intuitive concern for his friends; tolerance both of ordinary people and of the emptily opinionated; an agreeable manner with all, so that the pleasure of his conversation was greater than any flattery, and his very presence brought him the highest respect from all the company; certainty of grasp and method in the discovery and organization of the essential principles of life; never to give the impression of anger or any other passion, but to combine complete freedom from passion with the greatest human affection; to praise without fanfare, and to wear great learning lightly.
自塞克斯图斯处：仁厚之性情，家长治理家宅之典范；依自然而生活之观念；不事造作之尊严；于友人怀直觉之关切；包容凡俗之辈，亦容空洞自诩之徒；与众人交，和悦可亲，与之交谈之乐，胜于一切谄媚，其人之在场，即使众人油然而生至深敬意；于人生根本原则之发见与整理，确有把握，自成章法；从不流露恼怒之色，亦无任何激情之态，全无激情扰攘而兼怀至大之人间温情；褒扬而不事喧张，博学而举重若轻。
10
From Alexander the grammarian: not to leap on mistakes, or captiously interrupt when anyone makes an error of vocabulary, syntax, or pronunciation, but neatly to introduce the correct form of that particular expression by way of answer, confirmation, or discussion of the matter itself rather than its phrasing — or by some other such felicitous prompting.
自文法家亚历山大处：不急于纠正他人之误，亦不于他人用词、句法或发音有误时苛责打断，而是巧妙地在回答、确认或讨论其事而非其辞之际，将正确说法自然引入——或借其他恰如其分之提示达成此效。
11
From Fronto: to understand the effect of suspicion, caprice, and hypocrisy in the exercise of absolute rule; and that for the most part these people we call 'Patricians' are somewhat short of human affection.
自弗龙托处：洞悉猜忌、反复与虚伪在专制权力行使中之效应；且知此等被吾辈称为「贵族」者，大抵人情有所匮乏。
12
From Alexander the Platonist: rarely, and never without essential cause, to say or write to anyone that 'I am too busy'; nor to use a similar excuse, advancing 'pressure of circumstances', in constant avoidance of the proprieties inherent in our relations to our fellows and contemporaries.
自柏拉图主义者亚历山大处：鲜少——且从无必要之由——对人言或书「吾甚忙」；亦不以「事务紧迫」之类措辞为推脱，不断回避吾辈于同侪时人关系中应有之礼数。
13
From Catulus: not to spurn a friend's criticism, even if it may be an unreasonable complaint, but to try to restore his usual feelings; to speak of one's teachers with wholehearted gratitude, as is recorded of Domitius and Athenodotus; and a genuine love for children.
自卡图卢斯处：不拒友人之批评，纵其责难未必合理，亦当设法使其恢复常态；言及师长，满怀感激，一如文献所载之多米提乌斯与阿特诺多图斯；且有对孩童之真挚喜爱。
14
From Severus: love of family, love of truth, love of justice; to have come by his help to understand Thrasea, Helvidius, Cato, Dio, Brutus; to have conceived the idea of a balanced constitution, a commonwealth based on equality and freedom of speech, and of a monarchy which values above all the liberty of the subject; from him, too, a constant and vigorous respect for philosophy; beneficence, unstinting generosity, optimism; his confidence in the affection of his friends, his frankness with those who met with his censure, and open likes and dislikes, so that his friends did not need to guess at his wishes.
自塞维鲁斯处：爱家庭，爱真理，爱正义；赖其助力而得以理解特拉塞亚、赫尔维狄乌斯、加图、狄奥、布鲁图斯；得以构想均衡政制之理念——基于平等与言论自由之共和，及以臣民自由为至上之君主制；自彼处亦得对哲学恒久而热切之尊重；乐善好施，慷慨不吝，乐观向上；深信友人于己之情谊，于遭其责难者开诚布公，好恶坦荡，令友人不需猜测其心意。
15
From Maximus: self-mastery, immune to any passing whim; good cheer in all circumstances, including illness; a nice balance of character, both gentle and dignified; an uncomplaining energy for what needs to be done; the trust he inspired in everyone that he meant what he said and was well-intentioned in all that he did; proof against surprise or panic; in nothing either hurried or hesitant, never short of resource, never downcast or cringing, or on the other hand angry or suspicious; generosity in good works, and a forgiving and truthful nature; the impression he gave of undeviating rectitude as a path chosen rather than enforced; the fact that no one would ever have thought himself belittled by him, or presumed to consider himself superior to him; and a pleasant humour.
自马克西穆斯处：克己自制，不为一时意绪所移；诸般境遇中——包括病中——皆怡然自乐；品格均衡有度，温文而兼尊严；于当为之事，勤勉以赴而无怨言；众人皆信其言出必行，其行皆善；临意外而不惊，处慌乱而不惧；凡事无匆忙亦无迟疑，从不匮乏智谋，从不消沉畏缩，亦不愠怒多疑；行善慷慨，天性宽恕而真诚；其人所予之印象乃坚贞正直，如同自择之道而非强加之路；无人曾觉为其所轻，亦无人敢自以为胜于其人；兼有令人愉悦之幽默。
16
From my [adoptive] father: gentleness, and an immovable adherence to decisions made after full consideration; no vain taste for so-called honours; stamina and perseverance; a ready ear for anyone with any proposal for the common good; to reward impartially, giving everyone their due; experience of where to tighten, where to relax; putting a stop to homosexual love of young men; a common courtesy, excusing his court from constant attendance at dinner with him and the obligation to accompany him out of town, and those kept away by some other commitment always found him no different towards them; focused and persistent in deliberation in council, never satisfied with first impressions and leaving a question prematurely; the concern to keep his friends, with no extremes of surfeit or favouritism; his own master in all things, and serene with it; foresight for the longer issues and unfussy control of the least detail; the check he put in his reign on acclamations and all forms of flattery; his constant watch on the needs of the empire, his stewardship of its resources, and his tolerance of some people's criticism in this area; no superstitious fear of the gods, nor with men any populism or obsequious courting of the mob, but a sober steadfastness in all things, and nowhere any vulgar or newfangled taste. In those things which conduce to the comfort of life — and here fortune gave him plenty — to enjoy them without pride or apology either, so no routine acceptance of their presence or regret in their absence; the fact that no one would ever describe him as a fraud or an impostor or a pedant, but rather as a man of mellow wisdom and mature experience, beyond flattery, able to take charge of his own and others' affairs. Further, his high regard for genuine philosophers — for the other sort he had no hard words, but easily saw through them; sociability, too, and a sense of humour, not taken to excess; sensible care of his own body, neither vain nor valetudinarian, but not neglectful either, so that his own attention to himself left very little need for doctors, doses, or applications. Most importantly, his readiness to defer ungrudgingly to those with some special ability — it might be in literary expression, or the study of laws or customs or any other subject — and to give them his own active support to reach acknowledged eminence in their own specialities. Acting always in accordance with tradition, yet not making the preservation of tradition an overt aim; further, no liking for change and chance, but a settled habit in the same places and the same practices; to resume instantly after attacks of migraine, fresh again and vigorous for his usual work; not to keep many matters secret to himself, only a very few exceptional cases and those solely of state concern; sense and moderation in such things as the provision of shows, contracting of public works, doles and distributions — the acts of a man with an eye for precisely what needs to be done, not the glory of its doing. He was not one to bathe at all hours; he had no urge to build houses; he was not particular about food, the material and colour of his clothes, or youthful beauty in his slaves; the fact that his dress came from Lorium, sent up from his country house there; the many details of his way of life at Lanuvium; how he handled the apologetic customs officer in Tusculum, and all such modes of behaviour. Nothing about him was harsh, relentless, or impetuous, and you would never say of him that he 'broke out a sweat': but everything was allotted its own time and thought, as by a man of leisure — his way was unhurried, organized, vigorous, consistent in all. What is recorded of Socrates would apply to him too: that he could regulate abstinence and enjoyment where many people are too weak-willed to abstain or enjoy too indulgently. Strength of character — and endurance or sobriety as the case may be — signifies the man of full and indomitable spirit, as was shown by Maximus in his illness.
自吾父（养父）处：性情温良，于深思熟虑之后所下决断，坚如磐石；不慕虚名浮誉；耐力与恒心兼备；凡有益于公益之建言，无不虚怀以听；赏罚公允，予人以所当得；深知何处当紧何处当松；禁绝少年男色之风；谦恭有礼，免朝臣夜夜侍宴之劳及随驾出城之役，且彼等因他务不能至者，归来见之，其态度始终如一，毫无二致；庭议之中，专注而持重，不以初见为足，亦不草草了结议题；悉心维系友人之谊，不过分亲昵，亦不偏私专宠；万般事务，自主而不假人手，且安然自若；远虑长策而不忽略细节之掌控；其在位时，力禁欢呼颂扬及一切谄媚之辞；时刻关注帝国之需，善理财用，且宽忍他人于此方面之批评；敬神而不迷信，待人不媚俗、不迎合民众，凡事沉着坚定，全无粗俗新奇之品味。于生活安逸之物——此乃命运所厚赐——享用而不自矜，亦不抱愧，故不以其在而视为当然，亦不以其去而怅然若失；无人曾称其为骗子、伪君子或迂腐之人，皆视其为智慧圆融、经验成熟、超乎谄媚之外、能主宰自己与他人事务之人。再者，其敬重真正之哲人——至于其余伪托之辈，虽不出恶言，然洞烛其奸，一览无余；平易近人而有风趣，然不过度；于己身之保养，既不自恋亦不疑病，然亦不放任疏忽，如此自我照料之下，甚少需医者、药剂或敷治。最为要紧者，其心甘情愿向怀有专长之人退让——或为文学表达，或为法律风俗之研究，或为任何其他学问——并予以切实之支持，助其在各自领域达致公认之卓越。凡事遵循传统而行事，然并不刻意标榜保存传统；复次，不喜变化与偶然，而安居于同样之处所，持守同样之习惯；偏头痛发作之后，旋即恢复，精神焕然，一如往常般勤于公务；不将诸多事务秘藏于己，仅极少数例外且纯属国家机密；于举办演出、订立公共工程、发放救济与分配诸事，皆有分寸与节制——其人行事，着眼于精准之所需所当为，而非事后之荣光。彼非时时沐浴之人；无大兴土木之欲；于饮食、衣物之质料与颜色、或奴隶之青春美貌，并不挑剔；其衣袍来自洛里乌姆，由彼处乡居寄送而来；其于拉努维乌姆生活之诸多细节；其如何应对图斯库卢姆税吏之道歉——凡此种种行事风度。其人毫无苛刻、寡情或急躁之处，你永不会说他「汗流浃背」：一切皆各自有其恰当之时机与思虑，如闲适之人——其作风从容不迫、有条不紊、精力充沛、始终一贯。苏格拉底之记载可适用于彼：彼能在众人意志薄弱、或不能自禁、或放纵无度之处，调节节制与享乐之平衡。品格之力量——坚韧或节制，视情况而定——标志着其人精神之完满与不可征服，正如马克西穆斯于疾病中所展示者。

From the gods: to have had good grandparents, good parents, a good sister, good teachers, good family, relatives, and friends — almost everything; and that I did not blunder into offending any of them, even though I had the sort of disposition which might indeed have resulted in some such offence, given the occasion — it was the grace of the gods that no set of circumstances likely to show me up ever arose. That I was not brought up any longer than I was with my grandfather's mistress, and that I kept my innocence, leaving sexual experience to the proper time and indeed somewhat beyond it. That I came under a ruler and a father who was to strip me of all conceit and bring me to realize that it is possible to live in a palace without feeling the need for bodyguards or fancy uniforms, candelabra, statues, or the other trappings of suchlike pomp, but that one can reduce oneself very close to the station of a private citizen and not thereby lose any dignity or vigour in the conduct of a ruler's responsibility for the common good. That I was blessed with a brother whose character could spur me to care for myself, and whose respect and affection were likewise a source of joy to me. That my children were not born short of intelligence or physically deformed. That I did not make further progress in rhetoric, poetry, and the other pursuits in which I could well have been absorbed, if I had felt this my right path. That I was quick to raise my tutors to the public office which I thought they desired and did not put them off, in view of their youth, with promises for the future. That I came to know Apollonius, Rusticus, Maximus. That I acquired a clear and constant picture of what is meant by the life according to nature, so that, with regard to the gods, their communications from that world, their help and their inspiration, nothing now prevents me living the life of nature: my falling somewhat short, still, is due to my own fault and my failure to observe the promptings, not to say the instructions, of the gods. That my body has held out so far in a life such as mine. That I never touched Benedicta or Theodotus, and that later experience of sexual passion left me cured. That, though I was often angry with Rusticus, my behaviour never went to the point of regret. That my mother, fated to die young, nevertheless lived her last years with me. That whenever I wanted to help someone in poverty or some other need I was never told that there was no source of affordable money: and that I myself never fell into similar want of financial assistance from another. That my wife is as she is, so submissive, loving, and unaffected: and that I found no lack of suitable tutors for my children. That I was given help through dreams, especially how to avoid spitting blood and bouts of dizziness: and the response of the oracle at Caieta, 'Just as you use yourself'. That, for all my love of philosophy, I did not fall in with any sophist, or devote my time to the analysis of literature or logic, or busy myself with cosmic speculation. All these things need 'the help of gods and Fortune's favour'.
自诸神处：得享贤祖父、良父母、好姊妹、明师保、善家人、亲族与良友——近乎无所不备；且未因莽撞而冒犯其中任一，虽吾之秉性确可能在某种情境下导致此等过失——赖诸神之恩典，始终未遇令吾难堪之局面。未在祖父之妾手中抚养过久，得以保其纯真，延迟性事至恰当之年，甚至稍逾其时。得以受治于一君一父，其剥去吾一切自负，使吾领悟：居于宫廷，不必倚仗侍卫、华服、烛台、雕像及诸如此类之排场；人可自抑至于近乎一介平民之境地，而于担当治理国事之责时，并不因此而失其尊严与活力。蒙恩得有一弟，其品格足以激励吾自修持身，其敬重与爱护亦为吾之乐源。吾之子女，生而无智障或形体之缺。吾于修辞学、诗歌及其他本可醉心之技艺上未再深造，若当时自以为此乃正道。吾迅即擢拔吾之师保至彼等想望之公职，不以其年少而空许未来以敷衍之。得以结识阿波罗尼乌斯、鲁斯提库斯、马克西穆斯。得以清晰而恒定地领悟何为依自然而生活，故于诸神而言，彼等自另一世界之通示、其襄助与其启迪，皆无碍吾行自然之道：吾之有所不及，仍因己之过失，未能遵循诸神之提示——遑论其明示。此身至今仍能支撑如吾这般之生活。从未染指贝内迪克塔或西奥多图斯，而后之情欲体验反令吾痊愈。虽常对鲁斯提库斯动怒，所为从未至于追悔之境。吾母虽命中早逝，竟于最后之岁月与吾同住。每欲助人于贫困或他种急需之际，从未被告以无款可用：而吾自身亦从未落入类似之窘境，需向他人求援。吾妻之为人，温顺、深情而质朴：为吾子女寻得良师亦不匮乏。梦中获助，尤其如何避免咯血与眩晕之症：及至卡耶塔神谕之答：「如其用己。」虽酷爱哲学，却未堕入任何诡辩家之手，亦未将时日虚掷于剖析辞章或逻辑，或忙于宇宙之玄思。凡此一切，皆需「诸神之助与命运之眷顾」。

沉思录 卷二

1
Say to yourself first thing in the morning: today I shall meet people who are meddling, ungrateful, aggressive, treacherous, malicious, unsocial. All this has afflicted them through their ignorance of true good and evil. But I have seen that the nature of good is what is right, and the nature of evil what is wrong; and I have reflected that the nature of the offender himself is akin to my own — not a kinship of blood or seed, but a sharing in the same mind, the same fragment of divinity. Therefore I cannot be harmed by any of them, as none will infect me with their wrong. Nor can I be angry with my kinsman or hate him. We were born for cooperation, like feet, like hands, like eyelids, like the rows of upper and lower teeth. So to work in opposition to one another is against nature: and anger or rejection is opposition.
清晨即起，当自谓曰：今日我将遇好管闲事之人、忘恩负义之人、蛮横无礼之人、奸诈狡猾之人、心怀恶意之人、落落寡合之人。凡此种种，皆因其不识真善真恶之故。然我已洞见：善之本质在于其正，恶之本质在于其邪；我亦沉思，冒犯者之本性实与我同源——非血亲或种嗣之亲，乃共享同一理性、同禀一分神性之亲。故无人能损我分毫，因无人能将其罪愆染及我身。我亦不能对同源之人动怒或怀恨。吾辈生而为协作，犹如双足，犹如双手，犹如眼睑，犹如上下齿列。故彼此相悖而行，乃逆自然之道：愤怒与排斥，即是相悖。

Whatever it is, this being of mine is made up of flesh, breath, and directing mind. Now the flesh you should disdain — blood, bones, a mere fabric and network of nerves, veins, and arteries. Consider too what breath is: wind — and not even a constant, but all the time being disgorged and sucked in again. That leaves the third part, the directing mind. Quit your books — no more hankering: this is not your gift. No, think like this, as if you were on the point of death: 'you are old; don't then let this directing mind of yours be enslaved any longer — no more jerking to the strings of selfish impulse, no more disquiet at your present or suspicion of your future fate.'
无论如何，我之此身，乃由血肉、气息与主宰之心灵所构成。至于血肉，你当鄙薄之——血、骨，无非神经、静脉与动脉之织物与网络。再思气息为何物：风而已矣——且非恒常之风，不过时时呼出复又吸入者也。所余者，第三部分：主宰之心灵。放下你的书卷——不复眷恋：此非你所禀赋。不若如此思之，如你即将赴死：「你已年迈；勿再令此主宰之心灵受奴役——勿再为自私冲动之绳线所牵曳，勿再对当下惴惴不安，勿再对将来之命运满腹狐疑。」

The works of the gods are full of providence. The works of Fortune are not independent of Nature or the spinning and weaving together of the threads governed by Providence. All things flow from that world: and further factors are necessity and the benefit of the whole universe, of which you are a part. Now every part of nature benefits from that which is brought by the nature of the Whole and all which preserves that nature: and the order of the universe is preserved equally by the changes in the elements and the changes in their compounds. Let this be enough for you, and your constant doctrine. And give up your thirst for books, so that you do not die a grouch, but in true grace and heartfelt gratitude to the gods.
诸神之作为充满天意。命运之作为亦非独立于自然，亦非独立于天意所掌之经纬交织。万物皆自彼世涌流而来：且更有必然性与整个宇宙之福祉——你为其一部分。自然之每一部分皆受益于整体自然之所赐，受益于一切维系此自然之物：宇宙之秩序同样赖元素之变化及其复合物之变化而得以维持。此理于你当为足够，当作你恒常之道。且放下求书若渴之心，如此你方能不于怨愤中离世，而于真正之从容中对诸神满怀感激。

Remember how long you have been putting this off, how many times you have been given a period of grace by the gods and not used it. It is high time now for you to understand the universe of which you are a part, and the governor of that universe of whom you constitute an emanation: and that there is a limit circumscribed to your time — if you do not use it to clear away your clouds, it will be gone, and you will be gone, and the opportunity will not return.
记取你已将此推迟多久，诸神曾予你多少缓冲之时日而你未曾利用。如今正是时候，你当领悟你身为其一部分之宇宙，领悟你作为其流溢之宇宙主宰：且悟你的时间有一限界——若你不用之以驱散你心中之迷雾，则时间将逝，你亦将逝，而良机不再。

Every hour of the day give vigorous attention, as a Roman and as a man, to the performance of the task in hand with precise analysis, with unaffected dignity, with human sympathy, with dispassionate justice — and to vacating your mind from all its other thoughts. And you will achieve this vacation if you perform each action as if it were the last of your life: freed, that is, from all lack of aim, from all passion-led deviation from the ordinance of reason, from pretence, from love of self, from dissatisfaction with what fate has dealt you. You see how few things a man needs to master for the settled flow of a god-fearing life. The gods themselves ask nothing more of one who keeps these observances.
一日之中，每时每刻，当如罗马人，如堂堂男子，以精审之分析、不由矫饰之尊严、人之同情、不动于情之公正，专心致志于手头之务——并使心灵从一切杂念中抽身而出。你若将每一行动当作生命之最后一举来施行，便能成就此等放空：即，从一切目标缺失中解脱，从一切由激情驱使而违理性训令之偏轨中解脱，从虚伪中解脱，从自恋中解脱，从对命运所予之不满中解脱。你看，一个人需要掌控之事何其少也，便能获致虔敬生命之安流。诸神于恪守此等训诫者，亦别无他求。

Self-harm, my soul, you are doing self-harm: and you will have no more opportunity for self-respect. Life for each of us is a mere moment, and this life of yours is nearly over, while you still show yourself no honour, but let your own welfare depend on other people's souls. Do externals tend to distract you? Then give yourself the space to learn some further good lesson, and stop your wandering. That done, you must guard against the other sort of drift. Those who are dead to life and have no aim for the direction of every impulse and, more widely, every thought are drivellers in deed as well as word.
自伤也，吾之魂灵，你在行自伤之事：而你不再有自我尊重之机。于我等每人而言，生命不过一瞬，而你此生已近尾声，你犹未示己以尊重，却令一己之福祉依赖于他人之魂灵。身外之物岂易使你分心？那便予自己空间，学取更多良训，止住你的游荡。此事既成，你当慎防另一种飘移。那些于生命已死、无一目标以引导每一冲动、乃至每一思想之人，不唯言辞，即其行事，亦不过是口涎横流之徒。

Failure to read what is happening in another's soul is not easily seen as a cause of unhappiness: but those who fail to attend to the motions of their own soul are necessarily unhappy.
未能洞悉他人魂灵之所历，不易被视为不幸之因：然未能关注自身魂灵之动作者，其不幸乃是必然。

Always remember these things: what the nature of the Whole is, what my own nature is, the relation of this nature to that, what kind of part it is of what kind of Whole; and that there is no one who can prevent you keeping all that you say and do in accordance with that nature, of which you are a part.
当时刻铭记此等事理：整体之自然为何，我自身之自然为何，此自然与彼自然之关系如何，此一部分在何种整体中居于何种位置；且铭记，无人能阻挡你使你一切言行符合于你为其一部分之自然。

In his comparative ranking of sins, applying philosophy to the common man's distinctions, Theophrastus says that offences of lust are graver than those of anger: because it is clearly some sort of pain and involuntary spasm which drives the angry man to abandon reason, whereas the lust-led offender has given in to pleasure and seems somehow more abandoned and less manly in his wrongdoing. Rightly, then, and like a true philosopher, Theophrastus said that greater censure attaches to an offence committed under the influence of pleasure than to one under the influence of pain. And in general the one is more like an injured party, forced to anger by the pain of provocation: whereas the other is his own source of the impulse to wrong, driven to what he does by lust.
泰奥弗拉斯图斯在比较罪行之轻重时，将哲学运用于常人之区分，谓欲望之罪重于愤怒之罪：因驱使愤怒者抛弃理性者，显然为某种痛苦与非自愿之痉挛，而受欲望驱使而犯罪者，乃屈服于快乐，于其过犯中似更为放纵、更失丈夫气概。故泰奥弗拉斯图斯如真正哲学家一般，正确指出：受快乐影响而犯之罪，比之受痛苦影响而犯之罪，更当受谴责。概而言之，前者更似受伤之一方，为挑衅之痛苦所逼而怒；后者则自身即为作恶冲动之源，为其欲望所驱而为之。
11
You may leave this life at any moment: have this possibility in your mind in all that you do or say or think. Now departure from the world of men is nothing to fear, if gods exist: because they would not involve you in any harm. If they do not exist, or if they have no care for humankind, then what is life to me in a world devoid of gods, or devoid of providence? But they do exist, and they do care for humankind: and they have put it absolutely in man's power to avoid falling into the true kinds of harm. If there were anything harmful in the rest of experience, they would have provided for that too, to make it in everyone's power to avoid falling into it; and if something cannot make a human being worse, how could it make his life a worse life? The nature of the Whole would not have been blind to this, either through ignorance or with knowledge unaccompanied by the power to prevent and put right. Nor would it have made so great an error, through lack of power or skill, as to have good and bad falling indiscriminately, on good and bad people alike. Yes, death and life, fame and ignominy, pain and pleasure, wealth and poverty — all these come to good and bad alike, but they are not in themselves either right or wrong: neither then are they inherent good or evil.
你随时可能辞别此生：凡所行、所言、所思，心中皆当存此一念。若诸神存在，离开人世便无所可惧：因诸神必不陷你于任何伤害。若诸神不存在，或于人类漠不关心，则我居于此无神、无天意之世，生命又有何义？然诸神确实存在，且关切人类：彼等已将避免陷入真正之伤害的能力，全然置于人之掌控中。若其余经验中尚有危害之物，彼等亦必为之预备，使人皆能避免坠入其中；若某物不能使人变恶，又如何能使人之生命变恶？整体之自然必不致对此视而不见——或出于无知，或虽有知而无能预防与纠正。亦不致因力量或技巧之欠缺，而犯如此大错，以至善恶无别地降临于善人与恶人之身。诚然，死与生、荣与辱、苦与乐、富与贫——凡此种种，善人恶人皆同样遭遇，然其本身既非正亦非邪：故亦非固有之善或固有之恶。

How all things quickly vanish, our bodies themselves lost in the physical world, the memories of them lost in time; the nature of all objects of the senses — especially those which allure us with pleasure, frighten us with pain, or enjoy the applause of vanity — how cheap they are, how contemptible, shoddy, perishable, and dead: these are matters for our intellectual faculty to consider. And further considerations. What are they, these people whose judgements and voices confer or deny esteem? What is death? Someone looking at death per se, and applying the analytical power of his mind to divest death of its associated images, will conclude then that it is nothing more than a function of nature — and if anyone is frightened of a function of nature, he is a mere child. And death is not only a function of nature, but also to her benefit. Further. How does man touch god, with what part of his being, and when that part of him is in what sort of disposition?
万物何其速朽也——吾辈之躯体消失于物质世界中，其记忆湮灭于时间之中；感官对象之本质——尤其那些以快乐诱人、以痛苦恐吓人、或享有虚荣喝彩之物——何其廉价、何其可鄙、何其粗劣、易逝而枯死：此皆吾辈智识官能所当思虑者也。更有进者：那些以判断和声音赋予或剥夺尊敬者，究竟是何等人？死亡为何物？有人直视死亡本身，运用心智之分析力剥离死亡之种种联想意象，便将断言：死亡无非自然之一种功用——而若有人对自然之一种功用感到恐惧，那便是孩童无疑。且死亡不仅是自然之一种功用，亦有益于自然。更有进者：人如何触及神，以己身之哪一部分，且该部分处于何种境地之时？

Nothing is more miserable than one who is always out and about, running round everything in circles — in Pindar's words 'delving deep in the bowels of the earth' — and looking for signs and symptoms to divine his neighbours' minds. He does not realize that it is sufficient to concentrate solely on the divinity within himself and to give it true service. That service is to keep it uncontaminated by passion, triviality, or discontent at what is dealt by gods or men. What comes from the gods demands reverence for their goodness. What comes from men is welcome for our kinship's sake, but sometimes pitiable also, in a way, because of their ignorance of good and evil: and this is no less a disability than that which removes the distinction of light and dark.
世间最可怜者，莫过于终日在外奔走，绕一切事物团团转——如品达所谓「深掘大地之脏腑」——寻迹觅兆以揣测邻人之心意。彼浑然不知，专注其一己内心之神性并予以真正事奉，便已足够。此事奉即是保持其神性不为激情、琐屑，或对神人之所赐所予心怀不满所污染。自诸神中来者，需对其善德怀敬畏之心。自众人中来者，因吾辈之同源关系而欣然接纳，然有时亦以某种方式令人怜悯，因其对善恶之无知：此等残疾，不减于令人不辨光明与黑暗者。

Even if you were destined to live three thousand years, or ten times that long, nevertheless remember that no one loses any life other than the one he lives, or lives any life other than the one he loses. It follows that the longest and the shortest lives are brought to the same state. The present moment is equal for all; so what is passing is equal also; the loss therefore turns out to be the merest fragment of time. No one can lose either the past or the future — how could anyone be deprived of what he does not possess? So always remember these two things. First, that all things have been of the same kind from everlasting, coming round and round again, and it makes no difference whether one will see the same things for a hundred years, or two hundred years, or for an infinity of time. Second, that both the longest-lived and the earliest to die suffer the same loss. It is only the present moment of which either stands to be deprived: and if indeed this is all he has, he cannot lose what he does not have.
纵使你命中注定活上三千年，或十倍于彼，仍当铭记：无人能失去他所活过之外的任何生命，也无人能活出他所失去之外的任何生命。由此推论，最长与最短之生命皆归于同一状态。当下此刻，人人均等；故所逝去者亦均等；所失者原来不过是时间之最微片段。无人能失去过去或未来——人焉能被剥夺其所不拥有之物？故当时刻谨记此二事：其一，万物自永恒以来便属同类，循环往复而来，人之所见，百年、二百年、或无穷时间，并无分别。其二，最长寿者与最早夭者所失相同。二者所将被剥夺者，唯此当下此刻：且若此确为其所有之全部，则其不能失去其所无有。

'All is as thinking makes it so.' The retort made to Monimus the Cynic is clear enough: but clear too is the value of his saying, if one takes the kernel of it, as far as it is true.
「一切皆如思之所示。」对犬儒莫尼摩斯之反驳，自是显而易见：然若取其核心要义，以其真实所及为限，则其言之价值亦同样显然。

The soul of a man harms itself, first and foremost, when it becomes (as far as it can) a separate growth, a sort of tumour on the universe: because to resent anything that happens is to separate oneself in revolt from Nature, which holds in collective embrace the particular natures of all other things. Secondly, when it turns away from another human being, or is even carried so far in opposition as to intend him harm — such is the case in the souls of those gripped by anger. A soul harms itself, thirdly, when it gives in to pleasure or pain. Fourthly, whenever it dissimulates, doing or saying anything feigned or false. Fifthly, whenever it fails to direct any of its own actions or impulses to a goal, but acts at random, without conscious attention — whereas even the most trivial action should be undertaken in reference to the end. And the end for rational creatures is to follow the reason and the rule of that most venerable archetype of a governing state — the Universe.
人之灵魂自损，第一且最甚者，当其变为（尽其可能地）一种分离之赘生物，如宇宙上之肿囊：因怨恨所发生之任何事，即是反叛自然，与自然分离——自然本将所有其他事物之特殊本性统抱于怀。其次，当其背离另一人，或甚至在对立之中至于意欲害人——此乃为愤怒所攫之灵魂中之情状。灵魂自损，第三，当其屈服于快乐或痛苦。第四，当其伪饰，做出或说出任何假装或虚假之事。第五，当其未能指引自身任何行动或冲动朝向一目标，而是漫无目的地行动，无所知觉——然而即便是最为琐碎之举，亦当参照终极目的而行事。而理性造物之终极目的，即是遵循理性，遵循那最为庄严之治理邦国之原型——宇宙——的法则。
17
In man's life his time is a mere instant, his existence a flux, his perception fogged, his whole bodily composition rotting, his mind a whirligig, his fortune unpredictable, his fame unclear. To put it shortly: all things of the body stream away like a river, all things of the mind are dreams and delusion; life is warfare, and a visit in a strange land; the only lasting fame is oblivion. What then can escort us on our way? One thing, and one thing only: philosophy. This consists in keeping the divinity within us inviolate and free from harm, master of pleasure and pain, doing nothing without aim, truth, or integrity, and independent of others' action or failure to act. Further, accepting all that happens and is allotted to it as coming from that other source which is its own origin: and at all times awaiting death with the glad confidence that it is nothing more than the dissolution of the elements of which every living creature is composed. Now if there is nothing fearful for the elements themselves in their constant changing of each into another, why should one look anxiously in prospect at the change and dissolution of them all? This is in accordance with nature: and nothing harmful is in accordance with nature.
人之生命中，其时不过一瞬，其存在不过一潮流，其知觉蒙雾不清，其全部身体组织正在腐朽，其心灵如旋转之陀螺，其命运不可预测，其名声晦暗不明。简而言之：躯体之一切如河水奔逝，心灵之一切皆梦幻与虚妄；生命是战场，亦是异乡之旅；唯一持久之名乃是遗忘。然则，何物能伴我们行路？一事，唯此一事：哲学。此即是：保持吾辈内心之神性不受侵犯、不为所害，做苦乐之主宰，无事不怀目的、真理与正直而行，独立于他人之行动或不行动。更有进者，接纳所发生及所分配于己之一切，视其为源自另一源头——亦即其自身之源起：并时时心怀喜悦之确信等待死亡，知死亡不过是构成每一生灵之元素的分解而已。既然元素本身在彼此不断转化之中无所畏怖，则人何以要对这一切之变化与分解心怀焦虑？此乃符合自然之道：而凡符合自然之道者，皆非有害。

沉思录 卷三

1
We must take into our reckoning not only that life is expended day by day and the remaining balance diminishes, but also this further consideration: if we live longer, there is no guarantee that our mind will remain sufficient for the understanding of fact and for the theory which enables an expertise in things divine and human. It is true that dementia can set in, and then the functions of breath, nutrition, fancy, impulse, and so on will not fail. But the ability to make use of ourselves, to fill out the measure of our duties, to analyse appearances, to make the crucial decision whether it is time to take our leave — all this and other such processes which depend entirely on the exercised reason, is already extinguished. So we must press on, not simply because we move closer to death all the time, but because our comprehension of the world and our ability to pay proper attention will fade before us.
吾辈计虑之中，不仅当知生命日复一日耗减、余存日益萎缩，更当思及此节：纵使寿数稍延，心智是否仍足以理解事实、仍足以掌握通晓神人之事的理论，实无保证。痴愚确然可能降临，届时呼吸、营养、想象、冲动等诸般功能亦未必失却。然运用自身之力、践行职分之能、分析表象之明、作此关键决断——是否当辞世而去——凡此种种及其他一应全然依赖训练有素之理性的过程，已然熄灭矣。故吾辈当亟亟前行，非仅因时时趋近死亡，更因吾辈对世界之理解与专注于事之能力，将先于吾人而消逝。
2
We should also attend to things like these, observing that even the incidental effects of the processes of Nature have their own charm and attraction. Take the baking of bread. The loaf splits open here and there, and these cracks, though in a sense escaping the design of the baker, have their own charm and a lively stimulus to appetite. Figs, again, split open when fully ripe: in the olives left on the tree, the very proximity of decay lends a particular beauty to the fruit. So too the bending heads of corn, the lion's frown, the foam at the boar's mouth, and many other things — far from beautiful in isolation, yet the consequence of some other process beautifies them and gives them attraction. So any man with a feeling and deeper insight for the workings of the Whole will find some pleasure in almost every aspect of their disposition, including the incidental consequences.
吾辈亦当留意如此等事，察见即便是自然运化之附带效果，亦有其独有之韵味与引力。以烤制面包为例，面包坯体处处开裂，而这些罅隙虽在某种意义上逸出焙烤者之设计，却自有其韵致，更激人食欲。又如无花果，完全成熟之际自然裂开；橄榄之留在树上者，正因其近乎腐烂而赋予果实一种独特之美。弯垂之麦穗、雄狮之蹙额、野猪嘴边之唾沫，以及其他种种——单独观之远非美丽，然作为其他过程之后果，反增其美且赋予其魅力。故人若对宇宙整体之运作有情致与慧眼，在几乎每一方面之配置中——包括那些附带之后果——均能获得某种愉悦。
3
Hippocrates cured many diseases then died of disease himself. The Chaldean astrologers foretold the deaths of many people, then their own fated day claimed them. Alexander, Pompey, Julius Caesar annihilated whole cities time and again, slaughtering tens of thousands of cavalry and infantry in pitched battle, then their own day came. Heraclitus speculated long on the conflagration of the universe, then died swollen with water and caked with cow-dung. Vermin killed Democritus; another sort of vermin killed Socrates. What of it? You embarked, you set sail, you made landfall. Step ashore then. If that means a new life, there is no want of gods even in that other world. But if it is a state of blank unconsciousness, then you will cease to be subject to pain and pleasure and servitude to the flesh — this vessel so much baser than that which is its slave. The one is mind and divinity, the other clay and putrefying blood.
希波克拉底治愈无数疾病，而后自身死于疾病。迦勒底占星家预卜众多人等的死亡，而后他们自己命定之日也将他们收去。亚历山大、庞培、尤利乌斯·凯撒一次又一次夷平整座城池，在列阵之战中屠戮成千上万之骑兵与步兵，而后他们自己也等来了那一天。赫拉克利特长久沉思宇宙之大火，而后浑身水胀、满涂牛粪而死。虱虫杀死了德谟克利特；另一种虱虫杀死了苏格拉底。这又如何？你登船起航，扬帆而行，抵达彼岸。那便上岸吧。若彼处是新生，彼一世界中亦不匮乏诸神。但若只是空白无觉之境，则你将不再受制于苦乐，不再受役于肉体——此器皿远较为其奴者更为卑下。一者为心与神，一者为泥土与腐血。
4
Do not waste the remaining part of your life in thoughts about other people, when you are not thinking with reference to some aspect of the common good. Why deprive yourself of the time for some other task? I mean, thinking about what so-and-so is doing, and why, what he is saying, what he is plotting, and all the other lines of thought which make you stray from the close watch on your own directing mind. We must then avoid in the chain of our thoughts all that is random and aimless: still more important is the avoidance of meddlesome and malicious thought. We should habituate ourselves to think only of those things about which, if anyone were suddenly to ask: 'What are you thinking of now?', you could at once freely reply: 'This' or 'That.' Then it would be immediately plain from the answer that all is simplicity and kindness — the thought of a social being who has no regard for the seductions of pleasure or of self-indulgence, nor for rivalry, malice, suspicion, or anything else which you would blush to admit you had in mind.
莫将余生虚掷于思虑他人之事——若你所思并非涉及公共福祉之某一方面。为何剥夺自己从事他务之时日？我意所指，乃是：思量某人正在做何事、为何而做、所言何语、所图何谋，以及一切使你疏于严密看守自己主导心灵之思绪。故吾辈须在思绪之链条中避开一切漫无目的与随机漂移之物；更要紧者，须避干涉与恶意之思。当养成为此习惯：仅思虑此等事——若有人猝然发问：「你此刻所思何事？」，你能即刻坦然答曰：「某事某物」。如此，从答案中当即显明一切皆质朴而善好——此乃一个社会性存在者之思绪，彼不顾及逸乐或放纵之引诱，亦不顾及争强、恶意、猜忌，或任何使你羞于承认存于心中之事物。
5
You should take no action unwillingly, selfishly, uncritically, or with conflicting motives. Do not dress up your thoughts in smart finery: do not be a gabbler or a meddler. Further, let the god that is within you be the guardian of a real man, a man of ripe years, a statesman, a Roman, a ruler: one who has taken his post, waiting for the signal of his release from life, ready for his departure, needing no oath of others nor any witness to his readiness. And a happy countenance in yourself, with no need of external help or the peace given by others. You need to stand straight, not straightened by others.
凡所行动，不当心存不愿、自私自利、不加审察，或动机彼此冲突。勿以花巧之装束文饰你的思想；勿为喋喋不休者或好管闲事者。更有进者，令你心中之神明守护一个真正之人——一个年岁成熟之人，一个政治家，一个罗马人，一个统治者：他已就任其岗位，等候被释放出生之信号，准备好启程，不赖他人之誓言或证词以明其就绪。你当自有怡然之色，不假外助，亦无需他人所给之平静。你需自行直立，而非被人扶正。
6
If you discover in human life something better than justice, truth, self-control, courage — in short, something better than the self-sufficiency of your own mind which keeps you acting in accord with true reason and your fate in whatever is assigned to you without your own choice: if, I say, you can see something better than this, then turn to it with all your heart and enjoy this prime good you have found. But if nothing is shown to be better than the very god that is seated in you, which has brought all your own impulses under its control, which scrutinizes your thoughts, which (in the words of Socrates) has withdrawn itself from all prompting of the senses, which holds itself answerable to the gods and devotes its care to humankind — if you find all else paltry and trivial by comparison with this, give no room to anything else. Once diverted and inclined that way, you will no longer have the ability to honour without reservation that other good, which is your own proper good.
若你在人生中发现了优于正义、真理、自制、勇气之物——简言之，优于你那自足之心灵之物，此心灵使你依据真实理性而行动，并接纳命运分配于你而非你自择之一切——若你发现胜于此者，便倾全心以赴之，享受你所觅得的此一至善。然而，若无一物显示优于那高居你内之神明——此神明已统驭你所有之冲动，细察你之思想，如苏格拉底所言已从一切感官驱使中撤回自身，自负其对诸神之责，而将关切倾注于人类——若你发觉与此相较，其他一切皆微不足道、琐屑不堪，则当不容他物有一席之地。一旦偏转而倾向于彼方，你便再无能力毫无保留地尊崇那别一善者——那属于你自己的、本己之善。
7
Never regard as a benefit to yourself anything which will force you at some point to break your faith, to leave integrity behind, to hate, suspect, or curse another, to dissemble, to covet anything needing walls and curtains.
凡迫使你在某一刻背弃信义、抛弃正直、憎恨、猜忌或咒骂他人、伪饰假装、贪求需用墙帷遮掩之物的东西，永勿视之为于己有益。
8
In the mind of one who is chastened and cleansed you will find no suppuration, no simmering ulcer, no sore festering under the skin. Fate does not catch him with his life unfulfilled, as one might speak of an actor leaving the stage before his part is finished and the play ended. Moreover there is nothing servile or pretentious, no dependency on others or severance from them, no sense of responsibility, no sense of guilt.
在一个经锤炼而净化之人的心灵中，你找不到化脓之处、找不到慢火灼烧的溃疡，也找不到皮下溃烂的肿疮。命运不会将其生之未满而掳走，犹如人言一伶人未演完戏份之前便离开舞台。此外，其中既无奴颜婢膝，亦无矫揉造作，既无依赖他人，亦无与人疏隔，既无牵挂，亦无罪咎。
9
Revere your power of judgement. All rests on this to make sure that your directing mind no longer entertains any judgement which fails to agree with the nature or the constitution of a rational being. This guarantees deliberate thought, affinity with other men, and obedience to the gods.
敬汝判断之能。一切皆系于此：确保你那主导之心灵不再容纳任何与理性存在者之本性或构造不相合的判断。此即是审慎之思、与人之亲近、以及对诸神之服从的保证。
10
So discard all else and secure these few things only. Remind yourself too that each of us lives only in the present moment, a mere fragment of time: the rest is life past or uncertain future. Sure, life is a small thing, and small the cranny of the earth in which we live it: and small too even the longest fame thereafter, which itself is a succession of puny mortals, who will very soon be dead, with no knowledge of themselves, let alone people long since dead and buried.
故当抛弃其余一切，仅守护此寥寥数事。亦当自省：吾辈每人仅活在此一当下此刻，不过时间之一微片耳：其余或为已逝之生命，或为未定之将来。固然，生命是渺小之物，吾人赖以生存之地之罅隙亦渺小：甚至死后最长之名声亦然渺小——此名不过是递相传承之渺小凡人所传，彼等亦将很快死去，于己身尚且无知，遑论早已故去掩埋之人。
11
One addition to the precepts already mentioned. Always make a definition or sketch of what presents itself to your mind, so you can see it stripped bare to its essential nature and identify it clearly, in its entirety, and tell yourself its proper name, and the names of the elements from which it was compounded and into which it will be dissolved. Nothing so elevates the mind as the ability to examine methodically and truly every object presented to you in life, and always to look at things so as to see at the same time what kind of universe this is, what part it plays in that universe, what value it has in relation to the Whole and what value to man as a citizen of that highest city of which all other cities are like households.
于上述诸训之外，复加一条。凡有事物呈现于你心中，便为之作一定义或草图，如此你能见其赤裸剥离至其本质真性，清晰辨明其全貌，并告己以其确切之名，以及构成与终将分解之元素的名目。没有什么比此更提撕心灵：能以方法逐一真实审视生命中所呈现之每一对象，并恒常如此看待事物——同时洞见此为何等之宇宙、此物在此宇宙中扮演何等角色，与整体相对有何价值，与人——作为那最高之城邦的公民，其他一切城邦不过是此城邦之家族——相对又有何价值。
12
If you set yourself to your present task along the path of true reason, with all determination, vigour, and good will: if you admit no distraction, but keep your own divinity pure and standing strong, as if you must now give it back; if you engage with your task, expecting nothing and avoiding nothing, but content with each present action taken in accordance with nature and with heroic truth in all that you say and speak — then you will live a good life. And there is no one who can prevent this.
若你遵循真正理性之道路，以全部决心、精力与善意，专注于你当下之任务；若你不容任何纷扰，保持你自身之神性纯净而屹立强劲，仿佛此刻便须归还于彼；若你投入其事，无所期待亦无所回避，而满足于每一当下依自然而采取之行动，并于所说所言中持守英雄般之真诚——你便将善度此生。且无人能阻挡你如此。
13
Just as doctors always have their instruments and knives at hand for any emergency treatment, so you should have your doctrines ready for the recognition of the divine and the human, and the performance of even your smallest action in the consciousness of the bond which unites the two.
正如医者随时备有其器械与刀具于手边以应紧急治疗，你亦当备好你的教义——用以辨识神圣与人事，且令你之行止，即便最为琐屑之行动，也均在有意识于联结二者之纽带中进行。
14
No more wandering. You are not likely to read your own jottings, your histories of the ancient Greeks and Romans, your extracts from their literature laid up for your old age. Hurry then to the end, abandon idle hopes, and come to your own rescue, if you have any care for yourself, while the opportunity is still yours.
不要再游荡。你未必会去读你自己那些劄记、那些古希腊罗马史乘、那些为晚年而备辑之文献钞录。那便疾速奔赴终点吧，舍弃虚空之盼，自救于沉溺——若你对自己尚有丝毫顾念，趁机缘尚在手中。
15
They do not know all the meanings of theft, of sowing, of buying, of keeping at rest, of seeing what needs to be done — this is not for the eye, but for a different sort of vision.
彼等不知盗窃之诸义，不知播种之诸义，不知买卖之诸义，不知静守之诸义，不知察见本当为之事——此非肉眼所能见，而需另一种观照。
16
Body, soul, mind. To the body belong sense perceptions, to the soul impulses, to the mind judgements. The receipt of sense impressions is shared with cattle; response to the puppet-strings of impulse is shared with wild beasts, with catamites, with a Phalaris or a Nero; the guide of the mind to its proper objects is shared with those who deny the gods, with those who betray their own country, with those who do anything behind locked doors. So if all else is held in common with the categories mentioned above, it follows that the defining characteristic of the good person is to love and embrace whatever happens to him along his thread of fate; and not to pollute the divinity which is seated within his heart or trouble it with a welter of confused ideas, but to preserve its favour, following god in orderly manner, uttering nothing contrary to truth, doing nothing contrary to justice. And if all people mistrust him, or oppose him, or set upon him, he cannot be shaken. Nevertheless he meets his death with serenity: neither crushed by this nor carried away by his gift, but of himself a willing and unreluctant departure.
身体、魂、心灵。感官知觉属于身体，冲动属于魂，判断属于心灵。接受感官印象，与牲畜所共有；对冲动之提线木偶般回应，与野兽、娈童、法拉里斯或尼禄之辈所共有；以心灵引导至恰当之对象，与否神者、叛国者、以及一切在锁闭之门后为非作歹者所共有。故，若其他一切皆与上述诸类所共有，则善人之界定特征便是：爱且拥抱沿其命运之线落到他身上的一切遭遇；且不以混乱芜杂之念污染安座其内心之神性，亦不以此搅扰之，而是有序列地追随神明，保守其恩眷，口不出违背真理之言，事不有违正义之行。纵使众人不信任他、反对他、围攻他，他亦岿然不动。纵是如此，他坦然赴死：既不被死亡所摧垮，亦不为其禀赋所裹挟飘然，而乃出于自己意愿、无所勉强地辞行。

沉思录 卷四

1
Wherever it is in agreement with nature, the ruling power within us takes a flexible approach to circumstances, always adapting itself easily to both practicality and the given event. It has no favoured material for its work, but sets out on its objects in a conditional way, turning any obstacle into material for its own use. It is like a fire mastering whatever falls into it. A small flame would be extinguished, but a bright fire rapidly claims as its own all that is heaped on it, devours it all, and leaps up yet higher in consequence.
凡与自然相应合之处，吾人内在之统摄力皆以柔韧之姿迎接际遇，恒能轻松调适自身，既合乎实际，亦顺应所临之事。其于作为之事并无偏嗜之材料，而以有条件的方式趋向其对象，将一切障碍皆转为自己所用之资。犹如火焰，征服一切落入其中之物。微焰或为所灭，而炽盛之火则迅速将一切所加于其上者据为己有，吞尽一切，由此燃得愈发高涨。
2
No action should be undertaken without aim, or other than in conformity with a principle affirming the art of life.
凡有所为，必抱宗旨，必令其符合肯定生命艺术之原则。
3
Men seek retreats for themselves — in the country, by the sea, in the hills — and you yourself are particularly prone to this yearning. But all this is quite unphilosophic, when it is open to you, at any time you want, to retreat into yourself. No retreat offers someone more quiet and relaxation than that into his own mind, especially if he can dip into thoughts there which put him at immediate and complete ease: and by ease I simply mean a well-ordered life. So constantly give yourself this retreat, and renew yourself. The doctrines you will visit there should be few and fundamental, sufficient at one meeting to wash away all your pain and send you back free of resentment at what you must rejoin.
世人各寻隐退之所——或于乡间，或于海滨，或于山野——你自身亦特别易生此念。然凡此种种，皆颇不哲学，因你随时可以退隐于己身之内。世间无一隐退之所，比退归己之心灵更为安宁、更为宽弛，尤其若你能沉浸于彼处之思想，令你即刻全然安然——而我所言之安然，不过是一种秩序井然之生活。故当时时予自己此等退隐，更新自己。你将访寻之教义，当寥寥而根本，一次晤对便足以涤去你所有之痛苦，并使你毫无怨怼地重返你所必须面对之一切。
4
If mind is common to us all, then we have reason also in common — that which makes us rational beings. If so, then common too is the reason which dictates what we should or should not do. If so, then law too is common to us all. If so, then we are citizens. If so, we share in a constitution. If so, the universe is a kind of community. In what else could one say that the whole human race shares a common constitution? From there, then, this common city, we take our very mind, our reason, our law — from where else? Just as the earthy part of me has been derived from some earth, the watery from the next element, the air of my breath from some other source, the hot and fiery from its own origin (for nothing comes from nothing, nor returns to nothing) — so the mind also has its source.
若心灵为人人所共有，则吾辈亦共有理性——那使我们成为理性存在者之物。若是，则那规定何者当为何者不当为之理性，亦是共有的。若是，则法律亦为人人所共有。若是，则吾辈皆为公民。若是，则吾辈共享一种体制。若是，则宇宙乃某种共同体。除此之外，还能说全人类在何处共享一种共同之体制？自此，自这一共同之城邦，吾辈领受了我们之心智、我们之理性、我们之法律——复能从何处？正如我身中土性之部分源自某处之土，水性之部分源自邻近之元素，呼吸之气息源自另一来源，热与火则源自其自身之本原（因无物自虚无中生出，亦无物归于虚无）——心智亦有其源头。
5
Death, just like birth, is a mystery of nature: first a combination, then a dissolution, of the same elements. Certainly no cause for shame: because nothing out of the order for an intelligent being or contrary to the principle of his constitution.
死与生一般，同属自然之奥秘：同一批元素，先为组合，后乃分解。确然无可羞惭：因此事既不逸出理性存在者之秩序，亦不与其构造之理相悖。
6
With such people such an outcome is both natural and inevitable — if you wish it otherwise you are hoping that figs will no longer produce their rennet. In any case remember that in a very brief time both you and he will be dead, and shortly after not even your names will be left.
与如此之人相遇而得如此之结果，既自然亦不可免——你若愿其不然，便是在盼无花果不再泌其凝乳。无论如何，当记取：短暂岁月之内，你与他皆将死去，再过不久，连你们的名字亦不复存留。
7
Remove the judgement, and you have removed the thought 'I am hurt': remove the thought 'I am hurt', and the hurt itself is removed.
去其判断，则「我受伤害」之念已去；去「我受伤害」之念，则伤害本身已去。
8
What does not make a human being worse in himself cannot make his life worse either: it cannot harm him from outside or inside.
凡不能使人自身变得更坏者，亦不能使人之生命变得更坏：此事无论自外或自内，皆不能伤害他。
9
The nature of the beneficial was bound to act thus.
有益者之本性，必然如此行事。
10
'All's right that happens in the world.' Examine this saying carefully, and you will find it true. I do not mean 'right' simply in the context of cause and effect, but in the sense of 'just' — as if some adjudicator were assigning dues. So keep on observing this, as you have started, and in all that you do combine doing it with being a good man, in the specific conception of 'good man'. Preserve this in every sphere of action.
「世间所发生之一切，皆是得当的。」细察此言，你将发现其真。我所言之「得当」，非仅因果关联之意，而是如某裁判官在分派所应得一般——意即「公正」。故当如你已然开始那般，继续遵从此一观察，并在你作为一切中，兼具善人之行止，以「善人」之确切涵义。于每一行动领域，皆当持守此道。
11
When someone does you wrong, do not judge things as he interprets them or would like you to interpret them. Just see them as they are, in plain truth.
有人对你行事不义之时，勿以其人所解读或愿你所解读之方式来判断事物。唯以其本来面目见之，以素朴之真实。
12
Always have these two principles in readiness. First, to do only what the reason inherent in kingly and judicial power prescribes for the benefit of mankind. Second, to change your ground, if in fact there is someone to correct and guide you away from some notion. But this transference must always spring from a conviction of justice or the common good: and your preferred course must be likewise, not simply for apparent pleasure or popularity.
当时时备好此二原则。其一：仅行那内在于君权与司法权中之理性为人类之福祉所规定之事。其二：若确有某人能够纠正你、引导你脱开某种成见，便变更你的立场。然此一转移必当发自对正义或公共福祉之确信：且你所偏向之路径亦必当如是一样，非仅为表面之快意或人望。
13
'Do you possess reason?' 'I do.' 'Why not use it then? With reason doing its job, what else do you want?'
「你拥有理性否？」「我确有。」「那为何不用之？理性尽其职分之际，你还有何他求？」
14
You have subsisted as a part of the Whole. You will vanish into that which gave you birth: or rather you will be changed, taken up into the generative principle of the universe.
你曾作为整体之一部分而存续。你将消逝于那赋予你生命者之中：或更确切说，你将转化，被纳入宇宙之创生原理之中。
15
Many grains of incense on the same altar. One falls to ash first, another later: no difference.
诸多香粒同在一祭坛之上。其一先化为灰，另一稍后而已：并无分别。
16
Within ten days you will be regarded as a god by those very people who now see you as beast or baboon — if you return to your principles and the worship of Reason.
十日之内，如今视你为野兽或狒狒之人，便将视你为神明——若你复归于你的原则，复归于对理性之崇奉。
17
No, you do not have thousands of years to live. Urgency is on you. While you live, while you can, become good.
不，你没有千年可活。紧迫在你身。在你还活着、还能的时候，成为善的。
18
What ease of mind you gain from not looking at what your neighbour has said or done or thought, but only at your own actions, to make them just, reverential, imbued with good! So do not glance at the black characters either side, but run right on to the line: straight, not straggly.
你若不去看邻人说了什么、做了什么、想了什么，而仅关注己之行动，使之公正、虔敬、浸染善意——你将获致何等之心安！故勿左右瞥视那些晦暗之字，径直跑向底线：直道而行，不令偏斜。
19
One who is all in a flutter over his subsequent fame fails to imagine that all those who remember him will very soon be dead — and he too. Then the same will be true of all successors, until the whole memory of him will be extinguished in a sequence of lamps lit and snuffed out. But suppose immortality in those who will remember you, and everlasting memory. Even so, what is that to you? And I do not simply mean that this is nothing to the dead, but to the living also what is the point of praise, other than for some practical aspect of management? As it is, you are losing the opportunity of that gift of nature which does not depend on another's word.
为身后之名而焦躁不安者，未能想见：所有记得他的人很快也将死去——他自己亦是一样。而后，代代相继者皆如此，直至关于他的一切记忆在灯烛之递相点燃与熄灭中全然消失。然而，纵使记得你的人永生不死，记忆也永远长存。即便如此，这于你又有何干？我非仅言此于死者毫无意义：于生者，赞誉之意义何在，除了在某种实用之安排层面？事实是，你正在丧失自然所赋予的、不赖他人之辞的禀赋良机。
20
Everything in any way beautiful has its beauty of itself, inherent and self-sufficient: praise is no part of it. At any rate, praise does not make anything better or worse. This applies even to the popular conception of beauty, as in material things or works or art. So does the truly beautiful need anything beyond itself? No more than law, no more than truth, no more than kindness or integrity. Which of these things derives its beauty from praise, or withers under criticism? Does an emerald lose its quality if it is not praised? And what of gold, ivory, purple, a lyre, a dagger, a flower, a bush?
一切在某种意义上是美的东西，其美皆在于自身，内在而自足：赞誉并非其组成部分。无论如何，赞誉不能使任何事物变得更好或更坏。此理甚至适用于通行的美之观念，如物质对象或艺术作品。那么，真正之美还需要自身之外的任何东西吗？不多于法律，不多于真理，不多于仁慈或正直。此等事物中，何者因赞誉而得其美，或因批评而凋萎？一颗祖母绿若无人称赞，便失其品质乎？金、象牙、紫袍、竖琴、匕首、一朵花、一丛灌木，复又如何？
21
You may ask how, if souls live on, the air can accommodate them all from the beginning of time. Well, how does the earth accommodate all those bodies buried in it over the same eternity? Just as here on earth, once bodies have kept their residence for whatever time, their change and decomposition makes room for other bodies, so it is with souls migrated to the air. They continue for a time, then change, dissolve, and take fire as they are assumed into the generative principle of the Whole: in this way they make room for successive residents.
你或许会问：若灵魂继续存活，那空气何以能自时间肇始以来容纳如此众多的灵魂？好，那么大地又是如何容纳那同样永恒以来葬于其中的众多躯体？正如在地面之上，躯体居留随心之时日后，其变化与分解便为其他躯体腾出了空间，移居于空气中之灵魂亦是如此。它们存续一时，然后变化、分解、燃烧，被纳入宇宙整体之创生原理：如此这般，它们为后来之居者腾出了空间。
22
No wandering. In every impulse, give what is right: in every thought, stick to what is certain.
勿再游荡。每一冲动中，给出正当之物；每一思想中，坚守确实之物。
23
Universe, your harmony is my harmony: nothing in your good time is too early or too late for me. Nature, all that your seasons bring is fruit to me: all comes from you, exists in you, returns to you. The poet says, 'Dear city of Cecrops': will you not say, 'Dear city of Zeus'?
宇宙，你的和谐即是我的和谐：在你恰当之时令中，无一物于我太早或太迟。自然，你的季节所带来之一切皆为我之果实：一切自你而来，存于你中，归于你中。诗人说「亲爱的刻克洛普斯之城」：你岂不能说「亲爱的宙斯之城」？
24
'If you want to be happy', says Democritus, 'do little.' May it not be better to do what is necessary, what the reason of a naturally social being demands, and the way reason demands it done? This brings the happiness both of right action and of little action. Most of what we say and do is unnecessary: remove the superfluity, and you will have more time and less bother. So in every case one should prompt oneself: 'Is this, or is it not, something necessary?' And the removal of the unnecessary should apply not only to actions but to thoughts also: then no redundant actions either will follow.
德谟克利特说：「你若想快乐，便做少些。」岂非更好的乃是：做必要之事——即那作为天生社会性存在者之理性所要求之事，并依理性所要求的方式去做？这既带来了正确行动之乐，也带来了行动不多之乐。吾辈所言所行，多半皆非必要：去除多余，你将获更多时间，更少烦恼。故在每一情形下，人当自问：「此事是必要的，抑或不是？」而去除不必要之事，不仅应用于行动，亦当应用于思想：如此，则多余的举动亦不会随之而来。
25
Try out too how the life of the good man goes for you — the man content with his dispensation from the Whole, and satisfied in his own just action and kind disposition.
也不妨一试善人之活法于你如何——那种满足于整体所分配于己之一切，并在一己之公正行动与仁厚性情中获得满足的人的生活。
26
You have seen that: now look at this. Do not trouble yourself, keep yourself simple. Someone does wrong? He does wrong to himself. Has something happened to you? Fine. All that happens has been fated by the Whole from the beginning and spun for your own destiny. In sum, life is short: make your gain from the present moment with right reason and justice. Keep sober and relaxed.
你已见彼：现在看此。勿自扰扰，保持简单。有人行不义？他乃行不义于己身。有某事临到你？善。凡所发生，皆为整体自太初所注定，并为你的命运所纺成。一言蔽之，生命短促：以正确理性与公正，从当下此刻获益。保持清醒与从容。
27
Either an ordered universe, or a stew of mixed ingredients, yet still coherent order. Otherwise how could a sort of private order subsist within you, if there is disorder in the Whole? Especially given that all things, distinct as they are, nevertheless permeate and respond to each other.
要么是秩序井然的宇宙，要么是由混杂原料般搅和而成、却仍具某种连贯秩序之物。否则，若整体中是混乱，一种内在之秩序又如何能在你身内存在？尤其众物虽彼此殊异，却相互渗透、相互应和。
28
A black character, an effeminate, unbending character, the character of a brute or dumb animal: infantile, stupid, fraudulent, coarse, mercenary, despotic.
阴暗之品性、柔弱之品性、顽固之品性——犹如野兽或哑畜之品性：幼稚、愚蠢、欺诈、粗俗、唯利是图、暴虐专横。
29
If one who does not recognize the contents of the universe is a stranger in it, no less a stranger is the one who fails to recognize what happens in it. He is a fugitive if he runs away from social principle; blind, if he shuts the eye of the mind; a beggar, if he depends on others and does not possess within him all he needs for life; a tumour on the universe, if he stands aside and separates himself from the principle of our common nature in disaffection with his lot (for it is nature which brings this about, just as it brought you about too); a social splinter, if he splits his own soul away from the soul of all rational beings, which is a unity.
若有人不识宇宙之内容而为其间之异乡人，那么不识其间所发生之事者，亦同样是异乡人。逃离社会原则者，是逃犯；闭上心灵之眼者，是盲人；依赖他人、不于自身内怀具生命所需一切者，是乞丐；远离并因对自己遭际之不满而将自己与吾辈共同自然之原则分离开来者，是宇宙上之肿囊（因正是自然带来了这些遭际，正如她带来了你一样）；将自己的灵魂从那统一整体的理性存在者之灵魂中分裂开来者，是社会之碎片。
30
One philosopher has no shirt, one has no book. Here is another half-naked: 'I have no bread', he says, 'but I am faithful to Reason.' But I for my part have all the food of learning, and yet I am not faithful.
一位哲学家无衣遮体，一位无书可读。此处更有一位半裸之人：「我没有面包，」他说，「然我忠于理性。」然而就我而言，我满有学问之一切食粮，却未曾忠心。
31
Love the art which you have learnt, and take comfort in it. Go through the remainder of your life in sincere commitment of all your being to the gods, and never making yourself tyrant or slave to any man.
爱你所学得的技艺，并从中得安慰。度过余生，以你全副生命真诚委身于诸神，且永勿使自己成为任何人的暴君或奴隶。
32
Consider, for example, the time of Vespasian. You will see everything the same. People marrying, having children, falling ill, dying, fighting, feasting, trading, farming, flattering, pushing, suspecting, plotting, praying for the death of others, grumbling at their lot, falling in love, storing up wealth, longing for consulships and kingships. And now that life of theirs is gone, vanished. Pass on again to the time of Trajan. Again, everything the same. That life too is dead. Similarly, look at the histories of other eras and indeed whole nations, and see how many lives of striving met with a quick fall and resolution into the elements.
试想，譬如韦斯巴芗之时代。你眼前将见一切皆同。人们婚嫁、育子、患病、死亡、交战、宴饮、交易、耕作、谄媚、钻营、猜忌、图谋、祈愿他人早死、抱怨自身命运、坠入情网、囤积财富、渴望执政官与王座。而今，他们那一切生活已去，杳然无踪。再转到图拉真之时代。复次，一切皆同。彼等生命亦已死去。同样，审阅其他时代、乃至整个整个民族的史记，看看多少奋力拼搏的生命转眼间坠落，消解为元素之中。
33
Words in common use long ago are obsolete now. So too the names of those once famed are in a sense obsolete — Camillus, Caeso, Volesus, Dentatus; a little later Scipio and Cato, then Augustus too, then Hadrian and Antoninus. All things fade and quickly turn to myth: quickly too utter oblivion drowns them. And I am talking of those who shone with some wonderful brilliance: the rest, once they have breathed their last, are immediately 'beyond sight, beyond knowledge'. But what in any case is everlasting memory? Utter emptiness. So where should a man direct his endeavour? Here only — a right mind, action for the common good, speech incapable of lies, a disposition to welcome all that happens as necessary, intelligible, flowing from an equally intelligible spring of origin.
往昔通行之辞语如今已废。同样，那些曾经煊赫一时之人名，在某种意义上亦已作废——卡米卢斯、凯索、沃莱苏斯、登塔图斯；稍后则有西庇阿与加图，然后是奥古斯都，再后是哈德良与安东尼努斯。一切皆褪色，迅速化为传说：然后彻底的遗忘迅即淹没它们。我所讲的还是那些曾以某种璀璨光芒闪耀过的人：其余人等，一旦咽下最后一口气，当即「不在视线之中，不在知见之内」。然而，所谓永恒的记忆究竟是什么呢？全然虚空。那么，人该将努力指向何处？唯此一处——正确之心灵、为社会公益之行动、无谎言之言语、一种欢迎一切所发生之事如必然、如可解、如自一同样可解之元始源泉中流出之性情。
34
Gladly surrender yourself to Clotho: let her spin your thread into whatever web she wills.
欣然地把自己交给克洛托吧：让她将你的生命之线纺入她所乐意的任何织物中去。
35
All is ephemeral, both memory and the object of memory.
一切皆朝生暮死，记忆如此，被记忆者亦如此。
36
Constantly observe all that comes about through change, and habituate yourself to the thought that the nature of the Whole loves nothing so much as to change one form of existence into another, similar but new. All that exists is in a sense the seed of its successor: but your concept of 'seed' is simply what is put into the earth or the womb — that is very unphilosophic thinking.
恒常观察一切通过变化而生起之物，并令自己习惯于如下思想：宇宙整体之自然，最爱之事莫过于将存在之一种形式变换为另一种，相似然而是新的。一切存在之物，在某种意义上皆为其后继者之种子：然而你所理解的「种子」仅为投入泥土或子宫者——那是极不哲学的思考。
37
Your death will soon be on you: and you are not yet clear-minded, or untroubled, or free from the fear of external harm, or kindly to all people, or convinced that justice of action is the only wisdom.
死亡很快便会临到你身：而你尚未清明，尚未安宁，尚未摆脱对外在伤害之恐惧，尚未对所有人怀仁善之心，尚未确信行动之公正即是唯一之智慧。
38
Look into their directing minds: observe what even the wise will avoid or pursue.
察视那些主导之心灵：观察即便是智者，其所当避与所当追者为何。
39
Harm to you cannot subsist in another's directing mind, nor indeed in any turn or change of circumstance. Where, then? In that part of you which judges harm. So no such judgement, and all is well. Even if what is closest to it, your own body, is subjected to knife or cautery, or left to suppurate or mortify, even so that faculty in you which judges these things should stay untroubled. That is, it should assess nothing either bad or good which can happen equally to the bad man or the good: because what can happen to a man irrespective of his life's conformity to nature is not of itself either in accordance with nature or contrary to it.
于你之伤害，不能存在于他人之主导心灵中，亦不能存在于任何环境之转变或变化中。那么，存在于何处？在你那判断伤害的那一部分中。故不作如是判断，一切便安然。即使那与之最为切近者——你自己的身体——遭受刀割或烙灼，或任其化脓或枯死，纵然如此，你身中判断此等事之官能也应保持不为所扰。也就是说，它不应将那些可同等降临于恶人与善人之事，判为恶或善：因为能够不加分别地发生在一个人身上的事——不论他的生命是否符合自然——其本身既非符合自然，亦非违背自然。
40
Think always of the universe as one living creature, comprising one substance and one soul: how all is absorbed into this one consciousness; how a single impulse governs all its actions; how all things collaborate in all that happens; the very web and mesh of it all.
恒将宇宙思为一活物，由一实体与一灵魂所构成：一切如何被摄入此一意识之中；单一之冲动如何统摄其所有之行动；万物如何在所发生之一切中协和共作；那正是这一切之经纬与网络。
41
You are a soul carrying a corpse, as Epictetus used to say.
你是一个携带着一具死尸的灵魂，如爱比克泰德常言。
42
Change: nothing inherently bad in the process, nothing inherently good in the result.
变化：过程之中无固有之恶，结果之中亦无固有之善。
43
There is a river of creation, and time is a violent stream. As soon as one thing comes into sight, it is swept past and another is carried down: it too will be taken on its way.
有创造之河，时间则是一条湍急之流。一物方入眼帘，便被冲卷而过，另一物又被挟裹而下：此物亦将被带走于其路途之上。
44
All that happens is as habitual and familiar as roses in spring and fruit in the summer. True too of disease, death, defamation, and conspiracy — and all that delights or gives pain to fools.
一切所发生者，其惯常与熟悉正如春之玫瑰、夏之果实。疾病、死亡、诽谤、阴谋——以及一切令愚人快乐或痛苦之物，也莫不如此。
45
What comes after is always in affinity to what went before. Not some simple enumeration of disparate things and a merely necessary sequence, but a rational connection: and just as existing things are harmoniously interconnected, so the processes of becoming exhibit no mere succession, but a wonderfully inherent affinity.
后来之物总是与先前之物相亲相近。并非简单一个杂多事物之罗列，亦非仅仅是必然之前后序列，而是一种理性的联结：且正如存有之物和谐地彼此相连，那生成过程所展示的也非仅是先后接续，而是一种令人惊叹的内在亲和。
46
Always remember Heraclitus: 'The death of earth is the birth of water; the death of water is the birth of air; the death of air is fire, and back again.' Remember too his image of the man who forgets his way home; his saying that men are at odds with their most constant companion, the Reason which governs all things; that their everyday experience takes them by surprise; that we must not act or speak as if asleep, and sleep brings the illusion of speech and action; and that we should not be like children with their parents, simply accepting what we are told.
当时时铭记赫拉克利特：「土之死是水之生；水之死是气之生；气之死是火，复又循环。」也铭记他那个忘记归家之路的人的比喻；他所说的：人与他们最为恒常之伴侣——那统御万物的理性——相抵牾；人对他们日常之经验，总是猝不及防；我们不应如在梦中一般行动或言说，而睡眠所带来的不过是言说与行动之幻象；以及：我们不应像孩童对父母那般，简单地接受被告知的一切。
47
Just as if a god told you that you would die tomorrow or at least the day after tomorrow, you would attach no importance to the difference of one day, unless you are a complete coward (such is the tiny gap of time): so you should think there no great difference between life to the umpteenth year and life to tomorrow.
正如若有神明告你，你将于明日或至少后日死去，你不会把这一日之差看作有何紧要——除非你是个彻头彻尾之懦夫（时距之微，不过若此）：同样，你当思活到某遥远年岁与活到明日，并无大异。
48
Think constantly how many doctors have died, after knitting their brows over their own patients; how many astrologers, after predicting the deaths of others, as if death were something important; how many philosophers, after endless deliberation on death or immortality; how many heroes, after the many others they killed; how many tyrants, after using their power over men's lives with monstrous insolence, as if they themselves were immortal. Think too how many whole cities have 'died' — Helice, Pompeii, Herculaneum, innumerable others. Go over now all those you have known yourself, one after the other: one man follows a friend's funeral and is then laid out himself, then another follows him — and all in a brief space of time. The conclusion of this? You should always look on human life as short and cheap. Yesterday sperm: tomorrow a mummy or ashes.
请恒常思忖：多少医者在对己之病患皱眉凝思之后，自己便死去了；多少占星家在预言了他人之死亡——彷佛死亡是桩大事——之后，自己也面临了命定之日；多少哲学家在对死亡与不朽作了无尽之思辨之后；多少英雄在杀了众多他人之后；多少暴君在以可怖的傲慢运用其对人之生命权柄、彷佛自己永生不死之后。也请想想多少整座城邦已「死」——赫利刻、庞贝、赫库兰尼姆，以及无数其他。如今，再逐一回想你自己所知之人：一人参加友人之葬仪，随后自己亦躺在那里；另一人又随之而来——而一切不过发生在片刻之间。由此能得出何种结论？你当永视人生为短暂与不贵。昨为精液，明为木乃伊或灰烬。
49
So one should pass through this tiny fragment of time in tune with nature, and leave it gladly, as an olive might fall when ripe, blessing the earth which bore it and grateful to the tree which gave it growth. Be like the rocky headland on which the waves constantly break. It stands firm, and round it the seething waters are laid to rest. 'It is my bad luck that this has happened to me.' No, you should rather say: 'It is my good luck that, although this has happened to me, I can bear it without pain, neither crushed by the present nor fearful of the future.' Because such a thing could have happened to any man, but not every man could have borne it without pain. So why see more misfortune in the event than good fortune in your ability to bear it?
故人当与自然相协调，度过这时间之微小片段，并欣然离之，犹如一颗橄榄在其成熟时坠落，祝福生出它的土地，感激赋予它生长的树木。要像那岩石之海岬，任凭波浪不断拍击。它屹立不移，四周滚沸之水便归于平静。有人叹：「此灾降于我，真是厄运。」不，你更当说：「此灾虽降于我，我却能安然承受，不为当下所摧，亦不畏惧将来——这正是好运。」因这样的事可发生在任何人身上，但并非人人皆能安然承受。因此，为何要在事件中见出更多不幸，而不在你承受之能力中见出更多好运？
50
An unphilosophic but nonetheless effective help to putting death in its place is to run over the list of those who have clung long to life. What did they gain over the untimely dead? At any rate they are all in their graves by now — Caedicianus, Fabius, Julianus, Lepidus, and all others like them who took part in many funerals and then their own. In truth, the distance we have to travel is small: and we drag it out with such labour, in such poor company, in such a feeble body. No great thing, then. Look behind you at the huge gulf of time, and another infinity ahead. In this perspective what is the difference between an infant of three days and a Nestor of three generations?
一种不很哲学、但同样有效以安放死亡之死的方法，就是指历那些久久贪执生命之人。他们较之夭亡者，有何多得？无论如何，如今他们皆已在坟墓之中——凯迪奇亚努斯、法比乌斯、尤利安努斯、雷庇杜斯，以及所有类似之人，他们曾参加无数葬礼，然后自己也成为被葬者。事实上，所行之路短而又短：我们却以如此之劳苦、伴着如此糟糕之同伴、依靠如此脆弱之身体，延拖苟延。诚非大事。回顾你身后那时间之鸿渊，前瞻另一无限之无际。于此透观之下，三日之婴孩与年及三代的涅斯托耳，又有何异？
51
Always run on the short road: and nature's road is short. Go then for the healthiest in all you say and do. Such a purpose releases a man from the labours of service, from all need to manage or impress.
时时常取短途而行：自然之道本即简短。那便在你一切言行之中，取最健全之路。如此目的，使人从劳役之事务中释放，从经营与取悦于人的一切必要中释放。

沉思录 卷五

1
At break of day, when you are reluctant to get up, have this thought ready to mind: 'I am getting up for a man's work. Do I still then resent it, if I am going out to do what I was born for, the purpose for which I was brought into the world? Or was I created to wrap myself in blankets and keep warm?' 'But this is more pleasant.' Were you then born for pleasure — all for feeling, not for action? Can you not see plants, birds, ants, spiders, bees all doing their own work, each helping in their own way to order the world? And then you do not want to do the work of a human being — you do not hurry to the demands of your own nature. 'But one needs rest too.' One does indeed: I agree. But nature has set limits to this too, just as it has to eating and drinking, and yet you go beyond these limits, beyond what you need. Not in your actions, though, not any longer: here you stay below your capability. The point is that you do not love yourself — otherwise you would love both your own nature and her purpose for you. Other men love their own pursuit and absorb themselves in its performance to the exclusion of bath and food: but you have less regard for your own nature than the smith has for his metal-work, the dancer for his dancing, the money-grubber for his money, the exhibitionist for his little moment of fame. Yet these people, when impassioned, give up food and sleep for the promotion of their pursuits: and you think social action less important, less worthy of effort?

A second is not so quick in that way, but even so he privately thinks of the other as his debtor, and is well aware of what he has done. A third sort is in a way not even conscious of his action, but is like the vine which has produced grapes and looks for nothing else once it has borne its own fruit. A horse that has raced, a dog that has tracked, a bee that has made honey, and a man that has done good — none of these knows what they have done, but they pass on to the next action, just as the vine passes on to bear grapes again in due season. So you ought to be one of those who, in a sense, are unconscious of the good they do.

A prayer of the Athenian people: 'Rain, rain, dear Zeus, on the cornland and the plain of Attica.' Prayer should be thus simple and open, or not at all. Just as it is commonly said that Asclepius has prescribed someone horse-riding, or cold baths, or walking barefoot, so we could say that the nature of the Whole has prescribed him disease, disablement, loss, or any other such affliction. In the first case 'prescribed' means something like this: 'ordered this course for this person as conducive to his health'. In the second the meaning is that what happens to each individual is somehow arranged to conduce to his destiny.
雅典人有一句祈祷：「降雨，降雨，亲爱的宙斯，降在谷田与阿提卡平原之上吧。」祈祷当如是简单而开阔，否则便不若无。正如人常说阿斯克勒庇俄斯给某人开了骑马、冷水浴或赤足行走之方子，我们同样可以说，宇宙整体之自然给某人开了疾病、残障、丧损，或任何其他类似之苦楚。在前一种情形，「开方」之意约略若此：「为此人规定此一疗程，以便有益于他的健康。」在后一种情形，其意乃是：每一人所遭逢之事，均被以某种方式安排得有益于他的命运。

他人做错事。那与我何干？让他自去理会：他有他自己的性情，他自有他自己的作为。我此刻则拥有宇宙自然所愿我此刻拥有之物，我则做我自身本性愿我此刻所做之事。
26
The directing and sovereign part of your soul must stay immune to any current in the flesh, either smooth or troubled, and keep its independence: it must define its own sphere and confine those affections to the parts they affect.
你灵魂中主导与统御之部分，必须对肉身中一切流动——无论其平和或骚乱——保持免疫，守护其独立：它必须划定自己的领域，并将那些感受局限于它们所影响的相应部分。
27
'Live with the gods.' He lives with the gods who consistently shows them his soul content with its lot, and performing the wishes of that divinity, that fragment of himself which Zeus has given each person to guard and guide him. In each of us this divinity is our mind and reason.
「与诸神同住。」凡恒常向诸神示现一己之灵魂满足于其所分配之一切、且执行那神性之愿望的人——那神性即宙斯赋予每人以护卫并引导他自己的、己身之碎片——便是与诸神同住者。在我等每人之中，此一神性便是我们之心智与理性。
28
Are you angry with the man who smells like a goat, or the one with foul breath? What will you have him do? That's the way his mouth is, that's the way his armpits are, so it is inevitable that they should give out odours to match. 'But the man is endowed with reason', you say, 'and if he puts his mind to it he can work out why he causes offence.' Well, good for you! So you too are no less endowed with reason: bring your rationality, then, to bear on his rationality — show him, tell him. If he listens, you will cure him, and no need for anger. Neither hypocrite nor whore.
你生那气味如山羊之人的气吗，或那口有秽息者？你要他怎样呢？他的嘴就是那样的，他之腋下便是那样的，故不可免地，散发的气味也就与之相配。「可是这人身秉理性」，你说道，「若他用心想过，便能明白为何他会惹人反感。」不错，说得对！那么你也不缺理性：便用你的理性来作用于他的理性——明示他，告诉他。若他听从，你便治愈了他，也无需动怒。既非伪君子，也非娼妓。
29
You can live here in this world just as you intend to live when you have left it. But if this is not allowed you, then you should depart life itself — but not as if this were some misfortune. 'The fire smokes and I leave the house.' Why think this any big matter? But as long as no such thing drives me out, I remain a free man and no one will prevent me doing what I wish to do: and my wish is to follow the nature of a rational and social being.
你可以在此世中如此生活，正如你意欲在辞别此生之后那般生活。但若情形不容许你如此，那你便当辞别生命本身——但勿如遭遇祸殃一般。「屋子冒烟了，我离开这间屋。」何必视此为大事？然而，只要没有这等事将我驱赶出去，我便仍是一个自由人，无人能阻我做我所愿做之事：而我所愿的，乃是遵循一个理性且社会性存在者之本性。
30
The intelligence of the Whole is a social intelligence. Certainly it has made the lower for the sake of the higher, and set the higher in harmony with each other. You can see how it has subordinated some creatures, coordinated others, given each its proper place, and brought together the superior beings in unity of mind.
宇宙整体之理智，是一种社会性之理智。它确然将低者造为高者之缘故，并使高者彼此和谐相处。你可见它是如何将某些造物置于下位，将另些造物并列协动，赋予每者以恰当之位，并将那优越的存在者们汇聚于心灵之合中。
31
How have you behaved up to now towards gods, parents, brother, wife, children, teachers, tutors, friends, relations, servants? Has your principle up to now with all of these been 'say no evil, do no evil'? Remind yourself what you have been through and had the strength to endure; that the story of your life is fully told and your service completed; how often you have seen beauty, disregarded pleasure and pain, forgone glory, and been kind to the unkind.
至今为止，你对诸神、父母、兄弟、妻子、孩子、师长、保傅、朋友、亲族、仆役，行为如何？至今为止，你于这一切人的原则，是否乃是「不出恶语、不为恶行」？提醒自己：你经历过何等事，有力量承受过何等事；你生命的故事已全然讲完，你的服役已然完成；你多少次看见过美，漠视过乐与苦，放弃过荣耀，并以仁善待不善者。
32
Why do unskilled and ignorant minds confound the skilful and the wise? Well, what is the mind of true skill and wisdom? It is the mind which knows the beginning and the end, and knows the Reason which informs all of existence and governs the Whole in appointed cycles through all eternity.
为何无技艺与无知之心灵，会困惑那有技艺与有智慧者？那么，什么才是真正的技艺与智慧之心灵？那便是知晓开端与终局、知晓那贯透一切存有之理性、并知晓那在指定之周期中于一切永恒里统御整体之理性的心灵。
34
You can always ensure the right current to your life if you can first follow the right path — if, that is, your judgements and actions follow the path of reason. There are two things common to the souls of all rational creatures, god or man: they are immune to any external impediment, and the good they seek resides in a just disposition and just action, with this the limit of their desire.
你若能先遵循那正路——即，你的判断与行动皆遵循理性之途——便总能保证你生命之流向正直。有两事为一切理性造物、不论神或人之灵魂所共有：它们不受任何外在阻碍之影响；且它们所寻求之善好在于正直之性向与正直之行动，以此为它们欲望之界限。
35
If this is no wrongdoing of mine, nor the result of any wrong done to me, and if the community is not harmed, then why do I let it trouble me? And what is the harm that can be done to the community?
若此事非我之过错，亦非我受任何不义侵害之所导致，且若共同体亦未受伤害，那我又为何任其困扰于我？究竟什么事能对共同体造成伤害？
36
Don't let the impression of other people's grief carry you away indiscriminately. Help them, yes, as best you can and as the case deserves, even if their grief is for the loss of something indifferent: but do not imagine their loss as any real harm — that is the wrong way of thinking. Rather, you should be like the old man in the play who reclaimed at the end his foster-child's favourite toy, never forgetting that it was only a toy.
勿让对他人悲伤之印象不加分别地席卷你而去。帮助他们，固然——尽你所能，亦如情势所应得，纵使他们之悲伤是因失去了某件无足轻重之物：但不要将他们的损失想象为任何真正的伤害——那是错误之思维。相反，你当如同戏剧中的那位老人，在终场时将他的养子心爱的玩具索回，心里从未忘记那只是玩具而已。
37
'There was a time when I met luck at every turn.' But luck is the good fortune you determine for yourself: and good fortune consists in good inclinations of the soul, good impulses, good actions.
「曾几何时，我处处逢遇好运。」然而，运气乃是你为自己所决定之好运：而好运取决于灵魂之良好倾向、良好冲动、良好行动。

沉思录 卷六

1
The substance of the Whole is passive and malleable, and the reason directing this substance has no cause in itself to do wrong, as there is no wrong in it: nothing it creates is wrongly made, nothing harmed by it. All things have their beginning and their end in accordance with it.
宇宙整体之实体是顺从并可塑的，而引导此实体之理性本身并无作恶之由，因其内毫无邪恶：它所创生之物无一被造错，无一受其损害。万物皆依照它而有其起始与终结。
2
If you are doing your proper duty let it not matter to you whether you are cold or warm, whether you are sleepy or well-slept, whether men speak badly or well of you, even whether you are on the point of death or doing something else: because even this, the act of dying, is one of the acts of life. So it is enough 'to make the best of this move on to the next'.
若你正在执行你之本分，便无谓你或冷或暖，无论困倦抑或已得饱睡，无论人言于你是好是恶，甚至无论你是临死之际或正做他事：因为即便是死亡这一行为，亦乃生命之诸行为之一。故「善用当前之一步，随即转入下一步」，便已足够。
3
Look within: do not allow the special quality or worth of any thing to pass you by.
反观于内：莫令任何事物之独特品性或价值从你眼下滑过。
4
All that exists will soon change. Either it will be turned into vapour, if all matter is a unity, or it will be scattered in atoms.
一切现存者皆将迅速改变。若所有物质是一整体，则化为蒸气；否则便散为原子。
5
The governing reason knows its own disposition, what it creates, and what is the material for its creation.
那统御之理性知晓自身之性向，知晓它创生何物，亦知晓其创造所用之材料为何。
6
The best revenge is not to be like your enemy.
最佳之复仇，便是不像你的仇敌那般。
7
Let one thing be your joy and comfort: to move on from social act to social act, with your mind on god.
让一件事成为你的快乐与慰藉：自一社会性行动转向另一社会性行动，心思常念神明。
8
The directing mind is that which wakes itself, adapts itself, makes itself of whatever nature it wishes, and makes all that happens to it appear in the way it wants.
主导心灵乃是自我唤醒者、自我调适者——它令自己成为所愿之任何性质，并使发生在其上之一切皆依其所愿之方式显现。
9
All things have their accomplishment in accordance with the nature of the Whole: it could not be in accordance with any other nature, either enclosing from without or enclosed within, or any external influence.
万物皆依照宇宙整体之本性而成就：这不可能是依照任何其他本然——无论是自外包裹的，或内嵌其中的，抑或任何外在影响。
10
Either a stew, an intricate web, and dispersal into atoms: or unity, order, and providence. Now if the former, why do I even wish to spend my time in a world compounded at random and in like confusion? Why have any concern other than somehow, some time, to 'return to earth'? And why am I troubled? Dispersal will come to me, whatever I do. But if the latter, I am reverent, I am resolute, I put my trust in the governor.
要么是一锅乱炖、一张纷繁之网，以及离散为原子；要么是统一、秩序，与天意。若是前者，我何苦要活在一个偶然混成、秩序同样混乱的世界里？除了在某一刻、以某种方式「复归于尘土」之外，我还有何事可关切？我又为何烦恼？无论我怎样做，离散终究会临到我。然而，若是后者，我心怀虔敬，我坚定不移，我寄信任于那统御者。
11
When circumstances force you to some sort of distress, quickly return to yourself. Do not stay out of rhythm for longer than you must: you will master the harmony the more by constantly going back to it.
当际遇迫使你陷入某种忧苦，迅速回到你自己。勿在律动之外停留过你所必需要之久：恒常回归和律之中，你便会越发掌握其和谐。
12
If you had a step-mother and a mother at the same time, you would pay attention to your step-mother but nevertheless your constant recourse would be to your mother. That is now how it is with the Court and philosophy. So return to philosophy again and again, and find your rest in her: through her the life of the Court seems bearable to you, and you seem bearable in it.
若你同时有继母与生母，你会对继母有所顾应，但你不断返归的仍会是你的生母。宫廷与哲学，现在即是如此。故当时时复归于哲学，在她之中获你之安息：藉由她，宫廷之生活对你才显得可堪忍受，而你在此生活之中，亦显得可堪忍受。
13
How good it is, when you have roast meat or suchlike foods before you, to impress on your mind that this is the dead body of a fish, this the dead body of a bird or pig; and again, that the Falernian wine is the mere juice of grapes, and your purple-edged robe simply sheep's wool dipped in shellfish blood; and in sexual intercourse that it is no more than the friction of a membrane and a spurt of mucus ejected. How good these perceptions are at penetrating to the heart of the thing and laying it bare!
当你面前摆着烤肉之类食物时，能于心中印下此念——此为鱼之尸身，此乃禽鸟或猪之尸身；再者，法勒恩葡萄酒不过是葡萄之汁液，你那镶紫边的长袍不过是羊皮浸于贝血之中；至于交合之事，不过一层薄膜之摩擦与一股黏液之迸射——如此观之，何其善也！这些观照能穿透事物之核心，令其赤裸暴露，何其善也！
14
Most of the things valued by the masses come under the categories of what is sustained by cohesion (minerals, timber) or natural growth (figs, vines, olives). What is valued by the slightly more advanced belongs to the class of things sustained by a conscious life — flocks, herds, slaves. What is valued by the yet more sophisticated is governed by a rational life — not the Reason of the Whole, but the technical or otherwise skilled reason. But the man who honours the soul in full possession of all these other qualities, the soul that is rational and fit for society and the community, has no concern for anything else: above all he keeps his own soul in the full and constant persuasion of reason and social action, and works with his fellow man to the same end.
大众所珍视之物，大多归于靠凝聚而维系之类别（矿石、木材）或自然生长之类别（无花果、藤蔓、橄榄）。稍为进步者所珍视的，则属于靠有意识生命而维系者——羊群、牛群、奴隶。更为精熟者所珍视的，由一种理性生命所统御——非宇宙整体之理性，而是技术或某种其他娴熟技能之理性。然而，那尊崇完满地拥有其他一切品质之灵魂——即那种合乎理性且宜于社会与共同体的灵魂——的人，对任何别物皆漠不关心：首要者，他保持己之灵魂全然且恒常立于理性与社会行动之信念中，并与同侪携手共赴同一目的。
15
Some things are hurrying to come into being, others are hurrying to be gone, and part of that which is being born is already extinguished. Flows and changes are constantly renewing the world, just as the ceaseless passage of time makes eternity ever young. In this river, then, where there can be no foothold, what should any of these things hurtling past mean to us? No more than if we fell in love with a sparrow flitting by: already it has vanished from sight.
有物急急生成，有物急急消逝，而新生者之一部分已然熄灭。流转与变化不断更新世界，正如时间之不止流逝令永恒永葆青春。那么，在此无立足之处的河流中，这些飞掠而过之物对我们又有何意义？不比我们爱上一只倏然飞过之麻雀更真切：转瞬之间，它已消失于眼帘。
17
Up, down, round and round are the motions of the elements, but the movement of active virtue follows none of these: it is something more divine, and it journeys on to success along a path hard to understand.
元素之运动，或上升、或下降、或回环旋转，然而活跃之美德其运动却不循此轨：它乃是更近于神性之物，沿一条难于领悟之路，前行以至成就。
18
What a way to behave! They refuse to speak well of people who live as their contemporaries and in their company, but they set great store by their own good name among future generations which they have never seen nor ever will see. Yet this is brother to grieving that our praises were not sung by past generations.
何等之做派！他们不肯说与己同时同处之人的好话，却恋恋于自己在后代人中——后者他们从未见过，也永不会见——之名声。然而，这与为昔日之人未曾歌咏我们而悲伤，又有何异。
19
Do not imagine that, if something is hard for you to achieve, it is therefore impossible for any man: but rather consider anything that is humanly possible and appropriate to lie within your own reach too.
不要以为，若某事对你来说难于达成，它便对任何人而言皆是不可行的：反之，你应当设想：凡在人而言为可能且恰当之事，亦在你自身所能及之范围内。
20
In the field of play an opponent scratches us with his nails, say, or gives us a butting blow with his head: but we do not 'mark' him for that, or take offence, or suspect him afterwards of deliberate attack. True, we do keep clear of him: but this is not as an enemy or with suspicion — we give him a wide berth in a peaceful way. Let this be the way we view things in the other areas of life: let us overlook much of the behaviour of our opponents in the game. As I said, we can keep clear, but without suspicion or hatred.
赛场上，对手或许以指甲抓伤我们，或以头撞击我们，但我们不因此便盯记他，不动怒，也不事后疑他蓄意攻击。固然，我们确实会避开他：但这并非如待仇敌那般或心存猜疑——我们是以平和之方式保持距离。以此方式来看待我们生活其他领域之事吧：对那些在比赛中与我们对抗者之种种举动，多数都可略过。如我所说，我们可保持距避，但无猜疑、无恨憎。
21
If someone can prove me wrong and show me my mistake in any thought or action, I shall gladly change. I seek the truth, which never harmed anyone: the harm is to persist in one's own self-deception and ignorance.
若有人能证明我错了，指出我在某种思想或行动中之过错，我必欣然改变。我寻求真理，真理从未伤害任何人：伤害在于固执于己之自欺与无知。
22
I do my own duty: the other things do not distract me. They are either inanimate or irrational, or have lost the road and are ignorant of the true way.
我尽我之本分：其他事物不能令我动摇。它们或无生、或无理性，或者已迷失路途，不知真正之道。
23
Since you have reason and they do not, treat dumb animals and generally all things and objects with generosity and decency; treat men, because they do have reason, with social concern; and in all things call on the gods.
你拥有理性而它们没有，故当以宽厚与端正之心对待哑畜、及一般之物与用器；对待人——因他们有理性——则以社会性之关切；并在一切事上呼请诸神。
24
Alexander of Macedon and his muleteer were levelled in death: either they were taken up into the same generative principles of the universe, or they were equally dispersed into atoms.
马其顿的亚历山大与其骡夫，在死亡中归于齐平：他们要么被纳入宇宙同一之创生原理，要么同样散为原子。
25
Reflect on how many separate events, both bodily and mental, are taking place in each one of us in the same tiny fragment of time: and then you will not be surprised if many more events, indeed all that comes to pass, subsist together in the one and entire Whole which we call the universe.
试思在每一人身上，在同一微末时间里，发生着多少彼此分离之事件——无论身体上与心灵上：如此，你便不会惊异，若有远为更多之事件——事实上，所有发生之事——一并存在于那我们称为宇宙之独一而完整之整体中。
26
If someone puts to you the question 'How is the name Antoninus spelt?', will you shout your way through each of the syllables? What then if they get angry? Will you lose your temper too? Will you not rather calmly go through the sequence of letters, enumerating each one? So in life too, remember that every duty is made up of its constituent parts. These you must observe; without discomposure or mutual indignation you must complete in methodical order your appointed task.
若有人问你：「安东尼努斯之名如何拼写？」，你会逐个字母咆哮喊出吗？倘若他们发怒了呢？你也会大发脾气吗？你难道不会冷静地逐一列出字母顺序吗？在生活中也是如此，记住每一职责皆由其诸构成部分所组成。你须关注这些部分；不受扰乱，不激发彼此怒气，你必须按部就班地完成指派于你的任务。
27
How cruel it is not to allow people to strive for what seems to them their interest and advantage! And yet in a way you are forbidding them to do this, when you fuss that they are wrong: they are surely drawn to their own interest and advantage. 'But it is not so.' Then demonstrate that and teach them, without being put out.
不许人去追求那些在他们看来是己之利益与好处的事，何其残忍！然而在某方面，当你对他们的错误耿耿于怀时，你便正是禁止他们这样做：他们确是被自己的利益与好处所吸引。「可情况并非如此。」那么，便证明这一点并教导他们吧，而无需动气。
28
Death is relief from reaction to the senses, from the puppet-strings of impulse, from the analytical mind, and from service to the flesh.
死亡乃是从感官之反应、冲动之提线木偶、分析之心灵，以及对肉身之役事中的解脱。
29
Disgraceful if, in this life where your body does not fail, your soul should fail you first. Take care not to be Caesarified, or dyed in purple: it happens. So keep yourself simple, good, pure, serious, unpretentious, a friend of justice, god-fearing, kindly, affectionate, strong for your proper work.
若在此一生中，你的躯体尚未不济，而你的灵魂却先你而衰败，那真是可耻。留神切勿被凯撒化，或被染成紫色：这样的事情确实发生。故当保持自己素朴、善良、纯净、严肃、不务虚饰、做正义之友、敬神、仁慈、深情、且坚毅于你之本分工作。
31
Sober up, recall yourself, shake off sleep once more: realize they were mere dreams that troubled you, and now that you are awake again look on these things as you would have looked on a dream.
清醒过来，召回你自己，再一次抖落沉眠：明白那些搅扰你的不过是些梦境，而既然你已然再次醒着，便以你在梦中看待它们之方式来看待这些事。
32
I am made of body and soul. Now to the poor body all things are indifferent, as it cannot even make any distinction. To the mind all that is not its own activity is indifferent: and its own activities are all in its control. But within these the mind only concerns itself with the present: its future or past activities are themselves indifferent now.
我由身体与灵魂所构成。对于这可怜的身体，一切皆漠不相干，因它甚至不能作任何区分。对于心灵，凡非其自身活动者，皆漠不相干：而其自身活动，皆在其掌控之中。然而，即便在这些活动之中，心灵也仅专注于当下：它将来或过去的活动，在此刻自身也是漠不相干的。
33
The pain of labour for hand or foot is not contrary to nature, as long as the foot is doing the work of a foot and the hand the work of a hand. So likewise for a man, qua man, there is nothing contrary to nature in pain, as long as he is doing the work of a man. And if not contrary to nature, then not an evil either.
手、足劳作之苦并不违背自然，只要足在做足之工，手在行手之活。于人而言亦是如此，只要他作为一个人在做人之事，痛苦之中便没有任何违背自然之处。若不违背自然，那也就不是恶。
34
As for pleasure, pirates, catamites, parricides, and tyrants have enjoyed it to the full.
至于逸乐，海盗、娈童、弑亲者，以及暴君，皆曾享之至极。
35
Do you not see how the working craftsman, while deferring to the layman up to a point, nevertheless sticks to the principle of his craft and will not bear to desert it? Is it not strange, then, that the architect and the doctor will show greater respect for the principle of their own crafts than man shows for his own reason, which he shares with the gods?
难道你没看见工匠在某种程度上虽对外行有所让步，却仍坚持其技艺之原则而不忍弃之？那么，建筑师和医生对各自技艺之原则所展现的尊重，比人对己身与诸神所共享之理性的尊重更甚，难道不奇怪吗？
37
He who sees the present has seen all things, both all that has come to pass from everlasting and all that will be for eternity: all things are related and the same.
见当下者，即已见一切——既见了自永恒以来所发生之一切，也见了将在永恒中发生之一切：万物皆相连相通且同质。
38
You should meditate often on the connection of all things in the universe and their relationship to each other. In a way all things are interwoven and therefore have a family feeling for each other: one thing follows another in due order through the tension of movement, the common spirit, and the unity of all substance.
当时时沉思宇宙万物之联系及其彼此之关系。在某种程度上，万物皆彼此交织，因而彼此间有一种亲属般之相亲感：一物依适当之秩序，通过运动之张力、共同之精神，以及一切实体之统一而顺次追随另一物。
39
Fit yourself for the matters which have fallen to your lot, and love these people among whom destiny has cast you — but your love must be genuine.
令自己适配于命运所分派于你之事务，并爱命运将你投入其中之人们——但你的爱必须是真诚的。
40
An instrument, a tool, a utensil — all these are fine if they perform the function for which they were made. And yet in such cases the maker is external to the object made. In the case of things held together by organic nature, the power that made them is within, and remains with them. So you should hold these in the greater reverence, reckoning that if you conduct and order your life by this power, then all within you is in accord with intelligence.
一件器械、一柄工具、一个器皿——若它们履行了其被造之功能，便都是好的。然而在此等情形中，制造者外在于被造之物。而在由有机自然所维系凝聚之事物中，那创生之力量乃在其内部，且与之同在。故你当以此更大之敬意来珍视它们，思量你若依此力量来引导并规整你之生命，则你内中一切皆与智性相符。
41
If you set up as good or evil any of the things beyond your control, it necessarily follows that in the occurrence of that evil or the frustration of that good you blame the gods and hate the men who are the real or suspected causes of that occurrence or frustration. And indeed our frequent injustice to each other is caused by our making a distinction between these things. But if we judge only what is in our power to be good or evil, then there is no further reason either to blame god or to take a hostile stance to men.
若你将你掌控之外的任何事物定为好或坏，则必然的后果便是：当那坏事发生、或那好事受挫之时，你便谴责诸神，并仇恨那些造成此事发生或使其受挫的真实或可疑的人。而事实上，吾辈彼此间层出不穷之非义，正是由于我们在此等事物之间妄作区分。若我们仅判断那在我们权能之内者为好或坏，则既无由再责神，亦无由敌对他人。
42
We all work together to the same end, some with conscious attention, others without knowing it — just as Heraclitus, I think, says that even people asleep are workers in the factory of all that happens in the world.
吾辈皆协同工作，朝向同一目标——有些人有觉知地关注，有些人则在不知中为之——正如赫拉克利特所言，我想，即便是沉睡之人，也乃是世间所发生一切之工厂中的工人。
43
Does the sun presume to do the work of the rain-god, or Asclepius that of the goddess of harvest? And what of each of the stars? Is it not that they are different, but work together to the same end?
太阳岂敢去做雨神之工作？阿斯克勒庇俄斯又去做丰收女神之事？而每颗星辰又复如何？它们岂不是彼此不同，然却协同工作朝向同一目的？
45
All that happens to the individual is to the benefit of the Whole. So far, so clear. But if you look more closely you will also see as a general rule that what benefits one person benefits other people too — though here 'benefit' should be taken in its common application to things indifferent.
凡临到个体之事，皆有益于总体。到此为止，尚算清楚。但若你更细细去看，你还会见出一条通则：那有益于一人者，亦有益于他人——虽说此处「有益」一词当依其通常应用于漠不相干之物之意义来解。
46
Just as all the business of the amphitheatre and such places offends you as always one and the same sight, and this monotony of the spectacle bores you, so it is too with your experience of life as a whole: everything, up or down, is the same, with the same causes. For how long, then?
正如斗兽场及类似场所之诸般事务令你生厌——总是一再相同之景象，此等景象之单调令你厌倦——你之整个生命经验亦复如此：一切，无论上下高低，皆相同，皆有相同之因由。那么，又能多久呢？
48
Whenever you want to cheer yourself, think of the qualities of your fellows — the energy of one, for example, the decency of another, the generosity of a third, some other merit in a fourth. There is nothing so cheering as the stamp of virtues manifest in the characters of those around us. So we should keep these ready to hand.
每当你想要鼓舞自己时，想想你同侪之质量——譬如，一人的精力、另一人的端正、第三人的慷慨、第四人的某种其他长处。世间无物比我们周围人品性中所显现之美德印记更能鼓舞人。故我们当将这些随时备在左右。
49
You do not resent your weight, do you — that you weigh only so many pounds and not three hundred? So why resent either a life-span of so many years and not more? Just as you are content with the amount of matter allocated to you, so you should be content with your allocation of time.
你并不怨恨你的体重，对吧——你只有这么些磅而非三百磅？那么，又何必怨恨寿命仅有这么些年而非更长久呢？正如你满足于分配到你的物质量，你也当满足于分配给你的时间量。
50
Try to persuade them, but act even if they are unpersuaded, whenever the principle of justice so directs. But if someone forcibly resists, change tack to an unhurt acceptance, so using the obstacle to bring forth a different virtue. And remember that your impulse was conditional — you were not aiming at the impossible. At what then? Just at making the attempt itself. So you have achieved your object: what our own impulse aimed at is achieved.
尽力去说服他们，但若正义原则如此指引，即便他们未被说服，也当行动。若有人强力抗拒，便转换态度，转为一种不致受创之接纳，以利用障碍引出另一种美德。并记住：你的冲动原是有条件的——你并非志在不可企及之事。那你志在什么？不过志在此番尝试本身。如此，你已实现了你的目标：我们自己冲动之指向已得实现。
51
How to understand your own good: the lover of glory takes it to be the reactions of others; the lover of pleasure takes it to be his own passive experience; the intelligent man sees it as his own action.
如何理解自己之善好：爱荣耀者以他人之反应为善好；爱逸乐者以己之被动经验为善好；而有智之士则以自己之行动为善好。
52
It is possible to have no understanding of this and not to be troubled in mind: things of themselves have no inherent power to form our judgements.
不解此理亦可不致心烦意乱：事物本身并没有形成我们判断之固有力量。
53
Accustom yourself not to be disregarding of what someone else has to say: as far as possible enter into the mind of the speaker.
养成习惯：勿轻忽他人所言之语；尽可能深入言说者之心。
54
What does not benefit the hive does not benefit the bee either.
于蜂巢无益者，于蜂亦无益。
55
If sailors spoke ill of their captain or patients of their doctor, who else would they listen to? Otherwise how would the captain achieve a safe voyage for his passengers or the doctor health for those in his care?
若水手说其船长之歹话，或病患说其医生之歹话，他们还能听从谁呢？若非如此，船长何以令乘客安全航行，医生何以令人康复于其看护之中？
56
How many with whom I came into the world have already left!
多少与我一同来到此世之人，已然离去！
57
Appearances: to the jaundiced honey seems bitter, to those bitten by rabid dogs water is a terror, to little boys a ball is joy. Why then am I angry? Or do you think that false representation has less effect than bile in the jaundiced or poison in the rabid?
表象之效力：于患黄疸者，蜜似为苦；于被疯狗咬伤者，水可恐可怖；于小男孩，一颗球便是欢欣。那么，我为何动怒？莫非你以为虚假之呈现，其效力比黄疸病人之胆汁或狂犬病人之毒来得更弱？
58
No one will prevent you living in accordance with the principle of your own nature: nothing will happen to you contrary to the principle of universal nature.
无人能阻挡你依照你自身本性之原则而生活；没有事情会违背宇宙本性之原则而临到你身。
59
What sort of people they wish to please! And what kind of actions are the means of their success! How quickly time will cover everything — and how much is covered already.
他们想要取悦的是何等样人！而他们用以成功之手段又是何等样之行动！时间将何其迅速地淹没一切——又有多少早已被淹没。

沉思录 卷七

1
This is wickedness: this is what you have often seen. And you should have this thought ready to hand against any eventuality: 'I have seen this often before.' Generally wherever you look you will find the same things. The histories — ancient, more recent, modern — are full of them: whole cities and households are full of them today. There is nothing new: all is familiar and quickly over.
此即邪恶：此乃你所常见之事。你当常备此念以应万变：「此事我以前已屡见不鲜。」概而言之，无论你往何处看，所见者皆同。史乘之中——远者、近者、今者——充斥此类事：今日之全城全家亦充塞此类事。世间原无新事：一切皆已成惯见，且转眼即逝。
2
Your principles are living things. How else could they be deadened, except by the extinction of the corresponding mental images? And the constant rekindling of these is up to you. 'I am able to form the judgement I should about this event. If able, why should I be troubled? Things outside my understanding have no relation to my understanding.' Learn this, and you are upright. Recovery of life is possible. Look at things again as you used to look at them. Recovery of life consists in this.
你之原则乃是活物。它们怎能被杀死，除了那相应之心象被扑灭？而不断重新点燃这些心象，全在你。于此事件我能形成所当形之判断。若能为，我何须烦恼？我之理解外之事物，与我之理解毫无关涉。学此一事，便是挺直了。恢复生命是可能的。再来一次，以你从前看待事物之眼光看待它们。恢复生命即在于此。
3
The empty pomp of a procession, plays on the stage, flocks and herds, jousting shows, a bone thrown to puppies, tit-bits into the fishponds, ants toiling and carrying, the scurries of frightened mice, puppets dancing on their strings. Well, amid all this you must keep your good humour and not be patronizing. But realize that a man's worth is no greater than the worth of his ambitions.
游行队列之空洞盛况、舞台之戏目、畜群牲队、枪矛竞技之观演、掷给幼犬之骨、投进鱼池之精料、蚁群劳碌与搬运、惊鼠之窜逃、傀儡牵线之舞。身处此诸般之中，你当保你之好兴致而不居高临下。然亦须明白：一人之价值，不逾其抱负之价值。
4
In conversation one ought to follow closely what is said, in any impulse to follow closely what takes place. In the latter case, to see immediately the intended object of reference: in the former, to watch carefully what is meant.
谈话之时，当紧随所言之内容；任何冲动中，当紧随所发生之事。在后者中，当即看到所指向之目标；在前者中，当细察所意谓者为何。
5
Is my mind sufficient for this task, or is it not? If it is, I use it for the task as an instrument given me by the nature of the Whole. If it is not, I either cede the work (if it is otherwise my responsibility) to someone better able to accomplish it, or, if that is not proper, I do it as best I can, calling on the help of one who can use my directing mind to effect what is timely and beneficial for the community.
我心智足应付此事否？若足，则我将其用作宇宙整体之本性所赐我之工具来完成此任务。若不足，我则将此工作（若其在其他意义上乃我之职责）让与更能胜任之人，或若此举不妥，便尽我之所能为之，同时吁请那人来援助——他能运用我的主导之心智，为共同体成就那适时而有益之事。
6
How many who once rose to fame are now consigned to oblivion: and how many who sang their fame are long disappeared.
多少曾煊赫一时之人，如今已交付遗忘：多少曾咏唱其名者，亦早已杳然无踪。
7
Do not be ashamed of help. It is your task to achieve your assigned duty, like a soldier in a scaling-party. What, then, if you are lame and cannot climb the parapet by yourself, but this is made possible by another's help?
勿以接受帮助为羞。你之任务乃完成指派于你之职责，犹如一攻城之士兵。那么，若你瘸腿而不能独自攀登墙垣，而藉他人之助却可达成，又有何妨？
8
Do not let the future trouble you. You will come to it (if that is what you must) possessed of the same reason that you apply now to the present.
毋让未来困扰于你。你走向它时（若那是你必行之途），将带着与你此时运用于当下之相同的理性。
9
All things are meshed together, and a sacred bond unites them. Hardly a single thing is alien to the rest: ordered together in their places they together make up the one order of the universe. There is one universe out of all things, one god pervading all things, one substance, one law, one reason common to all intelligent creatures, and one truth.
万物彼此紧扣相织，一神圣之纽带联结着它们。几乎没有一物与他者相异陌：各居其位，有序汇聚，它们一同构成宇宙之单一秩序。由万物而成一宇宙，一神普彻透贯万物，一实体，一法律，一切智性生灵所共有之一理性，以及一真理。
10
Everything material rapidly disappears in the universal substance; every cause is rapidly taken up into the universal reason; and the memory of everything is rapidly buried in eternity.
一切物质者皆迅速地消逝于宇宙实体之中；一切原因皆迅速地被摄入那宇宙理性之中；一切事物之记忆皆迅速地被埋没于永恒之中。
11
For a rational being, to act in accordance with nature is also to act in accordance with reason.
于理性存在者而言，依照自然而行动，即是依照理性而行动。
12
Standing straight — or held straight.
自行直立——或被扶直。
13
Rational beings collectively have the same relation as the various limbs of an organic unity — they were created for a single cooperative purpose. The notion of this will strike you more forcefully if you keep on saying to yourself: 'I am a limb of the composite body of rational beings.'
众理性存在者集合在一起，其彼此间之关系，犹如一有机整体之诸般肢体——彼等乃为一个单一的协作目的而被造。如果你不断对己说「我是那由理性存在者所组成之复合躯体的一肢体」，则此观念将更加有力地冲击于你。
14
Let any external thing that so wishes happen to those parts of me which can be affected by its happening — and they, if they wish, can complain. I myself am not yet harmed, unless I judge this occurrence something bad: and I can refuse to do so.
凡外在事物所愿加之事，便加诸我身上那些能被其发生所影响之部分吧——彼等部分，若情愿，自可抱怨。我自己并未受伤害，除非我将此事判断为坏事：而我能够拒不作此判断。
15
Whatever anyone does or says, I must be a good man. It is as if an emerald, or gold or purple, were always saying: 'Whatever anyone does or says, I must be an emerald and keep my own colour.'
无论何人做何事、言何语，我必须是一个好人。就如同一块绿宝石、或黄金、或紫袍，恒常说：「无论何人做何事、言何语，我必须是一块绿宝石，保持我自身之色泽。」
16
The directing mind does not disturb itself: for example, it does not frighten itself or lead itself to desire. If anyone else can frighten it or cause it pain, let him do so: of itself, of its own judgement, it will not deliberately turn to such modes.
主导之心智不会自我搅扰：譬如其不自吓，亦不引己入欲望之途。若任何别人能吓它或使之痛苦，那人便去做吧：就其自身、凭其自己之判断，它不会蓄意转向此种情境。
17
Happiness is a benign god or divine blessing. Why then, my imagination, are you doing what you do? Go away, in the gods' name, the way you came: I have no need of you. You have come in your old habit. I am not angry with you. Only go away.
幸福乃是仁慈之神明或神灵之赐福。那么，我的幻念，你为何做你所做之事？以诸神之名，从你来的路上离去吧：我不需要你。你按你之旧习惯而来。我不对你生气。只是离去吧。
18
Is someone afraid of change? Well, what can ever come to be without change? Or what is dearer or closer to the nature of the Whole than change? Can you yourself take your bath, if the wood that heats it is not changed? Can you be fed, unless what you eat is changed? Can any other useful thing be achieved without change? Do you not see then that for yourself a change similar to theirs is both necessary and necessary to the nature of the Whole?
难道有人害怕变化？然而，若无变化，何物能生成？又有什么比变化更亲于、更近于宇宙整体之本性？你自己能沐浴，若加热水之木柴未经变化？你能进食，除非你吃掉之物被消化变化？任何其他有用之物，若无变化，能得以成就？你难道看不见，于你自己，与万物类似之变化，既是必须的，亦为宇宙整体本性所必须？
19
All our bodies pass through the universal substance as through a swirling stream. How many a Chrysippus, a Socrates, an Epictetus has eternity already swallowed! The same thought will apply to every man and thing.
吾辈一切躯体，皆穿过宇宙实体如穿过一涡旋之流。多少克律西波斯、苏格拉底、爱比克泰德，永恒早已吞没！此一念，适用于每一人和每一物。
20
I have only one anxiety: that I myself should not do something which the human constitution does not intend — or does not intend in this way or at this time.
我只有唯一之忧：我自己不要做任何不属于人类构造所意愿之事——或非以人类构造之意愿方式、在此时间所做之事。
21
Soon you will have forgotten all things: soon all things will have forgotten you.
须臾之间，你便将遗忘一切：须臾之间，一切便将遗忘你。
22
It is human nature to love even those who trip and fall. This follows if you reflect at the time that all men are brothers; that they go wrong through ignorance, not intent; that in a short while both you and they will be dead; and, above all, that the man has done you no harm — he has not made your directing mind worse than it was before.
怜爱那些跌倒犯错之人，亦是人类之本性。若你彼时思及：众人皆为兄弟；他们因无知而非蓄意才犯错；不消多时，你与他们皆将死去；而尤重要者，那人并未伤害到你——他并未使你的主导心灵变得比以前更坏。若这般思想，你便会如此。
23
Universal nature uses the substance of the universe like wax, making now the model of a horse, then melting it down and using its material for a tree; next for a man; next for something else. Each one of these subsists for only the briefest time. It is no more a hardship for the chest to be broken up than it was to be put together.
宇宙之本性运用宇宙之实体如同蜡脂，当下塑成一匹马之模型，随即融而化之，用其材料再造成一株树；接着塑造一人；接着又塑造他物。其中每一者之存续皆仅最短之时段。箱匣之被拆散，不比它被拼合时更为艰难。
24
A deep scowl on the face is contrary to nature, and when it becomes habitual expressiveness begins to die or is even finally extinguished beyond rekindling. Try to attend to this very point, that this is something against reason. In the field of moral offence, if the very consciousness of doing wrong is gone, what reason is left for living?
面上深蹙之眉乃违背自然，当其成为习惯，面部之表意能力便开始枯死，甚至最终熄灭至于无可复燃。你当努力关注此一关键点——此乃违背理性之事。在道德过失之领域，若连犯错之意识都已丧失，那活着还有什么理由？
25
All that you see will in a moment be changed by the nature which governs the Whole: it will create other things out of this material, and then again others out of that, so that the world is always young.
你所见之一切，顷刻间便将由统御宇宙整体之本性所改变：它将由此一材料创造出另外之物，然后又由那物再造出另外之物，如此，则世界永葆青春。
26
When someone does you some wrong, you should consider immediately what judgement of good or evil led him to wrong you. When you see this, you will pity him, and not feel surprise or anger. You yourself either still share his view of good, or something like it: you should then forgive him. But if you now judge differently, it will be easy for you to be gracious to one so mistaken.
当有人对你行了不义之事，你当立即思量：是什么样之善恶判断导致了他来伤害你。一旦认清此事，你便将怜悯他，而不是感到意外或愤怒。你自己要么仍与他持相同的善之观念，或类似之观念：那你便该原谅他。但若你如今已作不同之判断，你便不难对如此出错之人施以宽仁。
27
Do not dream of possession of what you do not have: rather reflect on the greatest blessings in what you do have, and on their account remind yourself how much they would have been missed if they were not there. But at the same time you must be careful not to let this sense of contentment lead you into overvaluing them, so that you would be distressed if you ever lost them.
勿梦寐以求你所不拥有之物：而当思量你现有之物中至大之福祉，并因此念及它们倘若不在、你将会何等若有所失。但与此同时，你亦当小心，勿令此一满足之感引你至过分珍视它们之地步，以致一旦失去它们你便悲苦不宁。
28
Withdraw into yourself. It is in the nature of the rational directing mind to be self-content with acting rightly and the calm it thereby enjoys.
退归于己。理性之主导心灵其本性即是：于端正行事及其所随之享有之安宁中，自我安足。
29
Erase the print of imagination. Stop the puppet-strings of impulse. Define the present moment of time. Recognize what happens to you or to another. Analyse and divide the event into the causal and the material. Think of your final hour. Leave the wrong done by that man where the wrong arose.
抹去想象之印痕。止住冲动之提线木偶。界定时间之当下此刻。辨识发生在你或他人身上的事。将该事件分析并划分为原因与物质二者。思及你的最后一刻。将那人所行之错，留在那错生起的地方。
30
Stretch your thought to parallel what is being said. Let your mind get inside what is happening and who is doing it.
延伸你之思想，与所言之语平行并进。令你的心灵进入正在发生之事，以及那正在行事之人之中。
31
Take your joy in simplicity, in integrity, in indifference to all that lies between virtue and vice. Love mankind. Follow god. Democritus says, 'All else is subject to the law of convention: only the elements are absolute and real' — but enough for you to remember that all things are as nothing.
在素朴中取悦，在正直中取悦，在对德行与恶行之间一切事物之不执不染中取悦。爱人类。追随神明。德谟克利特说：「其他一切皆服从于人为约定之律法：唯元素是真实而绝对者」——然于你而言，记住一切皆若虚无，便已足够。
32
On death. Either dispersal, if we are atoms: or, if we are a unity, extinction or a change of home.
论死亡。或为离散，若我们仅是原子；或若我辈是一统一体，则为灭绝或迁居。
33
On pain. Unbearable pain carries us off: chronic pain can be borne. The mind preserves its own serenity by withdrawal, and the directing reason is not impaired by pain. It is for the parts injured by the pain to protest if they can.
论痛苦。不堪忍受之苦会夺走我们的生命；慢性之痛则可以承受。心灵藉由退转而保持其自在之宁静，主导理性并不为痛苦所损。因痛苦而受伤之部分，若其能够，自可抗议。
34
On fame. Look at their minds, the nature of their thought and what they seek or avoid. And see how, just as drifting sands constantly overlay the previous sand, so in our lives what we once did is very quickly covered over by subsequent layers.
论名声。审视那些人之心，其思之性质，以及其所寻索或回避者。且看，正如流沙不断覆没先前之沙，吾辈生命中昔日所为亦很快被其后之层累所掩覆。
35
So, to a man endowed with noble intelligence and a vision of all time and all being, do you think that this human life will seem of great importance? 'Impossible,' he said. So such a man will not think there is anything fearful in death either? 'Certainly not.'
那么，于一个拥有高贵智识、堪能观照一切时间与一切存有者之人，你以为此一人之生命会是桩大不了的事吗？「绝不可能，」他答。那么这样一个人也不会认为死有什么可畏了？「当然不会。」
36
A king's lot: to do good and be damned.
为君者之命运：行善于世，而横遭毁谤。
37
It is shameful that the face should be so obedient, shaping and ordering its expression as the mind dictates, when the mind cannot impose its own shape and order on itself.
面容如此顺从——依心灵之指示而塑造并规整其表情——而心灵竟不能将自身之形态与秩序施加于己，这真是可耻。
40
'Ripe ears of corn are reaped, and so are lives: One stands, another falls.'
「成熟之谷穗被收割，生命亦复如此：一株挺立，另一株倒下。」
41
'If I and my two sons are now no more The gods' concern, this too will have its cause.'
「若我与我的两个儿子如今已不再为诸神所眷顾，此事亦必有其因由。」
42
'For good and right stand on my side.'
「因善与正道立于我之一侧。」
44
But I could give this man a proper answer. I would say: 'You are mistaken, my friend, if you think that a man of any worth at all should take into account the risk of life or death, and not have as his sole consideration in any action whether he is acting justly or unjustly, the part of a good man or a bad man.'
但我能给这人一恰当之答复。我会说：「我的朋友，你错了，若你认为一个有半丝价值之人当计及生命与死亡之风险，而不是在每一行动中唯独关心他是义还是不义地行动，是善人之行还是恶人之行。」
45
The truth of the matter, my fellow Athenians, is this. Whatever position a man has taken up in his own best judgement, or is assigned by his commander, there, it seems to me, he should stay and face the danger, giving no thought to death or anything else before dishonour.
事实之真相，吾雅典同胞，乃在于是。一个人无论以其自身最佳判断而选定之位置，抑或为其指挥官所分派之哨位，在我看来，他就应当守在那里并面对危险，丝毫不计及死亡或任何其他事——除了耻辱之外。
46
But, my dear fellow, consider it possible that nobility and virtue are something other than saving one's life or having it saved. Could it not be that anyone who is truly a man should dismiss any concern for a particular length of life, and not simply cling to life?
然而，我亲爱的朋友，不妨考虑：高贵与德性或许不是保住性命或任人拯救之事。难道，一个真正之人才应当释去对特定寿数长短之任何执念，并且不单纯地贪执生命，不是这样的吗？
47
Observe the movement of the stars as if you were running their courses with them, and let your mind constantly dwell on the changes of the elements into each other. Such imaginings wash away the filth of life on the ground.
观想星辰之运行，仿佛你与之同行其轨道一般，并使你之心智时时驻于诸元素彼此互变之中。如此之想象可洗尽地上生命之尘垢。
48
Further, when your talk is about mankind, view earthly things as if looking down on them from some point high above — flocks, armies, farms, weddings, divorces, births, deaths, the hubbub of the law-courts, desert places, various foreign nations, festivals, funerals, markets: all the medley of the world and the harmonious order of opposites.
此外，当你谈及人类时，不妨观世间万物如自高处某一地点俯瞰而下——羊群、军队、田庄、婚娶、离异、诞生、死亡、讼庭之喧哗、荒漠之所、诸般异族、节庆、葬仪、集市：世间一切混杂之万象，以及对立者彼此和谐之秩序。
49
Look back over the past — all those many changes of dynasties. And you can foresee the future too: it will be completely alike, incapable of deviating from the rhythm of the present. So for the study of human life forty years are as good as ten thousand years: what more will you see?
回望过去——那一切朝代更迭之层出不穷。你也能预见未来：它将完全一致，无法偏离当前之韵律。故于人类生命之研习，四十年或一万年并无分别：你还能看见更多什么吗？
52
Better at throwing his man: but not more public-spirited, or more decent, or more disciplined to circumstance, or more tolerant of neighbours' faults.
更善于摔倒对手：却并未更热心公益，亦未更端正，亦未更善于随境自约，亦未对邻人之过更加容耐。
53
Where a task can be accomplished in accordance with the reason which gods and men share, there is nothing to be afraid of: because where there is the possibility of benefit from an action which moves along the proper path, following our own human constitution, there is no place for fear of any harm.
若一件事可依照神人所共有的理性来完成，那便无所可畏：因为凡有可能从一沿着正途、遵循吾辈人类自身构造之行动中获益之处，便毫无恐惧任何伤害之余地。
54
Everywhere and all the time it is up to you to honour god in contentment with your present circumstance, to treat the men who are your present company with justice, and to lavish thought on every present impression in your mind, so that nothing slips into your mind without proper analysis.
随时随地，你都可以于满足现前之境中虔敬神明，以正义对待你当下所伴之人，并以细致之思倾注于你心中每一当下之印象，令无一物未经恰当之分析便滑入你的心中。
55
Do not look around at the directing minds of other people, but keep looking straight ahead to where nature is leading you — both universal nature, in what happens to you, and your own nature, in what you must do yourself. Every creature must do what follows from its own constitution.
莫左右环视他人之主导心灵，而径直注视自然正引领你前往之处——既看那宇宙之自然，于临到你身之事中；也看那你自己之自然，在你自身须行之事中。每一造物皆须做从其自身构造推演而出之事。
56
Imagine you were now dead, or had not lived before this moment. Now view the rest of your life as a bonus, and live it as nature directs.
想象你此刻已死，或在此刻之前尚未活过。如今将你余生视为一笔额外之赏，并依自然所指引而度之。
57
Love only what falls your way and is fated for you. What could suit you more than that?
只爱那些落入您途、且为您命定之物事。世间何物能比这更适宜于你？
58
In every contingency keep in your mind's eye those who had the same experience before, and reacted with vexation, disbelief, or complaint. So where are they now? Nowhere. Well then, do you want to act like them? Why not leave the moods and shifts of others to those who are shifted by them, and concentrate on how to use these contingencies yourself?
每一变故中，留心回想那些曾有过同样经历、并以烦恼、不信或怨诉回应的人。那么他们如今在哪里？无处可觅。那你还要像他们那样行事吗？为何不把别人之情绪与摇摆留给那些被它们所摇摆之人，而专注于你自己如何运用此等变故？
59
Dig inside yourself. Inside there is a spring of goodness ready to gush at any moment, if you keep digging.
向你自己内里开掘。你内里有一善之泉源，时时准备迸涌喷发，只要你持续挖掘。
60
The body, too, should stay firmly composed, and not fling itself about either in motion or at rest. Just as the mind displays qualities in the face, keeping it intelligent and attractive, something similar should be required of the whole body. But all this should be kept in place without affectation.
身体也当保持稳固之镇宁，不于运动中或静止中甩掷动荡。正如心灵在面容上展露其品质，使之智慧而悦目，于整个躯体亦应有一类似之要求。然这一切都应保持着而无做作之态。
61
The art of living is more like wrestling than dancing, in that it stands ready for what comes and is not thrown by the unforeseen.
生活之术，更似摔跤而非舞蹈，在于它随时备对即将到来之事，不为意外所颠扑。
62
All the time you should consider who are these people whose endorsement you wish, and what are the minds that direct them. When you look into the sources of their judgement and impulse, you will not blame their unwitting error, nor will you feel the need of their endorsement.
时时刻刻，你当思忖：你所愿得其首肯之人，究是何等人，以及那引导他们之心灵究竟是什么。当你探看其判断与冲动之源头时，你便既不责其无意之过失，亦不觉需要其首肯。
63
'No soul', says Plato, 'likes to be robbed of truth' — and the same holds of justice, moderation, kindness, and all such virtues. Essential that you should keep this constantly in your mind: this will make you more gentle to all.
柏拉图说：「无一灵魂甘愿被剥夺真理」——此理于正义、节制、仁善，及所有此类美德，亦复如是。至关紧要的是你当时时铭记此念于心：这将使你对所有人皆更加温良。
64
Whenever you suffer pain, have ready to hand the thought that pain is not a moral evil and does not harm your governing intelligence: pain can do no damage either to its rational or to its social nature. In most cases of pain you should be helped too by the saying of Epicurus: 'Pain is never unbearable or unending, as long as you remember its limits and do not add to it in your imagination.'
每当你遭遇痛楚，当备此念在手边：痛苦并非道德之恶，亦不损害你那统领之智识；痛苦绝难伤及它之理性或其社会本性。在多数痛苦之情形中，伊壁鸩鲁之语也当有助于你：「痛苦从非不堪忍受或永无竟时，只要你记住其界限，而不以你之想象增添于其上。」
65
Take care that you never treat the misanthropic as they treat mankind.
注意己行，永勿以厌世者对待人类之方式来待人。
66
How do we know that Telauges' character did not make him a better man than Socrates? It is not enough that Socrates died a more glorious death, that he argued more skilfully with the sophists, that he showed greater endurance in spending a whole night in the frost. The question is: what sort of soul did Socrates have? Could he be content with showing justice to men and piety to gods?
我们何以知道特拉乌格斯之品格并未令他成为比苏格拉底更好的人？苏格拉底死得更为荣耀，与智者们辩驳得更为精妙，能在霜冻中度过一整夜而展示出更大之坚忍——但仅此尚不足够。问题在于：苏格拉底拥有何等之灵魂？他是否能以施正义于人、虔敬于神而心满意足？
67
The way nature has blended you into the compound whole does not prevent you drawing a boundary around yourself and keeping what is your own in your own control. Always remember this: remember too that the happy life depends on very little. And do not think that just because you have given up hope of being a scholar or a scientist, you should therefore despair of being free, sure of yourself, a social being, obedient to god.
自然将你混合入那复合整体之中的方式，并不妨碍你在己身周围划出界线，并将属于你自身之物守持在你自身之掌控下。当时时铭记此事：亦铭记幸福之生活仅依赖于极少之物。且勿以为，只因你放弃了成为学者或科学家之希望，你便也因此当对自由、自信、做一个社会性存在者、顺从于神而感到绝望。
68
Live through your life without pressure and in the utmost contentment, even if all are clamouring what they will against you, even if wild beasts are tearing off the limbs of this poor lump of a body accreted round you. What in all this prevents the mind from keeping itself in a place of calm, with a true judgement on circumstance and ready use of all events submitted to it?
以无有压力、并极致满足之心，度过你之生命——即便众人在喧哗呐喊他们所意欲的、一切与你相悖之言，即便野兽正在撕扯这嵌在你周围之可怜肉块之四肢。在此一切之中，有什么能阻止那心灵将自身保持在平静之所，以对处境之真实判断和对一切提交于它之事变的善加利用？
69
Perfection of character is this: to live each day as if it were your last, without frenzy, without apathy, without pretence.
品格之完美即在于此：将每一日当作最后一日光景般去过——无有狂躁，无有麻木，无有虚伪自欺。
70
The gods, who are free from death, do not resent their need throughout all the length of eternity to tolerate in such numbers such worthless creatures as men: what is more, they even care for them in all sorts of ways. And do you, with the merest time before your own departure, refuse to bear with these worthless creatures — and one of them is you?
诸神，脱离了死亡之存在，亦不因在永恒之全部长度中须容耐如此众多、如此微不足道之造物如人类者而懊恼：更有甚者，他们还以诸般方式顾念他们。而你，在你自身离去之前仅剩这弹指之时光，却拒绝忍受这些微不足道之造物——况且，你自己亦是其中之一？
71
It is ridiculous not to escape from one's own vices, which is possible, while trying to escape the vices of others, which is impossible.
不逃避自己之恶习——这是可能的——而竭力逃避他人之恶习——这却是不可能的：这是荒唐可笑之事。
72
Whatever the rational and social faculty finds neither intelligent nor to the common good it judges, with good reason, beneath itself.
凡理性兼社会性之官能所发现为既非智性、亦不关乎公共福祉之物，它便以理据此判其为在其自身之下。
73
When you have done good and another has benefited, why do you still look, as fools do, for a third thing besides — credit for good works, or a return?
当你行事为善、他人因之获益之后，为何你仍像愚人一般，再寻找第三样东西——为善行之赞誉，或回报？
74
No one tires of receiving benefit: and action in accordance with nature is your own benefit. Do not then tire of benefit gained by benefit given.
无人会对受取好处感到厌倦：而依照自然之行动则是你自身之益处。那便不要厌倦于通过施与好处而获得之益处。
75
The nature of the Whole set itself to create a universe. So now either everything that comes into being springs from that as logical consequence, or else even the primary aims to which the directing mind of the universe sets its own impulse are irrational. Remembrance of this will bring you to a calmer mind in many things.
宇宙整体之本性曾致力于创造一个宇宙。故如今，要么一切生成之物皆从彼作为逻辑之推论而生，要么即便是宇宙主导之心智为其自身冲动所设定的那些首要目的也是非理性的。记住这一点，将使你在许多事情上获得更平静之心智。

沉思录 卷八

1
This too is a counter to pretension, that you have lost now the chance to live your whole life, or at least your adult life, as a philosopher: indeed it has become clear to many, yourself included, that you are far from philosophy. You are tarnished, then: difficult for you now to win the reputation of a philosopher, and your own station in life is an opposing factor. But if you have truly seen where the truth lies, then drop all other concerns and simply ensure that the rest of your life, whatever length the gods grant, is lived as your nature demands.
此亦是抗拒虚矫之一法——你如今已失去以哲人身份度过整个一生、或至少成年之一生的机会：事实上，在许多人和你自己看来，你离哲学已远。你已被玷染：如今要赢得哲人之名对你而言是困难的，而你自身在世间的处境亦是一种阻碍。然而，若你已真正看清真理之所在，那便放下其他一切顾虑，只须确保余下之人生——无论诸神所赐予者何其短长——皆依照你之本性所需而度过。
2
Ask yourself this about each action: 'How does this sit with me? Shall I regret it?' In a short while I am dead and all things are gone. What more do I want, if this present work is that of an intelligent and social being, sharing one law with god?
对每一行动，以此自问：「此事合我心意否？我会追悔否？」须臾之间我便将死去，一切皆逝。若此当下之作为，乃是一智性而社会性之存在者，与神共享同一法则——那我更有何求？
3
Alexander, Julius Caesar, Pompey — what are they to Diogenes, Heraclitus, Socrates? These men saw into reality, its causes and its material, and their directing minds were their own masters. As for the former, they were slaves to all their ambitions.
亚历山大、尤利乌斯·凯撒、庞培——比起第欧根尼、赫拉克利特、苏格拉底，这些算得什么？后者洞见实在、其原因与物质，其主导之心灵乃自我主宰。至于前者，他们不过是己之诸般野心的奴隶。
4
Even if you burst with indignation, they will still carry on regardless.
纵使你气得炸开，他们照样我行我素。
5
First, do not be upset: all things follow the nature of the Whole, and in a little while you will be no one and nowhere, as is true now even of Hadrian and Augustus. Next, concentrate on the matter in hand and see it for what it is. Remind yourself of your duty to be a good man and rehearse what man's nature demands: then do it straight and unswerving, or speak as you think best, as long as you do it without affectation.
首先，不要烦乱：万物皆遵循宇宙整体之本性，而稍过片刻，你便将寂寂无名、无处可觅——正如哈德良与奥古斯都如今亦是如此。其次，专注于手头之事，看清其本来面目。提醒自己要做一个好人，并温习人之本性所要求者：然后径直去做，不偏不倚，或如你所思为佳者去言说——只要你在做时毫无造作之态。
6
The work of universal nature is to translate this reality to another, to change things, to take them from here and carry them there. All things are mutations, but there is equality too in their distribution. All is familiar: no cause then for fear of anything new.
宇宙本性之工作，即是将此一实在转化为另一实在，改变事物，将物从此处取而运至彼处。一切皆是在变易之中，然而变化之分派亦是平等的。一切皆是熟悉之旧事：故无由惧怕任何新物。
7
Every living organism is fulfilled when it follows the right path for its own nature. For a rational nature the right path is to withhold assent to anything false or obscure in the impressions made on its mind, to direct its impulses solely to social action, to reserve its desires and aversions to what is in our power, and to welcome all that is assigned by universal nature.
每一活之有机体，当其遵循适宜其本性之正路时，便得了成全。对于理性本性，正路乃是：不赞同其心灵所接获印象中任何虚假或隐晦之物；将冲动纯粹导向社会性之行动；将其欲望与厌恶限定于吾辈权能之内；并接纳宇宙本性所分配之一切。
8
Not possible to study. But possible to rein in arrogance; possible to triumph over pleasures and pains; possible to rise above mere glory; possible not to be angry with the unfeeling and the ungrateful, and even, yes, to care for them.
未必能够研读。但可以克制傲慢；可以战胜逸乐与痛苦；可以超乎虚浮之荣名；可以不恼怒于那些麻木与忘恩负义之人——甚至，是的，仍去关心他们。
9
Let nobody any more hear you blaming palace life: don't hear yourself blaming it.
别让任何人再听见你抱怨宫廷生活：也别让自己听见你抱怨它。
10
Regret is a censure of yourself for missing something beneficial. The good must be something beneficial, and of concern to the wholly good person. No wholly good person would regret missing a pleasure. Therefore pleasure is neither beneficial nor a good.
后悔乃是因错失了某有益之物而对己之责备。善者必为有益之物，且为全然之善人所关切者。无一全然之善人会后悔错过一桩逸乐。故逸乐既非有益，亦非善好。
11
What is this thing in itself, in its own constitution? What are its elements of substance and material, and of cause? What is its function in the world? What is its duration?
此物在其自身构造中究是什么？其实体与物质，以及其原因的元素是什么？它在世间之功能为何？其存续之时间又几何？
12
When you are reluctant to get up from your sleep, remind yourself that it is your constitution and man's nature to perform social acts, whereas sleep is something you share with dumb animals. Now what accords with the nature of each being is thereby the more closely related to it, the more in its essence, and indeed the more agreeable.
当你不愿从睡乡起身时，提醒自己：履行社会性之行动是你之构造与人之本性，而睡眠则是你与哑畜所共有的。凡契合于每类存在者之本性者，便因此更为亲于该类存在者，更在其本质中，且也更令人满足愉快。
13
Constantly test your mental impressions — each one individually, if you can: investigate the cause, identify the emotion, apply the analysis of logic.
恒常检验你心灵之印象——若能，便逐一而试：探究其原因，识别其所涉之情，运用逻辑之分析。
14
Whenever you meet someone, ask yourself first this immediate question: 'What beliefs does this person hold about the good and bad in life?' Because if he believes this or that about pleasure and pain and their constituents, about fame and obscurity, death and life, then I shall not find it surprising or strange if he acts in this or that way: and I shall remember that he is forced to act so.
每遇见一人，先以此当下之问自问：「此人于人生中之善与恶持有何种信念？」因为若他对逸乐与痛苦及其诸组成、对名声与默默无闻、对生与死抱持这般这般之信念，那么他这般这般行事，我便不以为异、不以为怪了：并且我当记得，他乃是不得不如此行事。
15
It would be absurd to be surprised at a fig-tree bearing figs. Remember that there is as little cause for surprise if the world brings forth fruits such as these when the crop is there. Equally absurd for a doctor or ship's captain to be surprised at fever in a patient or a head-wind springing up.
无花果树结无花果，若因此惊异，便是荒谬。当记得：若世界在庄稼成熟时节产生如此之果实，亦同样不足为惊。医生若对病人发烧感到意外，或船长对逆风骤起感到吃惊，也同样的荒谬。
16
Remember that to change course or accept correction leaves you just as free as you were. The action is your own, driven by your own impulse and judgement, indeed your own intelligence.
切记：更改方向，或接受纠正，并不减少你之自由。行动仍是你自己的，为你自己之冲动与判断所驱使——甚至，就是你自身之智识。
17
If the choice is yours, why do the thing? But if it is another's choice, what do you blame — atoms or gods? Either is madness. There is no blame. If you can, put him right: if you can't, at the least put the matter itself right. If that too is impossible, what further purpose does blame serve? Nothing should be done without purpose.
若选择在你，为何要做那事？但若选择在别人，你责怪谁——原子，还是诸神？两者皆疯狂。其实并无责怪。若你能够，纠正他：若你不能，至少把事本身纠正。若这也行不通，责怪又有何意义？无事当无目的地施行。
18
What dies does not pass out of the universe. If it remains here and is changed, then here too it is resolved into the everlasting constituents, which are the elements of the universe and of you yourself. These too change, and make no complaint of it.
死亡之物并不逸出宇宙。若它留在此间而变化，则也在此间消解为永恒之构成元素——这些元素既属宇宙，亦属你自己。此等元素亦变化，并不抱怨。
19
Everything has come into being for a purpose — a horse, say, a vine. Does this surprise you? Even the sun will say, 'I came into being for a purpose': likewise the other gods. For what purpose, then, were you created? For your pleasure? Just see whether this idea can be entertained.
一切皆具一目的而生成——譬如一匹马、一株藤蔓。这让你惊讶吗？连太阳也会说：「我因一目的而存在」：其余诸神亦然。那么，你又是因何目的而被造？为了你的逸乐？且看此念能否被认真对待。
20
Nature's aim for everything includes its cessation just as much as its beginning and its duration — like someone throwing up a ball. How can it be good for the ball on the way up and bad on the way down, or even when it hits the ground? How can it be good for a bubble when it forms, and bad when it bursts? The same for a lamp too.
自然为每物所设定之目标，既囊括其终止，正如囊括其起始与延续——如同一个人向上抛球。球在上升之时如何为善好、在下落之时又如何为坏，甚至落于地上时为坏？一气泡形成时如何为好、破灭时又如何为坏？灯烛亦然。
21
Turn it inside out and see what it is like, what it becomes in age, sickness, death. Life is short both for praiser and praised, for the remembering and the remembered. And this, moreover, in just a cranny of one continent: even here not all are attuned to each other, or even an individual to himself. Yet the whole earth is just a point.
将物翻转过来，看透它本质如何，在年迈、疾病、死亡之中它又变得怎样。寿命之短促，不论于赞扬者或被赞扬者、于记忆者与被记忆者，皆是一般无二。况且，这一切不过发生在一块大陆之一罅隙中：即便在此处，也并非人人彼此协调——甚或个人与自己也不协调。然而整个地球，不过是宇宙中一粒微点。
22
Concentrate on the subject or the act in question, on principle or meaning. You deserve what you're going through. You would rather become good tomorrow than be good today.
专注于事物本身，或所问之行动，或所涉之原则或含义。你正在经历的，正是你应得的。你宁愿明日成善，而不愿今日即是善人。
23
Doing something? I do it with reference to the benefit of mankind. Something happening to me? I accept it in reference to the gods and the universal source from which all things spring interrelated.
在做什么事？我做它以涉及人类之福祉。有事临到我身？我接纳它以涉及诸神，及那万物彼此牵联所自流出之宇宙源头。
24
Just as you see your bath — all soap, sweat, grime, greasy water, the whole thing disgusting — so is every part of life and every object in it.
正如你看见你的浴场——满是肥皂沫、汗渍、污垢、油腻浊水，整件事令人作呕——生命之每一部分及其中之每一对象，亦皆如此。
25
Lucilla buried Verus, then Lucilla was buried. Secunda buried Maximus, then Secunda next. So with Epitynchanus and Diotimus, Antoninus and Faustina. The same story always. Celer saw Hadrian to his grave, then went to his own grave. Where are they now, those sharp minds, those prophets or prigs? Certainly all ephemeral. All dead long ago.
卢西拉葬了维鲁斯，而后卢西拉亦被人葬。塞昆达葬了马克西穆斯，而后塞昆达随之而去。埃皮廷卡努斯与狄奥提穆斯、安东尼努斯与福斯蒂娜——莫不如此。同样之故事，反复重演。凯勒送哈德良入葬，而后也入了自己之葬。那些敏锐之心思、那些先知或自诩不凡之徒——如今安在？无非皆朝生暮死。一切皆早已故去。
26
Man's joy is to do man's proper work. And work proper to man is benevolence to his own kind, disdain for the stirrings of the senses, diagnosis of the impressions he can trust, contemplation of universal nature and all things thereby entailed.
人之乐，在于行人之恰当事务。而人之恰当事务，乃是：善待同类，鄙弃感官之扰动，辨识他可信任之印象，沉思宇宙本性及由此而涉之一切。
27
Three relations. First, to your environment; second, to the divine cause which is the source of all that happens to all men; third, to your fellows and contemporaries.
三种关系。其一，与你之周遭环境；其二，与那作为一切人事源泉之神性原因；其三，与你的同侪及同时之人。
28
Pain is an evil either to the body — so let the body give its evidence — or to the soul. But the soul can preserve its own clear sky and calm voyage by not assessing pain as an evil. Every judgement, every impulse, desire and rejection is within the soul, where nothing evil can penetrate.
痛苦对身体而言或是恶——那便让身体就此作其证言——或对灵魂也如是。但灵魂可以保持自身清朗之天空与沉稳之航程，只要它不评断痛苦为恶。每一判断、每一冲动、每一欲望与拒绝，皆发生于灵魂之内——而在此处，无一恶物得以渗入。
29
Erase the impressions on your mind by constantly saying to yourself: 'It is in my power now to keep this soul of mine free from any vice or passion, or any other disturbance at all: but seeing all things for what they are, I can treat them on their merits.' Remember this power which nature gives you.
抹去你心灵上之印象，恒常自谓：「我此刻有能力使此灵魂不受任何恶行或激情、或任何其他侵扰之染：看清万物之本来面目，我便可依其应得之对待而处理之。」记取自然赋予你的此一能力。
30
When you speak in the senate or to any individual, be straightforward, not pedantic. Use language which rings true.
在元老院中发言，或任对某个人说话，当直截而不学究。用那听来真切的言辞。
31
The court of Augustus — wife, daughter, grandsons, step-sons, sister, Agrippa, relatives, household, friends, Areius, Maecenas, doctors, diviners: an entire court dead. Go on now to other cases, where it is not the death of just one individual but of a whole family, like the Pompeys. And there is the famous inscription on the tomb: 'Last of his line'. Think of all the anxiety of previous generations to produce a successor: and then, necessarily, someone is the last. Here again the death of a whole line.
奥古斯都之宫廷——妻子、女儿、诸孙、诸继子、姊妹、阿格里帕、诸亲族、家众、友朋、阿瑞乌斯、梅塞纳斯、医者、占卜师：整座宫廷皆已死去。再看其他案例——非仅一人之死，而是整族之家，如庞培一族。而墓碑上那著名的铭文写着：「此族最后一人」。想想前代之人何等焦虑地求嗣以延宗续祀：而必然地，某人终成最后一人。此处又是一整族血脉之泯灭。
32
You must compose your life action by action, and be satisfied if each action achieves its own end as best can be: and no one can prevent you from that achievement. 'But there will be some external obstacle.' No obstacle, though, to justice, self-control, and reason. 'But perhaps some other source of action will be obstructed.' Still, by willing acceptance of the obstacle and by a calm change over to what is allowed, another action will immediately take its place which fits the composition we are talking of.
你必须一行接一行地缀成你之生命，若每一行动尽其可能达成了它自身之目的，便当满足：且无人能阻你达成此点。「但会有些外在之障碍。」然而，对正义、自制与理性，没有障碍。「但也许行动之某些其他方面将受阻。」即便如此，藉由乐意接纳障碍，并平心静气地转到较可为之他事，另一行动便将立时取而代之，正合我们所说之生命缀构。
33
Accept humbly: let go easily.
谦卑地领受，轻易地放手。
34
If you have ever seen a severed hand or foot, or a head cut off and lying some way away from the rest of the body — analogous is what someone does to himself, as far as he can, when he will not accept his lot and severs himself from society or does some unsocial act.
你若曾见过一只切断之手或足，或一颗被砍下且远远脱离身体其余部分的头颅——类比于此，便是一个人尽其力所能地对自己所做的：不接受自己之遭际，将自己与社会分割开来，或行某种反社会之举动。
35
Just as the nature of the Whole is the source of all other faculties in every rational creature, so it has given us this power too. In the same way that nature turns to its own purpose anything obstructive or contrary, placing it in the fated scheme of things and making it part of itself, so the rational being can turn any obstacle into material for himself and use it for whatever purpose he has set.
正如宇宙整体之自然是每一理性造物中一切官能之来源，它也将此一能力赐给了我们。自然将任何障碍或相逆之物转用来为其自身之目的，纳入注定的事物格局中，使之成为自身之一部分——同样地，理性存在者也可将任何障碍转为己用之材料，并将其用于自己所设定之任何目的。
36
Do not let the panorama of your life oppress you, do not dwell on all the various troubles which may have occurred in the past or may occur in the future. Just ask yourself in each instance of the present: 'What is there in this work which I cannot endure or support?' You will be ashamed to make any such confession. Then remind yourself that it is not the future or the past that weighs on you, but always the present, and the present becomes lighter when you isolate it and take your mind to task.
勿让你生命之全幅景象压迫于你，勿沉湎于过去已发生或将来可能发生之种种烦恼。只需在每一当下之情形中自问：「此事之中，有何为我所不可忍、不可堪者？」你必将羞于作任何如此之坦认。接着提醒自己：非未来或过去压在你身上，而永远是当下——并且当下在你将其单独抽离、并盘诘自己的心念时会变得轻省。
37
Is Panthea or Pergamus still sitting by the coffin of Verus? Or Chabrias or Diotimus by Hadrian's? Ridiculous! And if they were still sitting there, would the dead be aware? And if they were aware, would they be pleased? And if they were pleased, would that make their mourners immortal? Was it not their fate too to age in time and die? And when they died, what would those mourned do?
潘忒亚或佩尔伽穆斯还坐在维鲁斯之棺旁吗？或卡布里亚斯或狄奥提穆斯还在哈德良之棺旁吗？可笑之极！即便他们仍守坐彼处，死者能知觉吗？若能知觉，他们会高兴吗？若他们高兴，这能让哀悼者永生不死吗？哀悼者不也注定要随时日而老迈、终归死去吗？当他们死去，那些被哀悼者又该何如？
38
If you have sharp sight, use it: but, as the poet says, add wise judgement.
若你有敏锐之眼力，使用它：但如诗人所言，更要加之明智之判断。
39
In the constitution of the rational being I can see no virtue that counters justice: but I do see the counter to pleasure — self-control.
在理性存在者之构造中，我看不出有任何美德可与正义相抗衡：然我确实看见了对抗逸乐者——那便是自制。
40
If you remove your judgement of anything that seems painful, you yourself stand quite immune to pain. 'What self?' Reason. 'But I am not just reason.' Granted. So let your reason cause itself no pain, and if some other part of you is in trouble, it can form its own judgement for itself.
若你将对任何看似痛苦之事物的评判移除，你自身便全然不受痛楚。哪一自我？理性。但我并非仅是理性。诚然。那便让你的理性不给自己制造痛楚，而若你某个其他部分有困扰，它自可为它本身做其判断。
41
An obstacle to sense perception is harmful to animal nature. An obstacle to impulse is likewise harmful to animal nature. It follows that an obstacle to the mind is harmful to intelligent nature. Now apply all this to yourself. Does pain or pleasure affect you? Let the senses see to it. Is there an obstacle to your impulse? If your impulse was unconditional, then indeed an obstacle is harmful to your rational nature. But if you anticipated the obstacle, then your impulse is not yet harmed.
感觉知觉之障碍有害于动物本性。冲动之障碍同样有害于动物本性。由此推之：心灵之障碍有害于智性本质。如今将至理应用于你自己身上。痛苦或逸乐影响你了吗？让感官自己去管此事。有障碍阻了你的冲动吗？若你的冲动乃是无条件的，那么障碍确然有害于你之理性本性。但若你预先计及了障碍，则你的冲动尚未受伤害。
42
I have no cause to hurt myself: I have never consciously hurt anyone else.
我没有伤害己身之由：我也从未有意伤害过任何他人。
43
Joy varies from person to person. My joy is if I keep my directing mind pure, denying no human being or human circumstance, but looking on all things with kindly eyes, giving welcome or use to each as it deserves.
悦乐因人而异。我之悦乐，在于我保持主导之心灵纯净，不拒斥任何人或任何人事之境，而以善意之眼观看一切，赋予每者以其所应得之接纳或运用。
44
Look, make yourself a gift of this present time. Those who are more inclined to pursue fame hereafter fail to reckon that the next generation will have people just like those they dislike now: and they too will die. What, anyway, is it to you if this is the echo in future voices and this the judgement they pass on you?
看，将此时光化为你给自己之厚礼。那些更热衷于追求身后之名的人，未能算及：下一代也将满是这样的人——正如如今他们所不喜的那些人一般无二：且他们也将死去。无论如何，若未来之回声中他们如此评断你，这于你又有何干？
46
Nothing can happen to any human being outside the experience which is natural to humans — an ox too experiences nothing foreign to the nature of oxen, a vine nothing foreign to the nature of vines, a stone nothing outside the property of a stone. So if each thing experiences what is usual and natural for it, why should you take exception? Universal nature has not made you such as to find intolerable what is part of her own dispensation.
没有任何事会发生于任何人类身外之事——那些超出人类自然经验之外的事。一头牛也不会经历与牛之本性相隔阂的事，一藤蔓亦不经验与藤蔓本性无关的事，一石头亦不历其石固有属性之外的事。故若每物皆经历对其为常见而自然之事，你又为何要生气？宇宙自然未将你造成会发现其自身安排之一部分为不可忍受者。
47
If your distress has some external cause, it is not the thing itself that troubles you, but your own judgement of it — and you can erase this immediately. If it is something in your own attitude that distresses you, no one stops you correcting your view. So too if you are distressed at not achieving some envisaged good, why not rather concentrate on the achievement rather than the distress?
若你之烦恼有某种外在原因，则非事物本身在困扰你，而是你于此事之判断——而你可以立即消除它。若困扰你得是你自身态度中之某种东西，那便无人阻止你纠正你的看法。若你于此焦虑则是困于未能达成某设想之善，那何不集中精力于达成而非烦恼？
48
Remember that your directing mind becomes invincible when it withdraws into its own self-sufficiency, not doing anything it does not wish to do, even if its position is unreasonable. How much more, then, when the judgement it forms is reasoned and deliberate? That is why a mind free from passions is a fortress: there is no stronger place of retreat for a man, and anyone taking refuge there is then invincible.
当记：你的主导心灵退归自我之自足中、不做它不愿做之事时——即便其所处之境不合理——便已不可战胜。那么当它所下的判断乃是经推理兼审慎的，又该何其更不可战胜呢？这便是何以无激情之心灵乃是一座堡垒：世间无更牢固之退隐处所，凡藏身于此之人，此后便不可征服。
49
Do not elaborate to yourself beyond what your initial impressions report. You have been told that so-and-so is maligning you. That is the report: you have not been told that you are harmed. I see that my little boy is ill. That is what I see: I do not see that he is in danger. So always stay like that, not adding from within to what you are told, and you will not be harmed.
勿在初次印象所报者之外再加油添醋。你听说某某在诋毁你。这是所报告之事：你并未听到你受了伤害。我见我小儿病了。此为我之所见：我未见他陷入危险。故恒常如此——不于所报之事外再添从内而生之内容——你便不会受伤害。
50
A bitter cucumber? Throw it away. Brambles in the path? Go round them. That is all you need, without going on to ask, 'So why are these things in the world anyway?' That question would be laughable to a student of nature, just as any carpenter or cobbler would laugh at you if you objected to the shavings or offcuts from their work in their shops.
一只苦黄瓜？扔掉便是。路中有荆棘？绕开它们便是。这便是你需要做的全部，不必再追问「那么这些物事到底为何存于世间？」此一问在有识于自然之学的人看来，是可笑的——正如任何木匠或补鞋匠若听见你反感作坊中之木屑或废皮料，也会嗤笑你一般。
51
Do not be dilatory in action, muddled in communication, or vague in thought. Don't let your mind settle into depression or elation. Allow some leisure in your life. They kill, they cut in pieces, they hunt with curses. What relevance has this to keeping your mind pure, sane, sober, just? As if a man were to walk up to a clear spring of sweet water and curse it — the spring will not stop bubbling up its fresh water.
勿于行动拖拉，勿于沟通含混，勿于思想模糊。勿令你的心灵陷入消沉或亢奋。允些许闲暇入你生命之中。他们杀戮，他们将人碎尸，他们以诅咒追猎——这与你持守心灵纯净、清明、清醒、正直又有何关涉？正如一人走到一甘泉清源前去咒骂它——泉源不会因此停止涌出其鲜活之水。
52
Someone who does not know that there is an ordered universe does not know where he is. Someone who does not know the natural purpose of the universe does not know who he is or what the universe is. Someone who fails in any one of these ways could not tell the purpose of his own existence either. So what do you think of the man who runs from or courts the applause of a public who have no idea where they are or who they are?
人不识有一秩序井然之宇宙，便不知己身在何处。人不识宇宙之自然目的，便不知自己是谁，亦不知宇宙为何物。在任何一项上有所失缺的人，亦讲不出自己存在之目的。那么，你对一个要避开或是追求那全然不知身在何处、不知己为何人之大众掌声的人，又作何想法？
53
Do you want the praise of a man who curses himself three times an hour? Do you want to please a man who can't please himself? Can a man please himself when he regrets almost everything he does?
你愿取一每小时内咒骂自己三次的人之赞赏吗？你想取悦一个不能取悦自己的人吗？一个人倘若几乎后悔自己所做的所有事，他能取悦自己吗？
54
Don't now just take your breath from the surrounding air, but take your thought too from the mind which embraces all things. The power of mind spreads everywhere and permeates no less than the air: it is there for all who want to absorb it, just like the air for those who can draw breath.
不要仅从周遭空气吸取你的呼吸，也从那怀抱万物的心智中吸取你的思想。心智之力弥漫遍地，渗透无所不在，不下于空气：它摆在那里为所有愿吸纳它的人备用——正如空气为能呼吸者备用一般。
55
Wickedness overall does no harm to the universe. Individual wickedness does no harm to the recipient: it is only harmful to the perpetrator, and he has the option to be rid of it just as soon as he himself decides.
总体而言，邪恶于宇宙并无害。个体之邪恶于承受者并无害：它仅有害于那行恶者，而他一俟自己决定，便可转身弃去此恶。
56
To my determining will my neighbour's will is as indifferent as his breath and his body. Sure, we are born above all for the sake of each other: nevertheless the directing mind of each of us has its own sovereignty. Otherwise my neighbour's wickedness would be my own harm: and this was not god's intention, that my misfortune could depend on someone else.
于我的决择之意志来说，邻人之意志正如其呼吸、身体一般，于我漠不相干。固然，吾辈天生最要紧的是为了彼此之缘故；然而吾辈各自之主导心灵皆有自身之主权。否则，邻人之邪恶便会成为我之伤害：而神意并非如此——我的厄运竟要系于他人之身。
57
The sun appears to pour itself down, and indeed its light pours in all directions, but the stream does not run out. This pouring is linear extension: that is why its beams are called rays, because they radiate in extended lines. You can see what a ray is if you observe the sun's light entering a dark room through a narrow chink: it travels in a straight line and strikes, so to speak, any solid body which blocks its passage through the air beyond. There the light stops, without slipping off or falling. Now the flow of your thoughts should be like that: not pouring out, but extended. Their pressure on any obstacles it meets should be firm and steady: no violence or collapsing impact.
太阳似向下倾泻己身，而实则是其光芒在向一切方向流溢，然其流却不枯竭。此一流溢乃是直线延伸：故其光束被称为射线，因它们以延伸之线而辐射。你若观察太阳之光经一窄隙渗入幽暗之室中，便可见何为一道射线：它沿直线行进，撞击在（可以如是说）任何挡在超出空气路径之外的固形物体之上。光便停在彼处，不滑落、不坍坠。你的思想之流动亦当如此：不漫溢，而是延伸而前进。对于其所遇障碍，其挤压力应坚定而平顺：无暴戾或垮塌之冲击。
58
He who fears death fears either unconsciousness or another sort of consciousness. Now if you will no longer be conscious you will not be conscious either of anything bad. If you are to take on a different consciousness, you will be a different being and life will not cease.
凡畏惧死亡者，其所畏者要么是无知觉，要么是别一种知觉。然若你将不再有知觉，你便也不会知觉任何坏事了。若你将承接一种不同之知觉，你便将是一个不同之存在，而生命亦不会终结。
59
Men are born for the sake of each other. So either teach or tolerate.
人皆彼此而生。故，要么教诲，要么容耐。
60
An arrow flies in one way, the mind in another. Yet even when it is keeping on the alert or circling round an inquiry, the mind moves no less directly, and straight to its target.
箭矢以某一方式飞行，心灵则以另一种方式。然即便当它保持警觉或围绕一探究盘旋之刻，心灵之移动仍是直线而向标的，一如其直接飞行时一般无误。
61
Enter into the directing mind of everyone, and let anyone else enter your own.
进入每人主导之心灵，亦容许别人进入你自己的。

沉思录 卷九

1
Injustice is sin. Universal nature has constituted rational creatures for the sake of each other: to help each other, then, as is deserved, and never to harm each other. Someone who transgresses this is clearly guilty of sin against the most venerable of gods.
不正义即是罪。宇宙之本性将理性造物造为彼此之缘故而存：彼此按其所应得而相助，而永不相害。违犯此道者，显然犯了亵渎那最庄严神圣之罪。
2
Lying, too, is a sin against the same goddess: her name is Truth, and she is the original cause of all that is true. The conscious liar sins to the extent that his deceit causes injustice: the unconscious liar to the extent that he is out of tune with the nature of the Whole and out of order with the universe.
说谎，亦是对此一同样之女神的罪过：她之名乃是真理，且她是一切真实之事物之元始原因。有意识之说谎者，其罪在其欺诓导致不正义之程度：无意识之说谎者，其罪在其与宇宙整体之本性失和、与宇宙之秩序脱轨。
3
Moreover, the pursuit of pleasure as a good and the avoidance of pain as an evil constitutes sin. Someone like that must inevitably and frequently blame universal Nature for unfair distribution as between bad men and good, since bad men are often deep in pleasures and the possessions which make for pleasure, while good men meet with pain and what causes pain.
再者，以追求逸乐为善、逃避痛苦为恶，此亦构成罪过。那般行事之人，必然且频繁地责备宇宙之自然分配不公——竟厚于恶人而薄于善人，因恶人常沉浸于逸乐及产生逸乐之财物中，而善人则遭逢痛苦及引发痛苦之事。
4
Further, anyone who fears pain will also at times be afraid of some future event in the world, and that is immediate sin. And a man who pursues pleasure will not hold back from injustice — an obvious sin. Those who wish to follow Nature and share her mind must themselves be indifferent to those pairs of opposites in which Nature is also indifferent.
此外，凡惧怕痛苦者，亦有之时会惧怕世间某未来之事变，而这即是当行之罪。而一追求逸乐之人，亦必不会在行不义之时收敛——这是昭然之罪。凡愿追寻自然、与自然之心意共契者，必须于自然同样漠然相视之那些对立两面，自身也同样漠然不动。
5
By 'universal Nature treating these things indifferently' I mean that they happen impartially by cause and effect to all that comes into being and owes its being to the fulfilment of an original impulse of Providence. Under this impulse Providence set out from a first premise to establish the present order of the universe.
我所谓「宇宙之自然对这些漠然处之」，意即：它们藉由因果而平等无偏地发生于一切生成之物上——而这一切生成之物其存有皆源于天意之元初冲动之实现。在这一冲动之下，天意自第一前提出发，建立了宇宙当前之秩序。
6
These will suffice: the present certainty of judgement, the present social action, the present disposition well content with any effect of an external cause.
如此便已足够：当下判断之确然，当下之社会性行动，以及当下欣然接纳任何外来原因效应之性情倾向。
7
Erase the print of imagination, stop impulse, quench desire: keep your directing mind its own master.
抹除想象之印迹，止住冲动，熄灭欲望：令你主导之心灵做自己之主宰。
8
Irrational creatures share in one animate soul, and rational creatures partake in one intelligent soul: just as there is one earth for all the things of earth, and one light to see by, one air to breathe for all of us who have sight and life. All things which share some common quality tend to their own kind.
非理性之造物共有一有生魂灵，理性之造物则共有一智性灵魂：正如地之一切土物有一共同之大地，所有具视觉与生命者皆赖同一光以视物、同一空气以呼吸。凡共有某种同类品质之物，皆倾向其同宗之类属。
9
Look then at what is happening now. Only the intelligent creatures have now forgotten that urge to be unified with each other: only here will you see no confluence. They may run from it, but nevertheless they are overtaken: such is the power of nature. Look carefully and you will see what I mean. You will sooner find something earthy not touching earth than a man severed from man.
再看现在正发生之事。唯具智性之造物如今忘却了彼此合一之冲动：唯在此处，你看不到汇聚。他们或会逃离，然而自然终将追上他们：此自然之力如是。仔细观看，你将明白我所指之意。你找到不与大地相接之土物，要比找到一个与人隔绝之人来的更容易。
10
Man, god, and the universe all bear fruit, each in its own due season. No matter if common use confines the strict sense of 'bearing fruit' to vines and the like. Reason too has its fruit, both universal and particular: other things grow from it which share its own nature.
人、神、宇宙皆结其果实，各在其当有之季节。不论惯常用法是否将「结果实」这层严格之意义仅限于藤蔓之类。理性亦有其果实，既有普遍者，亦有特殊者：其他与之同性之物皆因此生长而出。
11
If you can, show them the better way. If you cannot, remember that this is why you have the gift of kindness. The gods too are kind to such people, and in their benevolence even help them achieve some ends — health, wealth, fame. You can do it too. Or tell me — who is stopping you?
若你能够，便将更善之路示与他们。若你不能，便记住：这正是你被赋予仁善这一禀赋的因由。诸神亦是以仁善待这般人等，且在其仁慈中甚而帮助他们达致某些目的——健康、财富、声名。你亦能做到。否则请告诉我——谁拦阻了你？
12
Work. Don't work as a miserable drudge, or in any expectation of pity or admiration. One aim only: action or inaction as civic cause demands.
去工作。勿如一名悲惨之苦役那般工作，亦勿怀着任何博取同情或钦羡的期待去工作。只有一个目标：行动或不行动，均视公民事业之所需而定。
13
Today I escaped from all bothering circumstances — or rather I threw them out. They were nothing external, but inside me, just my own judgements.
今日我从一切烦扰之处境中得脱——或更确切说，我将它们逐出。它们并非外物，而全在己内，仅为己之判断而已。
14
All things are the same: familiar in experience, transient in time, sordid in substance. Everything now is as it was in the days of those we have buried.
一切皆相同：经验中悉为熟见，时间中皆属短暂，实质上尽是污浊。如今之每一事，恰如我们已埋葬的那些先人的代时所是一般。
15
Mere things stand isolated outside our doors, with no knowledge or report of themselves. What then reports on them? Our directing mind.
赤裸之物事孤独立在我们的门扉之外，对其自身毫无知识、亦无信息。那么是何物在报告它们呢？吾辈之主导心灵。
16
Good or ill for the rational social being lies not in feeling but in action: just as also his own virtue or vice shows not in what he feels, but in what he does.
理性而社会性之存在者，其善与恶不在于感受，而在于行动：正如其自身之德性与邪行并非显现于其感受之中，而乃显现于其所行之事中。
17
A stone thrown in the air: nothing bad for it on the way down or good for it on the way up.
一块抛向空中之石：下落之途于它无坏，上升之道于它亦无好。
18
Penetrate into their directing minds, and you will see what sort of critics you fear — and what poor critics they are of themselves.
穿透他们之主导心灵，便将看出你所畏惧的是何等样之评判者——而他们裁判起自身来，又是何等蹩脚。
19
All things are in a process of change. You yourself are subject to constant alteration and gradual decay. So too is the whole universe.
万物皆处于变化之流程中。你自身亦在恒常之变易与逐渐之凋朽中。整个宇宙亦是如此。
20
You should leave another's wrong where it lies.
你当将别人之过错，留在其所在之处。
21
The termination of an activity, the pause when an impulse or judgement is finished — this is a sort of death, but no harm in it. Turn now to the stages of your life — childhood, say, adolescence, prime, old age. Here too each change a death: anything fearful there?
活动之终结，一冲动或判断完结之时之暂停——此亦是一种死亡，但于中并无伤害。现在转而返观你生命之各阶段——童年，譬如，少年，壮岁，暮年。其中每一变化皆是一种死亡：其间有什么可畏吗？
22
Hurry to your own directing mind, to the mind of the Whole, and to the mind of this particular man. To your own mind, to make its understanding just; to the mind of the Whole, to recall what you are part of; to this man's mind, to see whether there is ignorance or design — and at the same time to reflect that he is your kinsman.
疾赴你自身之主导心灵，赴宇宙整体之心灵，以及赴此一特定人之心灵。赴己之心灵，以令其理解正当公允；赴整体之心灵，以铭记你所附属之部分是什么；赴此人之心灵，以查看他究竟是出于无知还是蓄意——同时亦反思，他乃是你的同源之人。
23
Just as you yourself are a complementary part of a social system, so too your every action should complement a life of social principle. If any action of yours, then, does not have direct or indirect relation to the social end, it pulls your life apart and destroys its unity. It is a kind of sedition, like a man in a popular assembly who stands apart and acts on his own.
正如你自身是一个社会体系中的互补之部分，你之每一行动也当弥补成全一种具有社会性原则之生命。若你任何之行动，没有直接或间接关联于社会性之目标，它便会在撕开你之生命，毁坏其整全之统一。这是一类叛乱——如同在公众集会中，一人自行单独站立一旁，独行其是。
24
Children's tantrums and toys, 'tiny spirits carrying corpses' — the Underworld in the Odyssey strikes more real!
孩童之闹脾气与玩偶，所谓「渺小之灵魂携带着死尸」——《奥德赛》中之冥界反倒显得更为真实！
25
Go straight to the qualifying cause and examine it separately from the material element. Then establish the maximum time for which this individual thing thus qualified can by its nature subsist.
径直走向那定性之原因，将其与物质之元素分开来细加检审。然后，确定此一个别之事物在如此之定性下、能依其本性而存续的最长时间。
26
You have endured innumerable troubles by not leaving your directing mind to do the work it was made for. But enough.
你已因不让你之主导心灵做它本被造为去做之功，而忍受了数不尽之烦扰。然，此事可以休矣。
27
When another blames you or hates you, or people voice similar criticisms, go to their souls, penetrate inside and see what sort of people they are. You will realize that there is no need to be racked with anxiety that they should hold any particular opinion about you. But you should still be kind to them. They are by nature your friends, and the gods too help them in various ways — by dreams, by oracles — to win what they set their hearts on.
当别人责备你或恨恶你，或有人说出类似之批评时，请走进他们的灵魂，穿透进去，看看他们是何等样人。你将明白，无需为他们对你怀有怎样的意见而忧心如焚。但你仍应对他们友善。他们从本性上便是你的朋友，而诸神也以种种方式帮助他们——通过梦境、通过神谕——去赢获那些他们心之所倾的东西。
29
The universal cause is a torrent, sweeping everything in its stream. So, man, what does that mean for you? Do what nature requires at this moment. Start straight away, if that is in your power: don't look over your shoulder to see if people will know. Don't hope for Plato's utopian republic, but be content with the smallest step forward, and regard even that result as no mean achievement.
宇宙之原因是一道湍流，携卷一切于其流中。那么，人啊，这于你又意味着什么呢？于此刻做自然所要求之事。如果此事在你的权能之内，即刻开始：莫回首张望看别人是否会知道。莫寄望于柏拉图之乌托邦，而满足于微小之脚步渐进，且将此即便是如此之结果，也不视作微不足道之成就。
30
Take a view from above — look at the thousands of flocks and herds, the thousands of human ceremonies, every sort of voyage in storm or calm, the range of creation, combination, and extinction. Consider too the lives once lived by others long before you, the lives that will be lived after you, the lives lived now among foreign tribes: how many have never even heard your name, how many will quickly forget it, how many praise you now but will very soon turn to blame.
从高处俯瞰——看那千千万万之畜群与牧队、千千万万之人间仪式、各种在风暴或平静中的航行、创造与结合与绝灭之百态万象。也想想那些远在你之前、别人曾经活过之生命，那些将在你之后被活出之生命，此刻正在异族部落中被活出之生命：多少人甚至从未听说过你的名字，多少人转眼便将忘记它，多少人此刻在赞誉你，转眼便会转而责骂。
31
Calm acceptance of what comes from a cause outside yourself, and justice in all activity of your own causation. In other words, impulse and action fulfilled in that social conduct which is an expression of your own nature.
对外因所来之一切安然接纳，对己身所起之因的一切行为持守公道。换言之，由冲动与行动在那表达了你自身本性之社会行为中得以完成。
32
You can strip away many unnecessary troubles which lie wholly in your own judgement. And you will immediately make large and wide room for yourself by grasping the whole universe in your thought, contemplating the eternity of time, and reflecting on the rapid change of each thing in every part — how brief the gap from birth to dissolution, how yawning the gulf of time before your birth, and an equal infinity after your dissolution.
你可以剥去许多全然存于你自己判断中不必要的烦恼。而你立即便能为自己腾出广大宽阔之空间——只要以你的思想把握整个宇宙，凝思时间之永恒，并反思每物在每个部分中何其迅速的变化：从出生到消亡之间隔何其短暂，你出生之前时间之鸿渊何其广袤，而你消亡之后另有一同等之无穷。
33
All that you see will soon perish; those who witness this perishing will soon perish themselves. Die in extreme old age or die before your time — it will all be the same.
你所见之一切，即将消亡殆尽；那些见证这消亡的人，很快他们自身亦将消亡。于极老极寿之年死去，或于应有时刻之前死去——终究都是一样。
34
What are the directing minds of these people? What are they set on, what governs their likes and values? Train yourself to look at their souls naked. When they think that their blame will hurt or their praise advantage, what a conceit that is!
这些人的主导之心智是怎样的？他们究竟所倾心者为何，是什么在统御着他们的好恶与价值？自己训练自己，去观看他们赤裸的魂灵。当他们自认其责斥能伤害、其称赞能施惠时，这是何等之自大妄想！
35
Loss is nothing more than change. Universal nature delights in change, and all that flows from nature happens for the good. Similar things have happened from time everlasting, and there will be more such to eternity. So why do you say that everything has always happened for the bad and always will, that all those gods have clearly been powerless to correct this, and that the world has been condemned to the grip of a never-ending evil?
丧失不过是变化而已。宇宙之自然以变为乐，而一切自自然流出者皆因善而发生。类似之事自永恒以来便一直在发生，且将有更多此类之事直至永恒。你为何要说一切向来且必定是朝着坏的方向发生，说那无数之神明显然无力纠正此点，而世界已被定谳为一永无止歇的恶所钳制？
36
The rotting of the base material of everything. Water, dust, bones, stench. Again: marble is a mere deposit in the earth, gold and silver mere sediments; your clothing is animal hair, your purple is fish blood; and so on with all else. And the vital spirit is just the same, changing from this to that.
一切之基质材料皆在腐坏：水、尘、骨、恶臭。再如：大理石不过是地中一层积沉，金银不过是些沉渣；你之衣袍是兽之毛发，你的紫袍是鱼之血；其他一切，莫不如是。而那生气之灵也相同，由此至彼不断变去。
37
Enough of this miserable way of life, enough of grumbling and aping! Why are you troubled? What is new in this? What is it that drives you mad? The cause? Then face it. Or rather the material? Then face that. Apart from cause and material there is nothing. But you should even now, late though it is, make yourself a simpler and better man in your dealings with the gods.
这种可怜悲惨之生活方式可以罢了，那牢骚抱怨、那效颦学舌也可以罢了！你为何烦恼？此事中有什么是新的？是什么在驱使你疯狂？原因吗？去直面它。或是那物质吗？也去直面它。离开原因与物质，便再无一物。但即在此刻——虽已甚迟——你也当使自己在那与诸神的关系中成为一较简朴、更善好的人。
38
If he did wrong, the harm is to himself. But perhaps he did not do wrong.
若他做了错事，其伤害乃是他自己承受。但也许，他并未做错。
39
Either all things flow from one intelligent source and supervene as in one coordinated body, so the part should not complain at what happens in the interest of the whole — or all is atoms, and nothing more than present stew and future dispersal. Why then are you troubled? Say to your directing mind: 'Are you dead? Are you corrupt? Are you playing the hypocrite? Have you become a beast? Do you herd with the rest and share their food?'
要么万物皆从一智性之源而流出，并如在一个协调一体中互相续随——如此，部分便不应抱怨那为整体利益而发生的事——要么一切皆不过是原子，不超过当下这一锅乱炖及将来之离散。那么，你又为何烦恼？对你之主导心灵说：「你是死了吗？你朽烂了吗？你在假扮吗？你已变成了野兽？你和那群人混在一起分享他们的饲料了吗？」
41
Epicurus says: 'In my illness my conversations were not about the sufferings of my poor body, and I did not prattle on to my visitors in this vein, but I continued to discuss the cardinal principles of natural philosophy, with particular reference to this very point, how the mind shares in such disturbances of the flesh but is nonetheless immune to distress and can preserve its own proper good.'
伊壁鸩鲁说：「我病中之时，我的谈话并不围绕着我那可怜躯体之诸般痛苦，亦未就此絮叨于访客，而是继续讨论自然哲学之根本原则，尤其着力于是乎于此一要点：即心智虽涉身在此等肉体之扰乱中，却如何不受苦恼之扰，并能持守其自身本有之善好。」
42
Whenever you are offended at someone's lack of shame, you should immediately ask yourself: 'So is it possible for there to be no shameless people in the world?' It is not possible. Do not then ask for the impossible. This person is just one of the shameless inevitably existing in the world. Have the same thought ready for the rogue, the traitor, every sort of offender.
当你为某人之无廉耻而感到不快时，你当立即自问：「那么，世间可能全无无耻之徒乎？」那是不可能的。既如此，便不要奢求那不可能之事。此人只是那些势必存在于世间之无耻之徒中的一个。对于无赖、背叛者和各类冒犯之徒，也当备有同样之念。
43
Anyway, where is the harm or surprise in the ignorant behaving as the ignorant do? Think about it. Should you not rather blame yourself, for not anticipating that this man would make this error? Your reason gave you the resource to reckon this mistake likely from this man, yet you forgot and are now surprised that he went wrong.
无论如何，无知者所为正如无知者之所为，这之中又有何伤害或意外？仔细想想。难道你不应更该责怪你自己，因你未曾预料到此人会犯此等错误？你之理性已给了你借此量出此人极可能犯此谬误的凭具，然你却忘了，如见却对他行差错步而感到愕然。

沉思录 卷十

1
My soul, will you ever be good, simple, individual, bare, brighter than the body that covers you? Will you ever taste the disposition to love and affection? Will you ever be complete and free of need, missing nothing, desiring nothing live or lifeless for the enjoyment of pleasure? Or time for longer pleasure, or place, or country, or fine climate, or even human company? Will you not rather be satisfied with your present state, taking pleasure in all your present circumstances?
我的魂灵啊，你将会有朝一日变好、变素朴、变为一体、变为赤裸、变得比覆盖你的躯体更为清明吗？你将会有朝一日尝到爱与深情那甘美之性情吗？你将变得完足而一无所缺，错过了无遗、不贪求任何有生或无生之物以享悦逸乐吗？亦不贪求更长之享乐时光，或场所，或异乡，或宜人之气候，或甚至人之相伴吗？你难道不愿满足于当下之状态，在你当下一切之处境中怡然自悦吗？
2
Observe what your physical nature requires, as one subject to the condition of mere life. Then do it and welcome it, as long as your nature as an animate being will not be impaired. Next, you should observe what your nature as an animate being requires: again, adopt all of this, as long as your nature as a rational being will not be impaired. And rationality is also social. Apply these rules and go beyond them no further.
观察你之物理本性所需者——作为一仅受生命条件所制约者之所需。然后去做并接纳它，只要那无损于你作为一有生命之存在者的本性。其次，当观察你作为一有生存在者之本性所需者：同样，接纳所有这一切，只要那无损于你作为一理性存在者之本性。而理性亦是社会性之理性。运用这些规则，无可更逾越一步。
3
All that happens is an event either within your natural ability to bear it, or not. So if it is an event within that ability, do not complain, but bear it as you were born to. If outside that ability, do not complain either: it will take you away before you have the chance for complaint. Remember, though, that you are by nature born to bear all that your own judgement can decide bearable, or tolerable, in action.
一切所发生之事，其事件要么在你自然承受能力之内，要么不在。若其事在此能力之内，便莫抱怨，只如你生来所当为那般去承受。若其在外于此能力，也莫抱怨：它在你有机会抱怨之前，便会夺你而去。然而切记：你天生自本性上就能承受任何一你自身判断能在行动中判为可堪者、或可为者之事。
4
If he is going wrong, teach him kindly and show him what he has failed to see. If you can't do that, blame yourself — or perhaps not even yourself.
若他行差踏错，便以善意点拨他，示知他所未能见及之处。若你做不到，便责怪自己——或也莫要责怪自己。
5
Whatever happens to you was being prepared for you from everlasting, and the mesh of causes was ever spinning from eternity both your own existence and the incidence of this particular happening.
凡临到你身之事，自恒远以来早已为你预备好了，那因果之网络自永恒之太初便已同时纺织了你自己之存有与此一特定事件之降临。
6
Whether atoms or a natural order, the first premise must be that I am part of the Whole which is governed by nature. Secondly, I have some close relationship with the other kindred parts. So remembering that I am part of a Whole so constituted will leave me happy with all that happens to me. And in so far as I have some close relationship with the other kindred parts, I shall do nothing unsocial, but rather look to the good of my kin and have every impulse directed to the common benefit and diverted from the opposite.
无论是原子或是自然秩序，首要之前提乃是我乃是由自然所统御之宇宙整体之一部分。其次，我与其他同源之部分有着某种亲近之关系。故此，若记取我乃是一如此构成之整体的一部分，便会使我欣然接纳那临到我身之一切。且在同样之层次上，因我与其他同源之部分有着某种亲近之关系，我当不做任何违背社会之事，而当关注同类之利益，并将每一个冲动导引至公共之福祉而远离其反面。
8
Claim your entitlement to these epithets — good, decent, truthful; in mind clear, cooperative, and independent — and take care then not to swap them for other names: and if you do forfeit these titles, return to them quickly. Remember, too, that 'clarity of mind' was meant to signify for you discriminating attention to every detail and the concentration of thought. Launch yourself, then, on these few claims. If you can stay within them, stay there like a man translated to some paradise, the Islands of the Blest. But if you feel yourself falling away and losing control, retire in good heart to some corner where you will regain control — or else make a complete exit from life.
你去领受你应得的这些称号——好、端正、真诚；心灵清明、协同而独立——且留心莫要以它们去换别的名分：若你真的失去了这些称号，便迅速返回其中。也当铭记：清明之心智其本意便是标识你于每一细节的那种分辨之专注，以及凝思之聚中。那便向此寥寥数端的资格指标启程奋进吧。若你能栖止于其中，便栖之如一位被接引至某一天堂——至福之群岛——的人。但若你感到自己在滑坠、在失控，则安心退至某一隅，在那里你将重获掌管——否则，便全然退出此生。
9
Farce, war, frenzy, torpor, slavery! Day by day those sacred doctrines of yours will be wiped out, whenever you conceive and admit them untested by natural philosophy. Every perception, every action must both satisfy the circumstantial and exercise the theoretical, so that you preserve the confidence of right knowledge in every particular, yet keep it unobtrusive.
闹剧、战争、疯狂、僵麻、奴役！你那诸般神圣之教义日复一日渐被抹去——每当你在未经自然哲学之验证的情形下就构想它们、采纳它们时，便是如此。每一知觉、每一行动都须既满足具体情境之需要，同时也履行并锻炼那理论法则，如此，你方能于每一具体物事中保守正确知见之确信，却又决不使之锋芒外露。
10
A spider is proud to trap a fly. Men are proud of their own hunting — a hare, a sprat in the net, boars, bears, Sarmatian prisoners. If you examine their motives, are they not all bandits?
蜘蛛以捕捉苍蝇而骄傲。人则为自己的猎获而得意——一只兔、网中之幼鱼、野猪、熊、萨尔马提亚战俘。若你检审其动机，他们岂不是皆为强盗吗？
11
Adopt a systematic study of the way all things change into one another: pay constant attention to this aspect of nature and train yourself in it. Nothing is so conducive to greatness of mind.
系统性地研习万物彼此互变之道：恒切关注自然这一侧面，并在其中训练自己。无一物能如此助益于心灵之壮阔。
12
What need of prompt or hint when it is open to yourself to discern what needs to be done — and, if you can see your way, to follow it with kind but undeviating intent. If you cannot see the way, hold back and consult your best advisers. If some other factors obstruct this advice, proceed on your present resources with careful deliberation, holding to what is clearly just.
若你自己便能辨识何者需为——且若你能看清道路，便怀着善意而不移不易之志去追随它——那还用得着什么提示暗示呢？若你看不清路，便暂且停住，请教你最好的谋士们。若另有些因素阻碍了这些建议之执行，则倚靠你当下可用之资，沉谋稳重地推进，持守明确之正义。
13
As soon as you wake from sleep ask yourself: 'Will it make any difference to you if others criticize what is in fact just and true?' No, it will not. You have surely not forgotten what these people who whinny in praise or blame of others are like in their bed and at their board, the sort of things they do or shun, how they steal or grab, not with hands and feet but with the most precious part of themselves, which gives birth, as the choice goes, to trust, decency, truth, law, a good divinity within.
一朝醒来，便问自己：「若别人批评那事实上是正确而真实之物，这对你能有何不同呢？」不，决然不能。你必然不曾忘记这些在夸骂别人时嘶嘶叫叫的人，在床席上、餐几边是何种模样，他们所为与所回避的那一类事，他们以何等方式窃取掠夺——不用手足，而用他们自身最珍贵之部分：此一部分，随其自择，能给诞信心、端正、真理、法律，及一内在之善好神明。
14
Nature gives all and takes all back. To her the man educated into humility says: 'Give what you will; take back what you will.' And he says this in no spirit of defiance, but simply as her loyal subject.
自然给予一切，也收取一切。于她面前，那受教而养出谦逊之心的人说道：「凡你所愿赐者，请赐；凡你所愿收回者，便收回。」他这样说着，全无任何抗拒之心情，仅作为她忠实顺从之子民而言之。
15
The time you have left is short. Live it as if you were on a mountain. Here or there makes no difference, if wherever you live you take the world as your city. Let men see, let them observe a true man living in accordance with nature. If they cannot bear him, let them kill him — a better fate than a life like theirs.
你余剩之时间不多了。将它当做你在山巅上那般而活。这里或那里并无区别，只要不论在何处你都将世界视作你的城邦。让人们看见，让他们观察一个依照自然而生活的真实之人。若他们无法容忍他，便让他们杀了他吧——这总好过像他们那般苟活于世。
16
No more roundabout discussion of what makes a good man. Be one!
再莫绕圈子讨论什么成就一良善之人。自己去做一个便是！
17
Keep constantly in your mind an impression of the whole of time and the whole of existence — and the thought that each individual thing is, on the scale of existence, a mere fig-seed; on the scale of time, one turn of a drill.
在你心中恒常保持对整个时间与整个存有之印象——且常抱持此念：每一单独之事物，在存有之尺度上，不过一粒无花果之籽种；在时间之尺度上，不过是一次钻针之旋转。
18
Consider any existing object and reflect that it is even now in the process of dissolution and change, in a sense regenerating through decay or dispersal: in other words, to what sort of 'death' each thing is born.
思及任何现存之对象，作如此之反思：它即便此刻已在消解与变化的过程中了，在某种意义上，它正透过腐烂或消散在不断更新——换言之，每一样物事皆是为某一种「死亡」而生的。
19
What sort of people are they when eating, sleeping, coupling, shitting, etc.? Then what are they like when given power over men? Haughty, quick to anger, punishing to excess. And yet just now they were slaves to all those needs for all those reasons: and shortly they will be slaves again.
当他们进食、睡觉、交媾、排泄等等之时，是何种样人？而后，当他们既得控人之权柄时，又变成了何般模样？傲慢不逊、易怒、罚越其度。然而就在刚才，他们还为诸般需求之种种理由而当着奴隶：且不久他们又将复为奴隶。
20
What universal nature brings to each is brought to his benefit. The benefit stands at the time of its bringing.
宇宙自然带给每个人之物，皆是为其利益而带来。这利益，就立于其被带至的时刻。
21
'Earth loves the rain, the proud sky loves to give it.' The whole world loves to create futurity. I say then to the world, 'I share your love.' Is this not the source of the phrase, 'This loves to happen'?
「大地恋雨」，骄傲之穹苍「乐于赐雨」。整个世界皆爱创造将来。那么，我对这世界说，「我与你怀有同一之爱。」这不正是那句「此事乐耶发生耳」之语源么？
22
Either you live on here, used to it now; or you retire, your own decision to leave; or you die, your service done. No other choice. Be cheerful, then.
要么你继续活在此地，至今也已惯了；要么你自行退去，依己之决定而离去；要么你死去，你之服役已经完成。别无可选。那么，常持乐悦之态吧。
23
Always have clear in your mind that 'the grass is not greener' elsewhere, and how everything is the same here as on the top of a mountain, or on the sea-shore, or wherever you will.
恒常清楚记在你之心中：别处之草并不更绿，一切在此处且与在山巅上、或在海岸边、或在任何你所愿之所在，皆一般相同。
24
What is my directing mind to me? What am I turning it into now, what use am I making of it? Is it drained of intelligence? Is it divorced and broken off from society? Is it so interfused and welded to the flesh that it sways with its tides?
我之主导心灵于我而言究为何物？我此刻正将其转变作什么，我又是把它当作何等用途而加以运用？它是否被抽干枯尽至于空无智性？它是否已被剥离割裂而脱离了社会？它是否已如此交融而与骨肉焊接在一处，致使其随其躯体之潮汐而起伏摇摆？
25
A slave running from his master is a fugitive. Law is our master: the law-breaker is therefore a fugitive. But also in the same way pain, anger, or fear denote refusal of some past, present, or future order from the governor of all things — and this is law, which legislates his lot for each of us. So the man who is subject to pain, anger, or fear is a fugitive.
自其主人而逃的奴隶是一逃犯。法律是我等之主人：故此违法者便是逃犯。然疼痛、愤怒或恐惧等，亦以同样之方式表示对其一过去、现在或将来自那统御万有者之次序的拒斥——而此即是他为吾辈每一人配定命运之法也。所以，那受制于痛苦、愤怒或恐惧的人，便是逃犯。
26
A man deposits his sperm in a womb and goes away. Thereafter another cause takes over, does its work, and produces a baby. What a result from what a beginning! Then again. The child takes food down its throat, and now another causal sequence takes over, creating sensation and impulse, the whole of life and strength, and all the other results: what a product!
一个人将精子注入一子宫，径自离去。其后另一原因接手，尽其工作，产出一婴孩。这是由何种之始产生了何种之果！再之，孩子摄进食物经由其喉，此时另一因缘序列又接手介入，创生了感觉、冲动，以及整体之生命与力量，并所有其余之果实：这是何等之产物！
27
Constantly reflect that all the things which happen now have happened before: reflect too that they will happen again in the future. Have in your mind's eye whole dramas with similar settings, all that you know of from your own experience or earlier history.
恒常反思：如今所发生之一切事，全都在从前发生过：也要反思：它们将来亦会复现。在你心目中，历历映现完整之诸出剧幕，场景何其相似——所有你自自身经验抑或较早历史中所知之一切情景。
28
Picture everyone voicing pain or discontent at anything, as like a pig at a sacrifice, kicking and squealing. Just the same is the man who keeps it to himself, silently resentful on his bed. Think of all the threads that bind us, and how only rational creatures are given the choice of submitting willingly to providence: mere submission is necessity for all.
试想一下，凡为某事发出痛楚或不满之声的人，都类如一只祭祀中之猪，尥蹄而嘶嚎。同样，那掩于一己之内自己之床位上默然怀怨的人，也并无不同。想想这些将我等系在一起的繁多丝线，并想想唯理性之造物才被赋予了那心甘情愿顺服于天意之选项：而所有愚蠢的造物，其顺服仅是必然。
29
Consider each individual thing you do and ask yourself whether to lose it through death makes death itself any cause for fear.
思及你所做之每一单独之事，并扪心自问：若因死亡而失去此物，此事是否使死亡本身构成了堪畏的理由？
30
Whenever you take offence at the wrong done by another, move on at once to consider what similar wrong you are committing — it could be setting value on money, or pleasure, or reputation, and so on through the categories. This reflection will quickly damp your anger, aided by the further thought that he is acting under compulsion: what else could he do? Or, if you can, remove the compulsion.
每当你对他人所犯之过错感到冒犯，当即转而省思你自己究竟所犯同类之过错是何——或是把价值寄托在金钱上，或是逸乐上，或是名声上，或通此等类别中的任何一者。此番省思将迅速消解你之愤怒，而如果再辅以另一重思量的话——即他乃是受迫而行事：试问他还能怎样？——则效果更佳。或，若你能除去那种强迫，便图而为之。
31
When you see Satyrion or Eutyches or Hymen, picture them in Socrates' circle; when you see Eutychion or Silvanus, picture Euphrates; when you see Tropaeophorus, picture Alciphron; when you see Severus, picture Crito or Xenophon; and when you look at yourself, picture one of the Caesars — for each, then picture the counterpart. Then let this thought strike you: 'Where are they now?' Nowhere — or who knows where?
你看见萨堤里翁或欧提刻斯或许门时，便想象他们在苏格拉底那个圈中；看见欧提基翁或西尔瓦努斯时，便想象欧弗拉特斯；看见特洛派奥弗鲁斯时，便想象阿尔基弗朗；看见塞维鲁斯时，便想象克力同或色诺芬；而当你审视自己时，则想着某位凯撒——换句话说，替每一人，想出其匹配之对应者。接着，让此念触动你：「他们如今安在？」无处可觅——又或者，又有谁知其在何处？
32
Let no one have the chance to accuse you, with any truth, of not being sincere or a good man: make sure that anyone taking this view of you is a liar. This is wholly up to you — who is there to prevent you being good and sincere? You must just decide to live no longer if you won't have these qualities. Not even does reason dictate living in the absence of reason.
莫给任何人以一丝一毫之真正凭据来指责你不真诚或不是个好人：确保任何这般看你的人，都是一个说谎者。此事全然在你——谁又能拦阻你做一个好人、做真诚的人呢？你只须下定决心：若你不能具备这些品质，便不再生存下去。即便是理性，也不要求你在一无理性之状态中存续。
33
Now the roller does not have the gift of following its own movement wherever it will, nor does water or fire, or anything else subject to a nature or life without reason: there are many barriers or impediments in their way. But mind and reason have the power, by their nature and at their will, to move through every obstacle. Keeping clear in your view this easy facility of reason to carry through all things — like fire rising, a stone falling, a roller on a slope — stop looking for anything more. Any remaining hindrances either come from the corpse which is our body, or — without the judgement and consent of our own reason — cause no damage and do no evil: otherwise anyone meeting such hindrance would immediately become bad himself.
滚轮并无追随它自身滚动、想去哪里就去哪里的天赋，水与火亦然，任何受制于某一无理性之自然或生命之一切其他物事亦然：它们之路途上有许多栅栏阻碍。但心灵与理性却有此一力量，依其本性与其意志，逾越一切障碍而前进。在你眼前清清晰晰地观看理性这份轻松穿透一切万物的能力吧——如火焰上腾，如石块下落，如滚轮沿斜坡而下——然后便不要再顾盼任何其余之物。所留之一切剩余障碍，要么出于吾辈之尸身即吾辈之躯体，要么——在没有己身理性之判断与同意之下——既不会造成伤害，也不构成恶事；否则，任何人一旦遭遇这类阻碍，便立时会变成坏人了。
34
One bitten by the true doctrines needs only a very short and commonplace reminder to lose all pain and fear — for instance: 'Your children are no more than leaves. Leaves too these loud voices of loyal praise, these curses from your opponents, this silent blame or mockery: mere leaves likewise the letters from posterity which will pass on your fame. All these things come round again in spring; then the wind drops them, and the forest grows more to take their place. Short-lived is all there is, yet you pursue or shun as if it were eternal.'
一被真道所咬之人，只需极区区且通俗之一点省醒，便足以丢掉一切苦楚与恐惧——比如：「你的子女亦不过尔尔树叶而已。这般那些高声歌功之忠敬赞颂、这般那些来自于你敌手之咒骂，这般那些默然之责斥或嘲讽——无不属于树叶；那将从后人中寄来传递你名声的种种书函，也都同样是些叶子罢了。所有这些皆在春天复又轮至；然后风将它们扫落，林木又长出更多的来替代。所存有之一切皆是昙花一现，然而你却竟似其永恒那般追逐或回避。」
35
The healthy eye must look at all there is to be seen, and not say 'I only want pale colours' — this is a symptom of disease. The healthy ear and nose must be ready for all sounds or smells, and the healthy stomach must accept all food in the same way that a mill accepts all it was made to grind. So too the healthy mind must be ready for all that happens. A mind which says 'My children must live' or 'All men must praise all I do' is like an eye demanding pale colours or teeth soft food.
健全之眼应看那一切可看之物，而不是说「我只要灰淡之色」——此乃疾病之症候。健全之耳与鼻应能纳受一切声音或气味，健全之胃亦须纳受一切食物，如同磨坊纳受一切它被造以为之碾磨的谷物一样。同样，健全之心灵也须为所发生之一切备便就绪。一心灵若说：「我的孩子必须活下去」或「所有人都须来赞美我所行之一切」，便仿似一只眼睛只要看灰淡之色，或一副牙齿只吃柔软之食。
36
No one is so fortunate as not to have standing round his deathbed some people who welcome the fate coming on him. Was he the earnest sage? Then maybe there will be someone at his final moment saying to himself: 'We can breathe again now, rid of this schoolmaster. He was not hard on any one of us, but I could feel his silent criticism of us all.' So much for the earnest sage. As for the rest of us, consider how many other reasons there are for a host of people to want rid of us. You will think of this when dying: 'I am leaving a life in which even my closest companions, for whom I strove, prayed, worried so much, themselves wish me to go.' Why would anyone grasp at a longer stay in the world?
没人能如此幸运，使其临终之床旁连一个欢迎那降临到他身上之命运的人也没有。他是那严肃的智者么？那么也许在他最后一刻，会有人心内自念道：「我们总算又能透过气来了，终于除掉了这个蹩师。他从没格外苛待我们哪一个过，但我能感觉到他那沉默的、对我们所有人的批评。」严肃之智者，风光如此。至若我们其余之人，且想有多少别的原因，会使得一大帮人想除掉我们。你临终之际将思及此节：「我将离开的这生涯中，即便是那些我最亲密的同伴——那些我为之奋斗、为之祈祷、为之焦心得要命的伙伴——他们竟然都希望我离开。」那又为何还恋世多挨一刻呢？
37
As far as you can, get into the habit of asking yourself in relation to any action taken by another: 'What is his point of reference here?' But begin with yourself: examine yourself first.
只要你能够，养成如此之习惯：于别人所做之任何行动，反首问一己：「他此举之参照据准是什么？」但请先从你自己开始：先检审你自己。
38
Remember that what pulls the strings is that part of us hidden inside: that is the power to act, that is the principle of life, that, one could say, is the man himself. So never give any equal thought to the vessel which contains it or the organs built round it. These are an instrument like an axe, differing only in that they are grown onto the body.
切记：那拉动绳线者乃是我们内中那隐而不见的部分：那即是行事之能力，那即是生命之原则，那——可谓——就是此一人自身。故此，永勿以同等之思虑去考量容载其的那一器皿，亦莫关注那构筑在它周围之诸般器官。这些器官不过是一件如斧头般的工具而已，它之差异，仅在于它们是长在了躯体之上罢了。

沉思录 卷十一

1
Further, the rational soul traverses the whole universe and its surrounding void, explores the shape of it, stretches into the infinity of time, encompasses and comprehends the periodic regeneration of the Whole. It reflects that our successors will see nothing new, just as our predecessors saw nothing other than we see now: so that in a man of forty, with a modicum of intelligence, has seen, in a sense, all that was and ever will be — the constant sameness of things.
更进一步，理性之灵魂周游整个宇宙及其周遭之虚空，探索其形态，延入时间之无穷，环抱并领会那宇宙整体之周期新生。它深省到：后来者将看不到任何新物，正如已逝之众先人所见者亦不逾于吾人今日之所见；故在一个四十岁之人，只要稍具智识，便已在某种意义上见识过一切曾发生之事——也见识了一切将永来者——概皆事物之恒常不变。
2
Particular qualities too of the rational soul are love of neighbour, truthfulness, integrity, no higher value than itself. This last is a defining quality of law also. There is thus no difference between the true principle of philosophy and the principle of justice.
理性灵魂之特别品质亦有：爱邻人、诚实、正直，及不以他物为高于己身者——此最后一端亦是法律之界定品质。故此，哲学之真原则与正义之原则之间，并无分别。
3
You will think little of the entertainment of song or dance or all-in wrestling if you deconstruct the melodic line of a song into its individual notes and ask yourself of each of them: 'Is this something that overpowers me?' You will recoil from that admission. So too with a comparable analysis of dance.
若你将一支曲目之旋律线分解为单独逐个之音符，且于每一音符反躬自问：「这音符能征服我吗？」，你便会不以为然。你将悻悻然避之而耻对自己的坦承。以相似之分析去肢解舞蹈也是一样。
4
What a noble thing is the soul ready for its release from the body, if now must be the time, and prepared for whatever follows — extinction, dispersal, or survival! But this readiness must come from a specific decision: not in mere revolt, like the Christians, but thoughtful, dignified, and — if others are to find you credible — without any air of tragedy.
灵魂若已备妥于一旦有需从其肉身得释之时——无论随后者为何物：灭绝、离散、或续存——这是一件何其高贵之事！但此等备妥，须从一种特定之决断中出来：不可仅在爆发般之叛逆中——并非那类出于一时之愤激——而是沉静、有尊严、且——若要令人信服——不带一丝悲剧表演之气。
5
Have I done something for the common good? Then I too have benefited. Have this thought always ready to hand: and no stopping.
我可曾为公共之福祉做了什么事？若然，则我自身亦已受益。常备此念于心：然后不要休止。
6
What is your profession? Being a good man. But this can only come about through philosophic concepts — concepts of the nature of the Whole, and concepts of the specific constitution of man.
你的职志为何？做一良善之人。但此仅能经由那些哲学之概念而成就——彼关乎宇宙整体本性及人之特定构造之概念方可达也。
7
First, tragedies were brought on stage to remind you of what can happen, that these happenings are determined by nature, and that what moves you in the theatre should not burden you on the larger stage of life. You can see the way things must turn out and that even those who cry 'Oh Cithaeron!' must bear it.
首先，悲剧之搬上舞台，乃为提醒你何事可能发生，而这些事情皆由自然所注定——且在那戏院里所动你心的那点事情，不该在人生这更大的舞台上成为你之负担。你能看清万物必然归趋之道，且即便那些口中狂呼「呜呼喀泰戎！」的人，也必须忍受这一切。
9
Just as those who try to block your progress along the straight path of reason will not be able to divert you from principled action, so you must not let them knock you out of your good will towards them. Rather you should watch yourself equally on both fronts, keeping not only a stability of judgement but also a calm response to those who try to thwart you in other ways. To be angry with them is no less a weakness than to abandon your course of action and be panicked into surrender.
正如那些妄图阻挡你在理性之正道上向前精进的人，终究不能将你从有原则之行动中拐开分毫——同样，你也不可让他们将你逐出你对他们的那份善意。倒是应当在两条阵线上同样审视自己，既要保持判断之安稳不动，也要保持对那些试图以其他方式挫败你者的平静回应。对他们发怒，同样是一软弱之举——正如放弃你之行动路线、因惊慌而屈服般无异。
10
'No nature is inferior to art': in fact the arts imitate the variety of natures. If that is so, then the most perfect and comprehensive of all natures could not be surpassed by any artistic invention. Now all arts create the lower in the interests of the higher: so this is the way of universal nature too. And from here is the origin of justice, which gives rise to all the other virtues. Justice cannot be maintained if we set value on things indifferent, or are easily deceived, or stumble at obstacles, or are subject to change.
「无一自然逊色于技艺」。事实上，诸门技艺所摹仿者，正是诸般自然之多姿样态。若果如此，则一切自然中之最完美且最涵盖者，断无任何人为手艺之发明可出其右。那么，一切技艺皆为更高者之利益而创造所低等者：故此，这也是宇宙本性所行之方式。正义正起源于此处——而正义又生发出一切其他之美德。正义若不能在行事时受到尊崇，便无可持守——不论我们是否执着地寄价值于那漠不相干之物，或轻易受欺，或在阻碍面前屡屡绊跌，或顺从于因果迁变之摆布——正义都将无法立足。
11
The external things whose pursuit or avoidance troubles you do not force themselves on you, but in a way you yourself go out to them. However that may be, keep your judgement of them calm and they too will stay still — then you will not be seen either to pursue or to avoid.
那些你趋之若骛或避之唯恐不及而致烦乱你的外在事物，并非它们自己强加于你的——相反，在某种意义下，是你自己先走向了它们。然不论情况为何，只要保持你对此类物之判断于安然，它们自身亦将随之不动——如是，你便不显得像在追索，也不像在躲闪。
12
The soul is a sphere which retains the integrity of its own form if it does not bulge or contract for anything, does not flare or subside, but keeps the constant light by which it sees the truth of all things and the truth in itself.
灵魂乃是一球体，若能不为任何事而凸鼓或挛缩，不燃出偏焰也不往下沉陷，而保持着那恒常之光芒——并以此光见照万物之真理与己内之真理——则此球即能持存其自身形式之完满。
13
Someone despises me? That is his concern. But I will see to it that I am not found guilty of any word or action deserving contempt. Will he hate me? That is his concern. But I will be kind and well-intentioned to all, and ready to show this very person what he is failing to see — not in any criticism or ostentation, but in genuine good faith.
某人鄙视我？那是他的事。但我将留意于此：决不让自己被发现在任何一言一行之举上，该当配得起轻蔑。他将恨我吗？那也是他的事。但我将对一切人都仁善而怀好意，且甘愿示知此一具体之当事者他所忽略了而未看见的是什么——并非存心存意地表显出责备或炫示苛责，而是以真正由衷之信念身体力行。
14
They despise each other, but still toady to each other: they want to win, but still grovel.
他们彼此鄙视，却又彼此谄媚迎合；他们想赢过对方，却仍旧卑躬屈膝趴伏在地。
15
The rotten pretence of the man who says, 'I prefer to be honest with you'! What are you on about, man? No need for this preface — the reality will show. It should be written on your forehead, immediately clear in the tone of your voice and the light of your eyes, just as the loved one can immediately read all in his lover's glance. In short, the good and honest man should have the same stamp as the rank-smelling goat, so that whoever stands close must be aware of it, will he nill he. Calculated honesty is a stiletto.
那口中说「我这人，宁可和你掏心见胆」的家伙，是何等腐臭之伪饰！你嚼什么舌根呢，人啊？何须此前言引子——那事实就是会将你揭露。它当下就该刻在你之前额上，当下在你音声的音调和目光的清度上清清楚楚——就如所爱者可在其情人一瞥中尽读一切情愫那般。简而言之：那良善而正直之人，当有着如同浑身发出刺鼻异味之公羊一样的印记——不论谁无论他愿意与否，靠近他一站便必觉知。狡计裁夺过的一副坦诚，那可真是一把利小刀。
16
Live through life in the best way you can. The power to do so is in a man's own soul, if he is indifferent to things indifferent. And he will be indifferent if he looks at these things both as a whole and analysed into their parts, and remembers that none of them imposes a judgement of itself or forces itself on us. These things stay still, while it is we who create the judgements about them, and inscribe them (so to speak) on ourselves, even though we need not inscribe them, or, if we do, we can erase them.
以你能达到的最善方式度此一生。这样的能力就在于一个人自家的灵魂里——若能不执于那些漠不相干之事物。而他于它们能做到淡然相看，便也须将此类事物视为整全、也将它们分解开来看成仔细分体来视察，并记取：其中并无任何一个是强加以它自身之判断给我们、或将自身硬塞到我们身上的。这些事物静止不动，乃是我们自己创造了关于它们的这些判断，又以某种方式书写、刻画在我们自己心里——尽管我们原本不必刻画上去；或者，即使未曾企及这般，也还是能够最终将它们擦除以去的。
17
With each object of experience consider its origin, its constituents, what it is changing into, what it will be when changed — and that no harm will come to it.
面对每一经验之对象，思量其由来、其构成部分、它将变成什么、变化之后将成为何物——并知晓，并无伤害降临于它。
18
First. How do I regard my relation to them, and the fact that we were all born for each other: and, turning the argument, that I was born to be their leader, as the ram leads his flock and the bull his herd? But start from first principles. If not atoms, then nature governing all: if so, then the lower are for the sake of the higher, and the higher for each other.
第一：我怎样看待我之于他们的关系，以及吾辈皆是为了彼此而出生这一事实；再换个角度说，我之被造乃为做他们的领头者——如公羊领其群，公牛统其兽群那般。不过，还是从头一项原则说起。既非原子，便是自然统御一切；若果如此，则低者乃为高者而有，而高者又彼此相为养惠。
19
There are four particular corruptions of the directing mind for which you must keep constant watch, and eliminate them whenever you detect them. First: 'This mental image is superfluous.' Second: 'This could weaken the bond of community.' Third: 'This would not be yourself speaking to yourself.' Fourth: 'This is a violation of the divine element within you.'
主导之心灵有四桩特定之腐败偏差，你必须恒常警觉防范，并一经察觉便立时将其消除。其一：「这心中形象乃是不必要之多余。」其二：「这势将削弱那共同体的连结纽带。」其三：「此非你自己在对你自己讲的话。」其四：「这乃是亵渎你内中神性之份量。」
21
'The man without one and the same aim in life cannot himself stay one and the same throughout his life.' The maxim is incomplete unless you add what sort of aim that should be. Judgements vary of the whole range of various things taken by the majority to be goods in one way or another, but only one category of aim is acceptable to me: the social good, that is, the common good.
「那个在生命中无一恒常相同之目标的人，断不能自己在全此一生中保持一恒常相同之状态。」此一格言若不增入其所应属之目标类项，则是不完整的。意见纷纭，于大多数人以这些或那些各般方式视作好的事物之通范围莫不如此；然而，唯有一类目标在我看是可以接受的：社群的善，也即公共之善好。
22
The hill mouse and the house mouse — and the frightened scurrying of the house mouse.
山上的野鼠与家中的室鼠——以及那屋中鼠的惊惶之蹿窜。
23
Socrates used to call the popular beliefs 'bogies', things to frighten children with.
苏格拉底习惯将那些流行之信条称为「妖恫之怪」，不过用之以吓唬孩童罢了。
24
At their festivals the Spartans would put seats for visitors in the shade, and sit themselves wherever they could.
在他们的节庆中，斯巴达人会把阴凉处之座位让予来访的客人们，而自己不拘何处随地落坐。
25
Socrates to Perdiccas of Macedon, declining the invitation to visit him: 'to avoid dying the worst of deaths' — that is, the inability to return in kind benefits received.
苏格拉底致信马其顿之佩尔狄卡斯（辞其邀请访问）道：「只为避免那最糟糕之死法」——即不能以同样恩惠报答所已受之好处也。
26
In Epicurean writings there is laid down the precept that one should continually keep in mind one of those who followed the path of virtue in earlier times.
在伊壁鸩鲁学派之著述中，有一训谕被规订明载：当令一己常恒铭念古代早期那些遵循美德之途的先行者中的一人在心。
27
The Pythagoreans say, 'Look at the sky at dawn' — to remind ourselves of the constancy of those heavenly bodies, their perpetual round of their own duty, their order, their purity, and their nakedness. No star wears a veil.
毕达哥拉斯学派有名句：「日出之前看那诸天」——为提醒我们那些穹苍星宿之恒常不变、它们各安其职的永续运行、它们肃穆的秩序、其纯粹，以及其赤露无遮。无一星辰佩戴罗帏面纱。
28
Think of Socrates in his underclothes when Xanthippe had gone out with his coat: and what he said to his friends retiring in embarrassment when they saw the state of his dress.
想想苏格拉底，当他的妻子将他的外套抱走外出之际，他自己仅穿着底衬内裳之时的情形罢；也再记他对其朋友们——当他们看到他衣装困窘之态便尴尬退避之时——所言之语。
29
In writing and reading you must learn before you can teach. Yet more so in life.
书写读阅，你必须先自学会，然后才能教人。而人生，却更是如此。
33
Only a madman looks for figs in winter: just as mad to hope for a child when the time of this gift is past.
惟狂者在冬日里寻无花果；同样疯狂若当那赐赠的时刻已然过去，还寄望守候一婴孩的降临。
34
Epictetus used to say that when you kiss your child you should say to yourself: 'Tomorrow you may be dead.' But these are ominous words! 'No,' he replies, 'nothing is ominous which points to a natural process. Otherwise it would be ominous to speak of the corn being reaped.'
爱比克泰德常说，你在亲吻自己孩子时，当在心里悄语自道：「明之日你或许便会死去了。」此终属一家不祥之言。「非也，」他答道「任何指示向一自然之过程之语，皆非不祥的——否则言及庄稼谷穗收割之事，也当成了不祥之辞了。」
35
Grapes unripe, ripened, raisined: all changes, not into nonexistence, but into not-yet existence.
葡萄未熟，然后变熟，变作葡萄干：变化皆是——并非走向虚无，而是走入那尚未存在的存在形态中。
36
'No thief can steal your will' — so Epictetus.
「窃贼无能盗走你的意志」——此为爱比克泰德之言。
37
Another saying of his: 'We must discover an art of assent, and in the whole field of our impulses take care to ensure that each impulse is conditional, has a social purpose, and is proportionate to the value of its goal.'
爱比克泰德又有一言：「吾辈必须发现一门『同意之术』，并于冲动之整个场域中留心确保每一冲动皆带有条件预设、抱持一社会性目的、且与其目标之价值恰成对称均衡。」
38
Again. 'So this is not a contest for a trivial prize: at issue is madness or sanity.'
复有言：「如此看来，这并非一场奖酬微薄之竞斗：较量之焦点乃在疯狂抑或明智之间。」
39
Socrates used to question thus. 'What do you want to have? The souls of rational or irrational beings?' 'Rational.' 'What sort of rational beings? The pure or the lower?' 'The pure.' 'Why then don't you aim for that?' 'Because we have it.' 'Why then your fighting and disagreements?'
苏格拉底习惯如此发问答疑：「你想拥有怎样的灵魂？理性存在者的，还是非理性的？」「理性的。」「哪一类的理性者？那纯洁的，还是那低劣的？」「纯洁的。」「如此，你缘何不追求此目标呢？」「因我们已拥有它了。」「既如此，你又为何而纷争不解与彼此龃龉呢？」

沉思录 卷十二

1
All that you pray to reach at some point in the circuit of your life can be yours now — if you are generous to yourself. That is, if you leave all the past behind, entrust the future to Providence, and direct the present solely to reverence and justice. To reverence, so that you come to love your given lot: nature brought it to you and you to it. To justice, so that you speak the truth freely and without complication, and act only as law and the individual value of things dictates.
凡你所求于生命轮回中某一天才达成的，此刻便可归你所有——若你愿厚待你自己。那即是：若你放下所有过去，将未来托付于天意，而将当下专致于虔敬与正义。虔敬，使你终能爱你所被分派之命运：自然将它带给你，也将你带给它。正义，使你从容而无迂曲地言说真理，仅依法则及事物本身之固有价值行事。
2
If, then, when you finally come close to your exit, you have left all else behind and value only your directing mind and the divinity within you, if your fear is not that you will cease to live, but that you never started a life in accordance with nature, then you will be a man worthy of the universe that gave you birth.
若你最终临近辞世之时，已将一切他物释去，唯珍视你主导之心智与内里之神性；若你所忧惧者不是你之生命将终，而是你从未曾开启过依自然而活的一生——那么，你将是一个堪配那诞你出世的宇宙之人。
3
God sees all our directing minds stripped of their material vessels, their husks and their dross. His contact is only between his own intelligence and what has flowed from him into these channels of ours. If you train yourself to do the same, you will be rid of what so much distracts you.
神观照吾辈所有主导之心智时，是将其物质之容器、外壳与渣滓皆剥离净尽的。他的接触，仅在他自己的智识与那自他流出而入于我等此诸通道者之间。你若训练自己做同样的事，便将摆脱那如此纷扰你心之物。
4
I have often wondered how it is that everyone loves himself more than anyone else, but rates his own judgement of himself below that of others. Anyway, if a god or some wise tutor appeared at his side and told him to entertain no internal thought or intention which he won't immediately broadcast out loud, he would not stand it for a single day. So it is that we respect what our neighbours will think of us more than what we think of ourselves.
我常感到疑虑：人皆自爱胜于爱他人，却把自己对己之评价置于他人评价之下。无论如何，若有神或某位明师现于彼之身旁，令他不得在心中存有任何一经丝毫思考便不能立即琅琅宣之于口的内在之思与意想，那他连一天也无法忍受。由此看来，我们之重邻人对己之想，胜于重自己内心之所思。
6
Practise even what you have despaired of mastering. For lack of practice the left hand is awkward for most tasks, but has a stronger grip on the bridle than the right — it is practised in this.
即便是你已绝望而放弃精熟之事，仍当予以练习。因欠缺练习，左手在多数任务中略显笨拙，然其握缰时的劲道却强过右手——正因为它在此一点上经了锻炼。
7
How one should be in both body and soul when overtaken by death; the shortness of life; the immensity of time future and past; the feebleness of all things material.
当死亡降临时，人之身与心当处于何等状态；生命何其短促；过去与未来时间之浩瀚无穷；一切物质事物之脆弱不堪。
8
Look at causation stripped bare of its covers; look at the ulterior reference of any action. Consider, what is pain? What is pleasure? What is death? What is fame? Who is not himself the cause of his own unrest? Reflect how no one is hampered by any other; and that all is as thinking makes it so.
观照那被剥去了外罩、赤裸裸的原因；观照任何行动背后之援引指向。思量：痛苦为何物？逸乐为何物？死为何物？名声为何物？谁不是自己内心不宁之原因？反思何以无人能被他人所桎梏；以及一切皆如思之所成。
9
The model for the application of your principles is the boxer rather than the gladiator. The gladiator puts down or takes up the sword he uses, but the boxer always has his hands and needs only to clench them into fists.
运用你之诸原则时，其模范当为拳手而非剑斗士。剑斗士将其所用之剑或放下或拾起，然拳手之双手常在其身，只须握而为拳即可。
10
See things for what they are, analysing into material, cause, and reference.
观事物如其本然，将其分析成物质、原因与指向。
11
What liberty man has to do only what god will approve, and to welcome all that god assigns him in the course of nature!
人拥有何等之自由——唯行神所肯定之事，并欢迎神于自然之行程中所分配于他的一切！
12
Do not blame the gods: they do no wrong, willed or unwilled. Do not blame men either: all their wrongs are unwilled. No one, then, should be blamed.
莫归责于诸神：他们不行错事，无论是有意或无意。也莫归责于人：他们所有的过失皆非其本意。如此，便无人当被归责。
13
How absurd — and a complete stranger to the world — is the man surprised at any aspect of his experience in life!
对其生命经验之任何层面感到意外的那人，何其荒谬——且全然是此世之一陌客！
14
Either the compulsion of destiny and an order allowing no deviation, or a providence open to prayer, or a random welter without direction. Now if undeviating compulsion, why resist it? If a providence admitting the placation of prayer, make yourself worthy of divine assistance. If an ungoverned welter, be glad that in such a maelstrom you have within yourself a directing mind of your own.
要么是命运之必然与一无以偏离的秩序，要么是一开放于祈祷的天意，要么是漫无方向之随机混沌。若是不移不易之必然，何故抗拒？若是容许祈祷告慰之天意，便令己配得上神灵之佑助。若是一无主之混沌，你当欣幸，于此漩涡之中你自身内里尚有自己的一个主导心灵。
15
The light of a lamp shines on and does not lose its radiance until it is extinguished. Will then the truth, justice, and self-control which fuel you fail before your own end?
灯之光芒持续照耀，直至熄灭方失去其辉华。那么，那些燃亮你生命的真理、正义与自制，难道会先于你自身之终点而消陨吗？
17
If it is not right, don't do it: if it is not true, don't say it.
若非正确的，便不要去做；若非真实的，便不要去说。
18
Your impulse on every occasion should be to a complete survey of what exactly this thing is which is making an impression on your mind — to open it out by analysis into cause, material, reference, and the time-span within which it must cease to be.
你的冲动在每一情形下，皆应导向一番彻底之勘察：究竟是何物在你心中留下了此一印象——将其以分析之法展开为原因、物质、指向，以及其必定终了之时间限度。
19
Realize at long last that you have within you something stronger and more numinous than those agents of emotion which make you a mere puppet on their strings. What is in my mind at this very moment? Fear, is it? Suspicion? Desire? Something else of that sort?
你终须了悟：你内在有着比那些将你当作提线木偶之情绪作用者更加强大、更有神性分量之某物。此刻我心中在思何所想？恐惧乎？猜忌乎？贪欲乎？抑或某种别般类此之物？
20
First, nothing aimless or without ulterior reference. Second, no reference to any end other than the common good.
其一，凡事不可漫无目的或全无遥远之依指。其二，不可指向任何不同于共同福祉之目的。
21
That in a short while you will be nobody and nowhere; and the same of all that you now see and all who are now alive. It is the nature of all things to change, to perish and be transformed, so that in succession different things can come to be.
稍过片刻，你便将寂寂无名、无处可觅；而你此刻所见一切、此刻一切在世之人，亦莫不皆然。变化、消亡、转化——此乃一切事物之本然，以使继之而起的不同之物得以生现。
22
That all is as thinking makes it so — and you control your thinking. So remove your judgements whenever you wish and then there is calm — as the sailor rounding the cape finds smooth water and the welcome of a waveless bay.
一切皆如思之所成——而你掌控着你的思。那么，随时随地，移除你的判断，随后便是风平浪静——正如水手绕过了岬角，便迎来平滑如镜之水波与那无浪湾澳之拥抱。
24
Three thoughts to keep at hand. First: in your own actions, nothing aimless or other than Justice herself would have done; in external happenings either chance or providence is at work, and one should not blame chance or indict providence. Second: the nature of each of us from conception to the first breath, and from that point to the giving up of the ghost. Third: you could be caught up to the sky at any moment, then think what sort of people you would leave, and the life they would then lead.
三桩须臾莫忘之念。其一：于己之行动，无一漫无目的或非正义本身所会做之事；于外在之变故，或机遇或天意所运，人不应责难机遇或指控天意。其二：吾辈每人之本性——自存孕之始成至始有呼吸那一刻，再自此而至气息归断之时刻。其三：你任一顷刻间可能便被提升触天而去，然后，不妨想想你将丢下的是些什么样的人，以及他们随后将过着何种生命。
25
Jettison the judgement, and you are saved. And who is there to prevent this jettison?
抛掉那判断，你便得救了。那么，又有谁能阻止你将其抛掉？
26
When you fret at any circumstance, you have forgotten a number of things. You have forgotten that all comes about in accordance with the nature of the Whole; that any wrong done lies with the other; further, that everything which happens was always so in the past, will be the same again in the future, and is now happening everywhere in the present. You have also forgotten the close kinship of each individual to the whole human race.
每当你为一处境而烦恼不宁，你已忘了若干事。你忘了：一切都依循宇宙整体之本性而发生；你忘了：任何所行之错皆留在那人自己那里；再者，你忘了：一切所发生之事昔日向来如此，将来亦将同样复现，而此刻在一切所在之处正亦发生着同样之事。你还忘了：每一个体与全人类之间那种密迩的、血缘般的亲近连带。
27
Continually review in your mind those whom a particular anger took to extremes, those who reached the greatest heights of glory or disaster or enmity or any other sort of fortune. Then stop and think: where is it all now? Smoke and ashes, a story told or even a story forgotten.
持之不断地在心间回溯：那些曾为一腔特殊之怒引入极端的人，那些曾蹿升到荣耀、或灾难、或仇怨、或任何其他命运之顶峰的人。而后，驻足凝想：这一切如今安在？烟云、与灰烬——一桩被人传叙的故事，或甚至一则早已为人遗忘的往事。
28
To those who ask, 'Where then have you seen the gods? What conviction of their existence leads you to this worship of them?', I reply first that they are in fact visible to our eyes. Secondly, and notwithstanding, that I have not seen my own soul either, and yet I honour it. So it is with the gods too: from my repeated experience of their power I am convinced that they exist and I revere them.
对那些问道「那么你在哪里见过诸神？你对他们存在之确信因何而起，竟至使你如此敬拜他们？」的人，我首先回答说：他们事实上是可见于吾辈的双目的。其次——即使撇开此点——我亦未曾见识过我自己的灵魂，然我却尊崇它。诸神亦复如此：从我反复体验其大能的经验中，我深信他们存在，并虔敬他们。
29
The salvation of life lies in seeing each object in its essence and its entirety, discerning both the material and the causal: in applying one's whole soul to doing right and speaking the truth. There remains only the enjoyment of living a linked succession of good deeds, with not the slightest gap between them.
生命之救赎，在于照见每一对象之本质与整全，辨别物质与原因二者：在于以整个灵魂投入于行义举、说真话之中。此外所余者，仅是乐享于一连串环环相接的善行交织而成之生活，彼此之间莫有半分丝毫之断隙。
31
What more do you want? To live on? Or is it to continue sensation and impulse? To wax and then to wane? To make use of your voice, your mind? What in all this strikes you as good cause for regret? But if every one of these objects is contemptible, go on then to the final aim, which is to follow reason and god. But it is inconsistent with honouring these aims to be grieved by the loss of things contemptible.
你更有何求？继续存活？抑或仅是延续知觉与冲动？此身之发育渐兴，然后随之衰朽凋萎？运用你之声音、你之心智？在这一切之中，有哪一点足以令你觉得可惜堪为后悔之由？然若其每一对象都不值一哂，那便径赴那终极之目标吧，那就是：追随理性与神。然若因失去那些微不足道之物而悲切，便与尊崇此等目标水火难容。
32
What a tiny part of the boundless abyss of time has been allotted to each of us — and this is soon vanished in eternity; what a tiny part of the universal substance and the universal soul; how tiny in the whole earth the mere clod on which you creep. Reflecting on all this, think nothing important other than active pursuit where your own nature leads and passive acceptance of what universal nature brings.
你我世人，所分得之时间何其微末——无几顷刻，转瞬即消逝于永恒之中；于宇宙实体之中、于宇宙灵魂之中所占之份何其区区渺渺；于整个大地之上，你匍匐其上的那小片泥土又何其纤芥。于此沉思之后，除却主动追随你自身本然所引之道，与默然接纳宇宙自然所带来的安排之外，勿以任何别事为重。
33
How does your directing mind employ itself? This is the whole issue. All else, of your own choice or not, is just corpse and smoke.
你主导之心智，其当下运转如何？此即是全部之枢纽。一切其他之事，无论在你自择与否，仅是死尸一缕与过眼之云烟而已。
34
The clearest call to think nothing of death is the fact that even those who regard pleasure as a good and pain as an evil have nevertheless thought nothing of death.
教人不必畏死之最明白之召示乃是：即便是那些以逸乐为善、以痛苦为恶的人，也不曾将死亡放在心上。
35
For one whose only good is what comes in its own proper season, who is equally content with a greater or lesser opportunity to express true reason in his actions, to whom it makes no difference whether he looks on this world for a longer or a shorter time — for him even death has no terrors.
于那唯一以适时而来者为其善好、以行动中或大或小之机缘平等从容地体现真正之理性、且凝注此世之时日是长是短了无关碍的人而言——于他而言，即便是死亡也了无所畏。
36
Mortal man, you have lived as a citizen in this great city. What matter if that life is five or fifty years? The laws of the city apply equally to all. So what is there to fear in your dismissal from the city? This is no tyrant or corrupt judge who dismisses you, but the very same nature that brought you in. It is like the officer who engaged a comic actor dismissing him from the stage. 'But I have not played my five acts, only three.' 'True, but in life three acts can be the whole play.' Completion is determined by that being who caused first your composition and now your dissolution. You have no part in either causation. Go then in peace: the god who lets you go is at peace with you.
有死之人啊，你曾在此伟大之城邦里以一公民之身分生活。这人生是五年，还是五十年，又有何分别？城邦之律法，普施一律，公允无遗。那么，你之被遣离此城邦，又有何可畏？遣你离去者并非暴君或贪腐之判官，而乃是那当初将你带引入来的同一自然。正如当初雇了那喜剧优伶之司事，最终遣他下台一般。「可是我尚未演完我这整五幕戏，我只行了三幕。」「或许如此，但人生之中三幕也够成一场整出的戏了。」完成此事的决裁，乃出于那当初首为造成你之合成、而如今又为你之消解的同一存在者。这两重因果的发生，你都没在其中占有一分促成之因由。那么，就平和而无牵怨地去吧：那放开你、让你离开之神，他对你也平和而无怨尤。
